Proposition 1 (Stabilizer of a Subspace) Let \(r: G \to \mathrm{GL}_V\) be a finite-dimensional representation of \(G\), and let \(W \subseteq V\) be a \(k\)-subspace. Then the functor: \[ \operatorname{Stab}_G(W): k\text{-}\mathbf{Alg} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Grp}, \quad R \longmapsto \{ g \in G(R) \mid r(g)(W_R) = W_R \} \] (also denoted by \(G_W\)) is represented by an algebraic subgroup of \(G\).

Proof. Let \((e_j)_{j \in J}\) be a \(k\)-basis of \(W\), and extend it to a basis \((e_i)_{i \in I}\) of \(V\) (with \(J \subseteq I\)). Write the comodule map on the basis elements as: \[ \rho(e_j) = \sum_{i \in I} e_i \otimes f_{ij}, \quad f_{ij} \in \mathcal{O}(G). \] For any \(R \in k\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}\) and \(g \in G(R)\), we have: \[ \begin{aligned} g \in \operatorname{Stab}_G(W)(R) &\iff r(g)(W_R) = W_R \\ &\iff r(g)(e_j) \in W_R \quad (\forall j \in J) \\ &\iff f_{ij}(g) = 0 \quad (\forall i \in I \setminus J, \, j \in J). \end{aligned} \] Therefore, \(\operatorname{Stab}_G(W)\) is represented by the closed subscheme: \[ \operatorname{Stab}_G(W) = \operatorname{Spec}\big(\mathcal{O}(G) / (f_{ij} \mid i \in I \setminus J, \, j \in J)\big), \] which is an algebraic subgroup of \(G\).

Theorem 1 (Chevalley’s Theorem) Every algebraic subgroup \(H \leqslant G\) arises as the stabilizer of a \(1\)-dimensional subspace \(L\) in a finite-dimensional representation \(V\) of \(G\).

(This is one of the main ingredients for the existence of the quotient variety \(G/H\).)

Proof. Step 1. There exists a finite-dimensional representation \(V\) of \(G\) and a subspace \(W \subseteq V\) such that \(H = \operatorname{Stab}_G(W)\).

Step 2. Let \(d := \dim_k W\), \(\tilde{V} := \bigwedge^d V\), and \(L := \bigwedge^d W \subseteq \tilde{V}\). Show that \(\operatorname{Stab}_G(L) = H\), then the theorem is proved.

Proof of Step 2: Let \((e_j)_{1 \le j \le d}\) be a \(k\)-basis of \(W\), and extend it to a \(k\)-basis \((e_i)_{1 \le i \le n}\) of \(V\). Let: \[ \omega := e_1 \wedge \dots \wedge e_d \in L = \bigwedge^d W, \quad \text{so that } L = k\omega. \] It suffices to prove that for all \(\alpha \in \mathrm{GL}_V(R)\): \[ \alpha(W_R) = W_R \iff \left(\bigwedge^d \alpha\right)(L_R) = L_R. \]

  • \((\Longrightarrow)\) Obvious.
  • \((\Longleftarrow)\) Notice that \(W_R = \{ v \in V_R \mid \omega \wedge v = 0 \in \bigwedge^{d+1} V_R \}\). If \(\left(\bigwedge^d \alpha\right)(L_R) = L_R\), then: \[ \left(\bigwedge^d \alpha\right)(\omega) = c \cdot \omega \quad \text{for some } c \in R^\times. \] For any \(v \in W_R\), \(\omega \wedge v = 0\), so: \[ 0 = \left(\bigwedge^{d+1} \alpha\right)(\omega \wedge v) = \left(\bigwedge^d \alpha\right)(\omega) \wedge \alpha(v) = c \cdot (\omega \wedge \alpha(v)). \] Since \(c \in R^\times\), this implies \(\omega \wedge \alpha(v) = 0\), hence \(\alpha(v) \in W_R\) for all \(v \in W_R\). Therefore \(\alpha(W_R) = W_R\).

Proof of Step 1: Goal: Construct a finite-dimensional representation \(r: G \to \mathrm{GL}_V\) and a subspace \(W \subseteq V\) such that \(H = \operatorname{Stab}_G(W)\).

Let \(\mathfrak{a} := \ker(\mathcal{O}(G) \to \mathcal{O}(H))\) be the defining ideal of \(H\). Since \(\mathcal{O}(G)\) is noetherian, \(\mathfrak{a}\) is finitely generated as an ideal, say by \(f_1, \dots, f_r\). Set \(W_0 := \operatorname{span}_k\{f_1, \dots, f_r\} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}\).

Since \(\mathcal{O}(G)\) is a filtered union of finite-dimensional subcomodules, there exists a finite-dimensional subcomodule \(V \subseteq \mathcal{O}(G)\) containing \(W_0\). Set \(W := V \cap \mathfrak{a}\). Then \(W\) generates \(\mathfrak{a}\) as an ideal.

Let \((f_j)_{j \in J}\) be a basis of \(W\), and extend it to a basis \((f_i)_{i \in I \cup J}\) of \(V\). Since \(V\) is a subcomodule: \[ m^*(f_j) = \sum_{i \in I \cup J} f_i \otimes a_{ij}, \quad a_{ij} \in \mathcal{O}(G). \] Then \(\operatorname{Stab}_G(W) = \operatorname{Spec}\big(\mathcal{O}(G)/\mathfrak{a}'\big)\), where \(\mathfrak{a}' := (a_{ij} \mid i \in I, \, j \in J)\). We claim that \(\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{a}'\):

  1. Since \(\mathfrak{a}\) is a Hopf ideal, \(m^*(\mathfrak{a}) \subseteq \mathfrak{a} \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) + \mathcal{O}(G) \otimes \mathfrak{a}\). For each \(j \in J\), \(f_j \in W \subseteq \mathfrak{a}\), so: \[ m^*(f_j) = \sum_{i \in I \cup J} f_i \otimes a_{ij} \equiv 0 \pmod{\mathfrak{a} \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) + \mathcal{O}(G) \otimes \mathfrak{a}} \implies \sum_{i \in I} f_i \otimes a_{ij} \equiv 0 \pmod{\mathfrak{a} \otimes \mathcal{O}(G)}. \] Since the images of \((f_i)_{i \in I}\) in \(V/W \subseteq \mathcal{O}(G)/\mathfrak{a}\) are linearly independent, we obtain \(a_{ij} \in \mathfrak{a}\) for all \(i \in I, j \in J\). Thus \(\mathfrak{a}' \subseteq \mathfrak{a}\).

  2. Conversely, applying \((\mathrm{id} \otimes e^*) \circ m^* = \mathrm{id}\) to \(f_j\), we have: \[ f_j = \sum_{i \in I \cup J} f_i(e) a_{ij} \equiv \sum_{j' \in J} f_{j'}(e) a_{j' j} \pmod{\mathfrak{a}'}. \] Since \(f_{j'} \in \mathfrak{a}\), we have \(f_{j'}(e) = 0\) for all \(j' \in J\). Thus \(f_j \equiv 0 \pmod{\mathfrak{a}'}\), meaning \(f_j \in \mathfrak{a}'\) for all \(j \in J\). Because \(W = \operatorname{span}_k\{f_j\}\) generates \(\mathfrak{a}\) as an ideal, this shows \(\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}'\).

Hence \(\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{a}'\), which implies \(H = \operatorname{Stab}_G(W)\).

Definition 1 (Character) A character of a linear algebraic group \(G\) over \(k\) is a homomorphism: \[ \chi: G \longrightarrow \mathbb{G}_m. \] Then \(\chi\) is determined by the comorphism: \[ \chi^*: k[T, T^{-1}] \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}(G), \quad T \longmapsto a := \chi^*(T) \in \mathcal{O}(G)^\times. \] The compatibility of \(\chi\) with group multiplication implies \(m^*(a) = a \otimes a\), corresponding to the commutative diagram: \[ \begin{CD} k[T, T^{-1}] @>{\chi^*}>> \mathcal{O}(G) \\ @VV{T \mapsto T \otimes T}V @VV{m^*}V \\ k[T, T^{-1}] \otimes k[T, T^{-1}] @>{\chi^* \otimes \chi^*}>> \mathcal{O}(G) \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \end{CD} \]

Proposition 2 (Group-like Elements and Characters)  

  1. There are natural bijections: \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}}(G, \mathbb{G}_m) \xleftrightarrow{\quad 1:1 \quad} \{ a \in \mathcal{O}(G)^\times \mid m^*(a) = a \otimes a \} \xleftrightarrow{\quad 1:1 \quad} \{ \text{$1$-dimensional representations of } G \}. \] An element \(a \in \mathcal{O}(G)\) satisfying \(m^*(a) = a \otimes a\) is called a group-like element.

  2. Group-like elements in \(\mathcal{O}(G)\) are linearly independent over \(k\).

Proof. (Proof of 2) Suppose distinct group-like elements \(a_1, \dots, a_n\) are linearly dependent. Without loss of generality, assume any \(n-1\) of them are linearly independent. We may assume \(k = \bar{k}\). Suppose there exists a non-trivial relation: \[ \sum_{i=1}^n c_i a_i = 0 \quad \text{with } c_i \in k^\times. \] Applying \(m^*\) to this equation gives: \[ 0 = m^*\left(\sum_{i=1}^n c_i a_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \, a_i \otimes a_i. \] Since \(a_1 \neq a_2\), there exists \(x \in G(k)\) such that \(a_1(x) \neq a_2(x)\). Applying \((\mathrm{id} \otimes x)\) to the equation above gives: \[ \sum_{i=1}^n c_i a_i(x) a_i = 0. \] Multiplying the original relation \(\sum c_i a_i = 0\) by \(a_1(x)\) and subtracting yields: \[ \sum_{i=2}^n c_i \big(a_i(x) - a_1(x)\big) a_i = 0. \] Since \(c_2 \neq 0\) and \(a_2(x) - a_1(x) \neq 0\), the coefficient of \(a_2\) is non-zero. This provides a non-trivial linear relation among \(a_2, \dots, a_n\), contradicting the minimality of \(n\).

Definition 2 (Eigenspaces)  

  1. Let \(r: G \to \mathrm{GL}_V\) be a representation of \(G\), and \(\chi: G \to \mathbb{G}_m\) a character. We say that \(G\) acts on \(V\) through \(\chi\) (\(G \circlearrowright V\) through \(\chi\)) if \(r\) factors as: \[ \begin{CD} G @>{r}>> \mathrm{GL}(V) \\ @V{\chi}VV @AA{a \mapsto a \cdot \mathrm{id}_V}A \\ \mathbb{G}_m @= \mathbb{G}_m \end{CD} \] i.e., \(\rho(v) = v \otimes a(\chi)\), where \(a(\chi) := \chi^*(T) \in \mathcal{O}(G)\).

  2. If \(W \subseteq V\) is a \(G\)-stable subspace, we say \(G \circlearrowright W\) through \(\chi\) if the restricted representation acts through \(\chi\). The largest subspace of \(V\) on which \(G\) acts through \(\chi\) is called the \(\chi\)-eigenspace (or \(\chi\)-weight space), denoted by \(V_\chi\).

Proposition 3 (Sum of Eigenspaces is Direct) Let \(r: G \to \mathrm{GL}_V\) be a representation of \(G\). If \(V = \sum_\chi V_\chi\), then the sum is direct: \[ V = \bigoplus_\chi V_\chi. \]

Proof. Suppose \(v_1 + \dots + v_s = 0\) with \(v_i \in V_{\chi_i}\) (\(1 \le i \le s\)), where \(\chi_1, \dots, \chi_s\) are pairwise distinct characters. We want to show that \(v_i = 0\) for all \(1 \le i \le s\). Applying the comodule map \(\rho\) to the relation: \[ 0 = \rho(v_1 + \dots + v_s) = v_1 \otimes a(\chi_1) + \dots + v_s \otimes a(\chi_s). \] For any linear functional \(f \in V^\vee\), applying \(f \otimes \mathrm{id}\) yields: \[ f(v_1) a(\chi_1) + \dots + f(v_s) a(\chi_s) = 0 \quad \text{in } \mathcal{O}(G). \] By Proposition 2(2), the distinct group-like elements \(a(\chi_i)\) are linearly independent over \(k\). Hence \(f(v_i) = 0\) for all \(f \in V^\vee\) and all \(1 \le i \le s\). Thus \(v_i = 0\) for each \(i\), showing that the sum is direct.

Definition 3 (Category of Representations) Let \(\mathbf{Rep}_k G\) denote the category of linear representations of \(G\) on finite-dimensional \(k\)-vector spaces. - It is a tensor category equipped with: \[ \otimes: \mathbf{Rep}_k G \times \mathbf{Rep}_k G \longrightarrow \mathbf{Rep}_k G, \quad (V, W) \longmapsto V \otimes W, \] and identity object \(\mathbf{1} := (G \to \mathrm{GL}_1(k))\) (the trivial \(1\)-dimensional representation). - \(\mathbf{Rep}_k G\) is a neutral Tannakian category equipped with the natural forgetful functor: \[ \omega: \mathbf{Rep}_k G \longrightarrow \mathbf{Vect}_k, \quad (G \to \mathrm{GL}_V) \longmapsto V. \] - A tensor functor between tensor categories is a functor \(F\) satisfying: \[ F(V \otimes W) = F(V) \otimes F(W), \quad F(f \otimes g) = F(f) \otimes F(g), \quad F(\mathbf{1}) = \mathbf{1}. \] - A natural transformation of tensor functors is a natural transformation \(\eta: F \to F'\) such that: \[ \eta_{\mathbf{1}} = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbf{1}}, \quad \eta_{X \otimes Y} = \eta_X \otimes \eta_Y \quad \text{with respect to } F(X \otimes Y) \xrightarrow{\sim} F(X) \otimes F(Y). \]

Definition 4 (Automorphism Functor of the Fibre Functor) Define the functor of tensor automorphisms of \(\omega\): \[ \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega): k\text{-}\mathbf{Alg} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Grp} \] \[ R \longmapsto \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega)(R) := \operatorname{Aut}^\otimes(\omega_R) = \operatorname{Aut}^\otimes(\mathbf{Rep}_k G \otimes R \xrightarrow{\;\omega_R\;} \mathbf{Vect}_k \otimes R), \] where: - \(\mathbf{Rep}_k G \otimes R\) is \(\mathbf{Rep}_k G / R\), whose objects are \(V \otimes R\) and morphisms are \(f \otimes \mathrm{id}_R\) for \(V \in \operatorname{Ob}(\mathbf{Rep}_k G)\) and \(f \in \operatorname{Mor}(\mathbf{Rep}_k G)\); - \(\mathbf{Vect}_k \otimes R\) consists of \(\{ V \otimes R, \, f \otimes \mathrm{id}_R \}_{V \in \mathbf{Vect}_k, f \in \operatorname{Mor}}\).

Explicitly, an element \(\lambda \in \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega)(R)\) is a family of \(R\)-linear isomorphisms: \[ \lambda = (\lambda_X)_{X \in \operatorname{Ob}(\mathbf{Rep}_k G)}, \quad \lambda_X: X \otimes R \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} X \otimes R \] such that: 1. \(\lambda_{\mathbf{1}} = \mathrm{id}_R\); 2. \(\lambda_{X_1 \otimes X_2} = \lambda_{X_1} \otimes_R \lambda_{X_2}\); 3. For every morphism \(f: X \to Y\) in \(\mathbf{Rep}_k G\), the diagram commutes: \[ \begin{CD} X \otimes R @>{\lambda_X}>> X \otimes R \\ @V{f \otimes \mathrm{id}}VV @VV{f \otimes \mathrm{id}}V \\ Y \otimes R @>{\lambda_Y}>> Y \otimes R \end{CD} \]

For any \(k\)-algebra homomorphism \(R \to R'\), the induced map: \[ \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega)(R) \longrightarrow \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega)(R'), \quad (\lambda_X) \longmapsto (\lambda_X \otimes_R R') \] makes \(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega)\) into a group functor.

Theorem 2 (Tannakian Reconstruction of \(G\)) The natural morphism: \[ G \longrightarrow \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega), \quad G(R) \longrightarrow \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega)(R), \quad g \longmapsto (\rho_g \otimes R) \] is an isomorphism of group functors.

Proof. It suffices to prove that for all \(R \in k\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}\), \(G(R) \xrightarrow{\sim} \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega)(R)\) is a bijection.

Step 1. Extension to infinite-dimensional representations: Let \(r: G \to \mathrm{GL}_V\) be a representation with \(\dim V = \infty\). Write \(V = \bigcup_i V_i\) as the union of its finite-dimensional subcomodules. For any two finite-dimensional subcomodules \(V_i, V_j \subseteq V\), the intersection \(V_i \cap V_j\) is a finite-dimensional subcomodule, and by functoriality: \[ \begin{CD} (V_i \cap V_j) \otimes R @>{\lambda_{V_i \cap V_j}}>> (V_i \cap V_j) \otimes R \\ @VVV @VVV \\ V_i \otimes R @>{\lambda_{V_i}}>> V_i \otimes R \end{CD} \] Passing to the colimit yields a unique \(R\)-linear isomorphism \(\lambda_V: V \otimes R \xrightarrow{\sim} V \otimes R\) such that \(\lambda_V|_{V_i \otimes R} = \lambda_{V_i}\).

Step 2. The regular representation: For \(R \in k\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}\), let \(X_0 := \mathcal{O}(G)\) be the regular representation of \(G\).

Claim: \(\lambda_{X_0}: \mathcal{O}(G) \otimes R \to \mathcal{O}(G) \otimes R\) equals \(r_0(g)\) for some unique \(g \in G(R) = \operatorname{Hom}_{k\text{-alg}}(\mathcal{O}(G), R)\).

(Proof of Claim: Since \(\lambda\) commutes with all comodule endomorphisms and preserves the algebra structure via tensor compatibility, \(\lambda_{X_0}\) is an algebra automorphism commuting with the right \(G\)-action, hence is given by left translation by some element \(g \in G(R)\)).

Step 3. Surjectivity: The regular representation settles the case \(R = \mathcal{O}(G)\). For any \((V, r) \in \operatorname{Ob}(\mathbf{Rep}_k G)\), \(V \hookrightarrow \mathcal{O}(G)^n\) for some \(n\) as a \(G\)-representation. Then: \[ \begin{CD} V \otimes R @>>> \mathcal{O}(G)^n \otimes R \\ @V{\lambda_V}VV @VV{\lambda_{X_0^n} = (r_0(g), \dots, r_0(g))}V \\ V \otimes R @>>> \mathcal{O}(G)^n \otimes R \end{CD} \] By functoriality with respect to the embedding \(V \hookrightarrow \mathcal{O}(G)^n\), we deduce \(\lambda_V = r(g)\) for all \(V\). Thus the map is surjective.

Step 4. Injectivity: The element \(g \in G(R)\) is uniquely determined by any faithful representation: if \(V\) is faithful, i.e., \(G \hookrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_V\), then \(G(R) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_V(R)\) for all \(R\), via \(g \mapsto \lambda_V\). (Since every representation \(W\) is obtained from \(V\) via tensor products, duals, direct sums, and subquotients, functoriality forces \(\lambda_W = r(g)\)).

Remark 1 (Equivalence of Categories via Tannakian Duality). (Reference: [Milne, §4.7])

Let \((\mathcal{C}, \otimes)\) be a neutral Tannakian category over \(k\) with a \(k\)-valued fibre functor \(\omega: \mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Vect}_k\). Then: 1. \(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^\otimes(\omega): k\text{-}\mathbf{Alg} \to \mathbf{Grp}\) is representable by an affine group scheme \(G\) over \(k\). 2. The functor \(\mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Rep}_k(G)\) defined by \(\omega\) is an equivalence of tensor categories.

Remark 2 (Neutral Tannakian Categories). A tuple \((\mathcal{C}, \otimes, \Phi, \Psi)\) is a neutral Tannakian category over \(k\) if: 1. \(\mathcal{C}\) is a \(k\)-linear abelian category (a subabelian category of \(\mathbf{Vect}_k\)). 2. \(\otimes: \mathcal{C} \times \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}\) is a \(k\)-bilinear functor. 3. Associativity and symmetry constraints: \[ \Phi_{X,Y,Z}: X \otimes (Y \otimes Z) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} (X \otimes Y) \otimes Z, \quad \Psi_{X,Y}: X \otimes Y \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} Y \otimes X, \] which imply the existence of an identity object \(\mathbf{1}\) such that \(\mathcal{C} \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{C}\), \(X \mapsto \mathbf{1} \otimes X\). 4. Existence of duals: for every \(X \in \operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})\), there exists \(X^\vee \in \operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})\) and an evaluation morphism: \[ \operatorname{ev}: X^\vee \otimes X \longrightarrow \mathbf{1} \] inducing an isomorphism: \[ \operatorname{Hom}_\mathcal{C}(T, X^\vee) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \operatorname{Hom}_\mathcal{C}(T \otimes X, \mathbf{1}), \quad \alpha \longmapsto \operatorname{ev} \circ (\alpha \otimes \mathrm{id}_X). \] 5. There exists a \(k\)-valued fibre functor \(\omega: (\mathcal{C}, \otimes) \to \mathbf{Vect}_k\), which is an exact, faithful \(k\)-linear functor preserving tensor products.
