23.1 Representation I

Published

November 6, 2026

Definition 23.1.0 (Action)

  1. An action of an algebraic group \(G\) on a scheme \(X\) over \(k\) is a morphism: \[ G \times X \longrightarrow X \] or equivalently, a natural transformation: \[ \forall R \in k\text{-alg}, \quad G(R) \circlearrowright X(R) \quad \text{naturally}. \]

  2. A linear representation of \(G\) is a homomorphism of algebraic groups: \[ G \longrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_n \] for some \(n\), i.e., \(G \circlearrowright \mathbb{A}^n\).

Lemma 23.1.1 (Properties of Action Morphisms)

Let \(\varphi: G \times X \to X\) be an action. Then \(\varphi\) is fppf. Moreover, \(\varphi\) is smooth (resp. finite) if \(G\) is smooth (resp. finite).

Proof.

Consider the commutative diagram: \[ \begin{CD} G \times X @>{\sim}>{(g, x) \mapsto (g, gx)}> G \times X @>>> G \\ @V{\varphi}VV @VV{\mathrm{pr}_2}V @VVV \\ X @= X @>>> \operatorname{Spec} k \end{CD} \] The right square is Cartesian, so \(\mathrm{pr}_2\) inherits the respective property (fppf, smooth, finite) from \(G \to \operatorname{Spec} k\) by base change. Since the first horizontal map is an isomorphism, \(\varphi = \mathrm{pr}_2 \circ \sim\) satisfies these properties.

Proposition 23.1.2 (Orbit)

  1. Let \(\varphi: G \times X \to X\) be an action, and \(x \in X(k)\). Define the orbit map: \[ \varphi_x: G = G \times \operatorname{Spec} k \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{id} \times x\;} G \times X \xrightarrow{\;\varphi\;} X. \] The orbit of \(x\) is defined set-theoretically as \(\operatorname{Im} \varphi_x\).

  2. \(\operatorname{Im} \varphi_x\) is always locally closed. \(G \cdot x\) is open dense in \(\overline{G \cdot x}\).

Proof.

Since \(X_{\bar{k}} \to X\) is a quotient open map of topological spaces, without loss of generality we may assume \(k = \bar{k}\).

Apply Chevalley’s Theorem to \(\varphi_x\): \(G \cdot x\) is constructible. Thus there exists an open dense subset \(U \subseteq \overline{G \cdot x}\) such that \(U \subseteq G \cdot x\). Then: \[ G \cdot x = G \cdot U = \bigcup_{g \in G} g \cdot U \subseteq \overline{G \cdot x}. \] Since each \(g \cdot U\) is open in \(\overline{G \cdot x}\), the union \(G \cdot x\) is open in \(\overline{G \cdot x}\). Therefore, \(G \cdot x\) is an open subset of a closed subset, hence locally closed.

Proposition 23.1.3 (Closed Orbits)

If \(k = \bar{k}\) and \(G \circlearrowright X\), every orbit of minimal dimension is closed. In particular, closed orbits always exist.

Theorem 23.1.4 (Structure of General Algebraic Groups)

(Reference: [Milne, Chapter 8])

  1. There exists an exact sequence: \[ 0 \longrightarrow G^\circ \longrightarrow G \longrightarrow \pi_0(G) \longrightarrow e \] with \(G^\circ\) geometrically connected.

  2. (Barsotti–Chevalley) For every connected algebraic group \(G\) over \(k\), there exists an exact sequence: \[ 0 \longrightarrow N \longrightarrow G \longrightarrow Q \longrightarrow 0 \] where \(N\) is connected linear and \(Q\) is an abelian variety. If \(k\) is perfect, then \(N\) is unique.

Theorem 23.1.5 (Maximal Affine Quotient)

Every algebraic group \(G\) has a maximal affine quotient, defined by: \[ G \xrightarrow{\;\phi\;} \operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}(G) \] since \(\mathcal{O}(G)\) is a commutative Hopf \(k\)-algebra.

Facts:

  1. \(\mathcal{O}(G)\) is a finitely generated \(k\)-algebra, and \(G_{\mathrm{aff}} := \operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}(G)\) is a linear algebraic group (LAG).
  2. \(G \to G_{\mathrm{aff}}\) is universal: for every linear algebraic group \(H\) and every homomorphism \(G \to H\), there exists a unique factorization: \[ \begin{CD} G @>{\phi}>> G_{\mathrm{aff}} \\ @VVV @VV{\exists!}V \\ H @= H \end{CD} \]
  3. \(\phi\) is fppf.
  4. \(\mathcal{O}(\ker \phi) = k\). If \(\mathcal{O}(G) = k\), \(G\) is called anti-affine (\(G\) is commutative anti-affine).
Definition 23.1.6 (Hopf Subalgebra)

Let \(B\) be a Hopf algebra over \(k\). A \(k\)-subalgebra \(A \subseteq B\) is called a Hopf subalgebra if: \[ m^*(A) \subseteq A \otimes A \quad \text{and} \quad i^*(A) \subseteq A. \] Then \(m_A^* = m_B^*|_A\), \(i_A^* = i_B^*|_A\), and \(e_A^* = e_B^*|_A\).

Proposition 23.1.7 (Image, Kernel, and Hopf Ideals)
  1. If \(f: A \to B\) is a morphism of Hopf algebras, then \(\operatorname{Im} f = f(A)\) is a Hopf subalgebra of \(B\).
  2. A Hopf ideal in a Hopf \(k\)-algebra \(A\) is an ideal \(\mathfrak{a} \subseteq A\) such that: \[ m^*(\mathfrak{a}) \subseteq \mathfrak{a} \otimes A + A \otimes \mathfrak{a}, \quad i^*(\mathfrak{a}) \subseteq \mathfrak{a}, \quad \text{and} \quad e^*(\mathfrak{a}) = 0. \]
  3. If \(f: A \to B\) is a morphism of Hopf algebras, then \(\ker f\) is a Hopf ideal of \(A\).
  4. If \(\mathfrak{a} \subseteq A\) is a Hopf ideal, then \(A/\mathfrak{a}\) has a natural Hopf algebra structure and \(A \to A/\mathfrak{a}\) is a morphism in the category of Hopf \(k\)-algebras. For every \(f: A \to B\) such that \(\ker f \supseteq \mathfrak{a}\), there exists a unique \(\bar{f}\): \[ \begin{CD} A @>{f}>> B \\ @VVV @AA{\exists! \bar{f}}A \\ A/\mathfrak{a} @= A/\mathfrak{a} \end{CD} \]
  5. For every morphism of Hopf algebras \(f: A \to B\), there is a canonical isomorphism: \[ A/\ker f \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} f(A) \subseteq B. \]
Corollary 23.1.8 (Factorization of Morphisms of LAG)

A homomorphism \(\varphi: G \to H\) of linear algebraic groups factors as: \[ \begin{CD} G @>{\varphi}>> H \\ @V{q}VV @AA{i}A \\ I @= I \end{CD} \qquad \begin{CD} \operatorname{Spec} B @>{\varphi}>> \operatorname{Spec} A \\ @V{q}VV @AA{i}A \\ \operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{a}) @= \operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{a}) \end{CD} \] where \(q\) is fppf and \(i\) is a closed immersion.

Fact: If \(A \subseteq B\) is a Hopf subalgebra, then \(B\) is fppf over \(A\) (i.e., \(\operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A\) is fppf).

Proposition 23.1.9 (Closed Immersion Criterion)

Let \(\varphi: G \to H\) be a morphism of linear algebraic groups. If \(\ker \varphi = \operatorname{Spec} k\), then \(\varphi\) is a closed immersion.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that \(q: G \to I\) is an isomorphism.

Since \(\ker q = \ker \varphi = \operatorname{Spec} k\), for every \(R \in k\text{-alg}\), the map \(\varphi(R): G(R) \hookrightarrow I(R)\) is injective.

Claim: \(\varphi(R)\) is surjective for all \(R\).

For any \(y \in I(R)\), there exists an fppf extension \(R' \in k\text{-alg}\) over \(R\) and \(x \in G(R')\) such that \(\varphi(R')(x) = y|_{R'}\). (Take \(R' = R \otimes_{\mathcal{O}(I)} \mathcal{O}(G)\) via the pullback): \[ \begin{CD} R' @<<< G \\ @AAA @VV{\mathrm{fppf}}V \\ R @>>> I \end{CD} \] Now the sequence \(0 \to R \to R' \rightrightarrows R' \otimes_R R'\) is exact, giving the exact sequence: \[ 0 \longrightarrow G(R) \longrightarrow G(R') \rightrightarrows G(R' \otimes_R R') \] \[ \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}(G), R) \longhookrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}(G), R') \rightrightarrows \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}(G), R' \otimes_R R') \] Consider the commutative diagram: \[ \begin{CD} \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}(G), R) @>>> \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}(G), R') @>>> \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}(G), R' \otimes_R R') \\ @V{\varphi(R)}VV @V{\varphi(R')}VV @VVV \\ I(R) @>>> I(R') @>>> I(R' \otimes_R R') \end{CD} \] \[ y \longmapsto y|_{R'} \longmapsto 0 \] Then \(x \in G(R')\) maps to \(y|_{R'}\), and its difference in \(G(R' \otimes_R R')\) maps to \(0\) in \(I(R' \otimes_R R')\). By injectivity, the difference is \(0\). By descent, \(x\) descends to \(x_0 \in G(R)\) with \(x_0 \mapsto y\). Thus \(\varphi(R)\) is surjective.

Definition 23.1.10 (Representations)

  1. Let \(V\) be a \(k\)-vector space. Define the affine algebraic group functor: \[ \mathrm{GL}_V: k\text{-alg} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Grp}, \quad R \longmapsto \mathrm{GL}_V(R) = \operatorname{Aut}_R(V \otimes R). \] If \(V\) is finite-dimensional, then after fixing a basis, \(\mathrm{GL}_V \cong \mathrm{GL}_n\).

  2. A linear representation of \(G\) is a morphism of algebraic groups: \[ r: G \longrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_V. \]

    • \(r\) is faithful if \(\ker r = \operatorname{Spec} k \iff r\) is a closed immersion.
    • \(r\) is trivial if \(r(G) = e\).
Definition 23.1.11 (Comodule)

A right comodule is a \(k\)-vector space \(V\) equipped with a \(k\)-linear map \(\rho: V \to V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G)\) such that:

  1. \((\mathrm{id}_V \otimes e^*) \circ \rho = \mathrm{id}_V\): \[ \begin{CD} V @>{\rho}>> V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \\ @| @VV{\mathrm{id}_V \otimes e^*}V \\ V @= V \end{CD} \]

  2. \((\mathrm{id}_V \otimes m^*) \circ \rho = (\rho \otimes \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{O}(G)}) \circ \rho\): \[ \begin{CD} V @>{\rho}>> V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \\ @V{\rho}VV @VV{\mathrm{id}_V \otimes m^*}V \\ V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) @>{\rho \otimes \mathrm{id}}>> V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \end{CD} \]

Definition 23.1.12 (Comodule Morphism and Submodule)

Let \((V, \rho)\) be an \(\mathcal{O}(G)\)-comodule.

  1. A morphism of \(\mathcal{O}(G)\)-comodules is a \(k\)-linear map \(f: V \to W\) such that: \[ \begin{CD} V @>{f}>> W \\ @V{\rho_V}VV @VV{\rho_W}V \\ V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) @>{f \otimes \mathrm{id}}>> W \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \end{CD} \]

  2. A submodule (subcomodule) is a subspace \(W \subseteq V\) such that \(\rho(W) \subseteq W \otimes \mathcal{O}(G)\).

Theorem 23.1.13 (Bijection between Representations and Comodules)

Let \(r: G \to \mathrm{GL}_V\) be a representation and \(A := \mathcal{O}(G)\). \[ r(A): G(A) = \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}(G), A) \longrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_V(A) = \operatorname{Aut}_A(V \otimes A) \] \[ \mathrm{id} \longmapsto (u: V \otimes A \xrightarrow{\sim} V \otimes A) \in \operatorname{Aut}_A(V \otimes A). \] Consider \(\rho: V \xrightarrow{v \mapsto v \otimes 1} V \otimes A \xrightarrow{u} V \otimes A\). There is a bijection: \[ \big\{ \text{Representations } r: G \to \mathrm{GL}_V \big\} \xleftrightarrow{\quad 1:1 \quad} \big\{ \mathcal{O}(G)\text{-comodule structures on } V \big\}, \quad r \longleftrightarrow \rho_r. \]

Proof.

By Yoneda’s Lemma on \(G\) and \(\mathrm{GL}_V(-)\), for every \(R \in k\text{-alg}\) and \(f: A \to R\) (\(f \in G(R)\)), we have: \[ \begin{CD} G(A) @>{r(A)}>> \mathrm{GL}_V(A) \\ @V{f_*}VV @VV{f_*}V \\ G(R) @>{r(R)}>> \mathrm{GL}_V(R) \end{CD} \qquad \begin{array}{rcl} \mathrm{id} & \longmapsto & u \\ f & \longmapsto & f_* u \end{array} \] Then \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}}(G, \mathrm{GL}_V) \cong \dots\)

Given a \(k\)-morphism \(V \xrightarrow{\rho} V \otimes A\), one has \(V \otimes A \to V \otimes A\), \((v, a) \mapsto a\rho(v)\). Then: \[ \operatorname{Hom}_k(V, V \otimes A) = \operatorname{Hom}_k(V, \operatorname{Hom}_A(A, V \otimes A)) = \operatorname{Hom}_A(V \otimes A, V \otimes A). \] We narrow down to \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}}(G, \mathrm{GL}_V) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{comod}}(V, V \otimes A)\).

Proposition 23.1.14 (Filtered Union of Finite-Dimensional Submodules)

Every \(\mathcal{O}(G)\)-comodule \((V, \rho)\) is a filtered union of finite-dimensional submodules.

Proof.

If \(W_1, W_2 \subseteq V\) are submodules, then \(W_1 + W_2\) is a submodule. It suffices to show that every \(v \in V\) is contained in a finite-dimensional submodule of \(V\).

Let \((f_i)_{i \in I}\) be a \(k\)-basis of \(\mathcal{O}(G)\), then: \[ \rho(v) = \sum_{i \in I} v_i \otimes f_i \in V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \quad (\text{finite sum}). \] Set \(W := \operatorname{span}_k\{v_i\}\).

Claim: \(W\) is a subcomodule of \(V\) containing \(v\).

First, \(v = \sum_{i \in I} v_i f_i(e) \in W\). It remains to show that \(\rho(v_i) \in W \otimes \mathcal{O}(G)\) for all \(v_i\). From the coassociativity diagram: \[ \begin{CD} V @>{\rho}>> V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \\ @V{\rho}VV @VV{\mathrm{id} \otimes m^*}V \\ V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) @>{\rho \otimes \mathrm{id}}>> V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \end{CD} \] we have: \[ \sum v_i \otimes m^*(f_i) = \sum a_{ijl} \, v_i \otimes f_j \otimes f_l \] \[ \sum \rho(v_i) \otimes f_i = \sum \rho(v_l) \otimes f_l. \] Then \(\rho(v_l) = \sum a_{ijl} \, v_i \otimes f_j \in W \otimes \mathcal{O}(G)\).

Remark 23.1.15 (Algebraic Action)

If \(V = \mathcal{O}(X)\) with \(X\) affine over \(k\), then \(V\) is an \(\mathcal{O}(G)\)-comodule. Given \(\rho: V \to V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G)\), suppose \(\rho\) is a morphism of \(k\)-algebras. Then \(X \times G \xrightarrow{a} X\): \[ \begin{CD} \mathcal{O}(X) @>{\rho}>> \mathcal{O}(X) \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \\ @| @VV{\mathrm{id} \otimes e^*}V \\ \mathcal{O}(X) @= \mathcal{O}(X) \end{CD} \qquad \begin{CD} \mathcal{O}(X) @>{\rho}>> \mathcal{O}(X) \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \\ @V{\rho}VV @VV{\mathrm{id} \otimes m^*}V \\ \mathcal{O}(X) \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) @>{\rho \otimes \mathrm{id}}>> \mathcal{O}(X) \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \end{CD} \] \(\implies a\) is a right algebraic action, which is in fact: \[ G \longrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}_X \longrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_{\mathcal{O}(X)} \] \[ \begin{array}{rcl} \operatorname{Aut}_X(R) & \longrightarrow & \mathrm{GL}_{\mathcal{O}(X)}(R) \\ \operatorname{Aut}(X \times R) & \longrightarrow & \mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O}(X) \otimes R) \\ f & \longmapsto & f^*: \mathcal{O}(X \times R) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{O}(X \times R). \end{array} \]

Definition 23.1.16 (Simple Representation)

A representation \(V\) of \(G\) is simple if \(V \neq 0\) and has no non-trivial subrepresentations.

Then every simple representation of a linear algebraic group is finite-dimensional.

Definition 23.1.17 (Regular Representation)

Let \(X = G\), with \(m: X \times G \to X\) the right action. Then \(\mathcal{O}(X) = \mathcal{O}(G)\) is an \(\mathcal{O}(G)\)-comodule. The morphism: \[ G \longrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_A \] is called the regular representation, where \(A = \mathcal{O}(G)\).

Theorem 23.1.18 (Finite-Dimensional Faithful Action)

The regular representation of \(G\) has a faithful finite-dimensional subrepresentation. In particular, the regular representation is faithful and \(G \hookrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_n\).

Proof.

For any finite-dimensional submodule \(V \subseteq \mathcal{O}(G)\), if \(f_1, \dots, f_n\) is a basis of \(V\), then \(\mathrm{GL}_V \cong \mathrm{GL}_n\): \[ \begin{CD} V @>>> V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \\ @VVV @VVV \\ \mathcal{O}(G) @>{m^*}>> \mathcal{O}(G) \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \end{CD} \] Suppose \(m^*(f_i) = \sum_j f_j \otimes b_{ij}\). Then: \[ G \longrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_V \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \mathrm{GL}_n, \quad g \longmapsto (b_{ij}(g)). \] Then we want: \[ \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_n) = k[T_{ij}, \det{}^{-1}] \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}(G), \quad T_{ij} \longmapsto b_{ij} \] to be surjective. It suffices to find \(b_{ij}\) generating \(\mathcal{O}(G)\).

Since \(\mathrm{id}: G \xrightarrow{e \times \mathrm{id}} G \times G \xrightarrow{m} G \implies f_i = \sum_j f_j(e) b_{ij}\), it suffices to choose \(V \subseteq \mathcal{O}(G)\) such that: - \(V\) generates \(\mathcal{O}(G)\), - \(V\) is a submodule of \(\mathcal{O}(G)\), - \(\dim_k V < +\infty\).

Definition 23.1.19 (Free Comodule)

For a vector space \(V/k\), \(V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G)\) equipped with: \[ V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{id} \otimes m^* \;} V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \] is an \(\mathcal{O}(G)\)-comodule, called the free comodule.

Proposition 23.1.20 (Finite-Dimensional Comodules Embed into Free Comodules)

Every finite-dimensional \(\mathcal{O}(G)\)-comodule \(V\) is a submodule of \((\mathcal{O}(G)^n, m^*)\).

Proof.

\(\rho: V \to V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G)\) is injective (since \((\mathrm{id} \otimes e^*) \circ \rho = \mathrm{id}_V\)), and: \[ \begin{CD} V @>{\rho}>> V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \\ @V{\rho}VV @VV{\mathrm{id} \otimes m^*}V \\ V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) @>{\rho \otimes \mathrm{id}}>> V \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \otimes \mathcal{O}(G) \end{CD} \] is a morphism of comodules.

Theorem 23.1.21 (Generating Representation)

Let \(G \hookrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_V\) be a faithful finite-dimensional representation of \(G\). Every finite-dimensional representation \(W\) of \(G\) can be constructed from \(V\) by tensor, direct sum, dual, and subquotient.

Proof.

See [Milne].
