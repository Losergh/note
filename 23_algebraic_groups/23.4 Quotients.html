23.4 Quotients

Published

October 26, 2026

Theorem 1 (Universal Property of Quotients) Let \(k\) be a field, and let \(G, G'\) be linear algebraic groups over \(k\).

  1. A homomorphism \(p: G \to Q\) of linear algebraic groups is a quotient map if and only if it is faithfully flat of finite presentation (fppf). Equivalently, at the level of coordinate Hopf algebras, \(A = \mathcal{O}(Q) \hookrightarrow B = \mathcal{O}(G)\) is an injective Hopf subalgebra map such that \(B\) is faithfully flat over \(A\).

  2. Let \(p: G \to Q\) be a quotient map of linear algebraic groups, and let \(N = \ker p\). Every homomorphism \(\varphi: G \to G'\) such that \(\ker \varphi \supseteq \ker p\) factors uniquely through \(p\): \[ \begin{CD} G @>{p}>> Q \\ @V{\varphi}VV @VV{\exists!\,\bar{\varphi}}V \\ G' @= G' \end{CD} \]

Proof. To prove (2), consider the kernel pair diagram (coequalizer): \[ G \times_Q G \;\rightrightarrows\; G \xrightarrow{\;\varphi\;} G'. \] We check this condition on points: for every \(R \in \mathbf{Alg}_{/k}\), \[ \begin{CD} G(R) \times_{Q(R)} G(R) @>{p_1, p_2}>> G(R) @>{\varphi}>> G'(R) \\ @V{p \times p}VV @VV{p}V @VV{\varphi}V \\ Q(R) \times Q(R) @>>> Q(R) @>>> G'(R) \end{CD} \] Since \(\ker p \subseteq \ker \varphi\), for every \(R \in \mathbf{Alg}_{/k}\) we have \(\ker p(R) \subseteq \ker \varphi(R)\). For any pair \((g_1, g_2) \in G(R) \times_{Q(R)} G(R)\), we have \(p(g_1) = p(g_2)\), so: \[ g_1 g_2^{-1} \in \ker p(R) \subseteq \ker \varphi(R) \implies \varphi(g_1 g_2^{-1}) = 1 \implies \varphi(g_1) = \varphi(g_2). \] Hence the two compositions \(G \times_Q G \rightrightarrows G \xrightarrow{\varphi} G'\) coincide.

At the level of coordinate rings, let \(B = \mathcal{O}(G)\) and \(A = \mathcal{O}(Q)\). Since \(B\) is faithfully flat over \(A\), the Amitsur complex \[ A \longrightarrow B \;\rightrightarrows\; B \otimes_A B \] is exact (i.e., \(A = \{ b \in B \mid b \otimes 1 = 1 \otimes b \}\)). The equality of the two pullbacks on \(B \otimes_A B\) ensures that the comorphism \(\varphi^\sharp: \mathcal{O}(G') \to B\) has image landing in the equalizer \(A = \mathcal{O}(Q)\): \[ \begin{CD} B \otimes B @<<< B @<{\varphi^\sharp}<< \mathcal{O}(G') \\ @AAA @AAA @| \\ B \otimes_A B @<<< A @<<{\exists!\,\bar{\varphi}^\sharp}< \mathcal{O}(G') \end{CD} \] Thus, there exists a unique \(k\)-algebra homomorphism \(\bar{\varphi}^\sharp: \mathcal{O}(G') \to \mathcal{O}(Q)\), inducing the unique group homomorphism \(\bar{\varphi}: Q \to G'\).

Definition 1 Let \(G\) be a linear algebraic group and \(N \unlhd G\) a normal closed subgroup. If there exists a quotient map \(q: G \to Q\) such that \(\ker q = N\), then \(Q\) is called the quotient group of \(G\) by \(N\), denoted \(G/N\). By Theorem 1, \(Q\) is unique up to unique isomorphism.

Definition 2 Let \(G\) be a linear algebraic group, and let \(r: G \to \mathrm{GL}_V\) be a representation of \(G\) on a finite-dimensional \(k\)-vector space \(V\). The subspace of invariants (or fixed points) is defined by: \[ V^G := \big\{ v \in V \;\big|\; g \cdot v_R = v_R \quad \forall R \in \mathbf{Alg}_{/k}, \, \forall g \in G(R) \big\}, \] where \(v_R = v \otimes 1 \in V \otimes_k R\).

Lemma 1 For every \(k\)-algebra \(R \in \mathbf{Alg}_{/k}\), the formation of invariants commutes with base change: \[ V^G \otimes_k R = \big\{ v' \in V \otimes_k R \;\big|\; g \cdot v'_S = v'_S \quad \forall S \in \mathbf{Alg}_{/R}, \, \forall g \in G(S) \big\}. \]

Proof. The inclusion \(\mathrm{LHS} \subseteq \mathrm{RHS}\) is clear. Conversely, choose a \(k\)-basis \(\{e_i\}_{i \in I}\) of \(R\). Any element \(v' \in V \otimes_k R\) can be uniquely written as a finite sum: \[ v' = \sum_{i \in I} v_i \otimes e_i, \quad v_i \in V. \] Suppose \(v' \in \mathrm{RHS}\). We want to show that \(v_i \in V^G\) for each \(i \in I\). For any \(S \in \mathbf{Alg}_{/k}\) and any \(g \in G(S)\), consider the canonical base extension \(S \to S \otimes_k R\) given by \(a \mapsto a \otimes 1\). This induces a map \(G(S) \to G(S \otimes_k R)\), sending \(g \mapsto g'\). Viewing \(S \otimes_k R\) as an \(R\)-algebra, the assumption on \(v'\) implies: \[ g' \cdot v'_{S \otimes_k R} = v'_{S \otimes_k R} \quad \text{in } V \otimes_k S \otimes_k R. \] Expanding in the basis \(\{e_i\}\): \[ \sum_{i \in I} \big( g \cdot (v_i)_S \big) \otimes e_i = \sum_{i \in I} (v_i)_S \otimes e_i \quad \text{in } (V \otimes_k S) \otimes_k R. \] Since the elements \(e_i\) are linearly independent over \(k\), we deduce that for each \(i \in I\): \[ g \cdot (v_i)_S = (v_i)_S \quad \text{in } V \otimes_k S. \] Because this holds for all \(S\) and all \(g \in G(S)\), it follows that \(v_i \in V^G\) for all \(i \in I\). Thus \(v' \in V^G \otimes_k R\).

Lemma 2 Let \(G\) be a linear algebraic group, \(N \unlhd G\) a normal subgroup, and \((V, r) \in \mathbf{Rep}_k(G)\). Then the invariant subspace \(V^N\) is stable under \(G\) (i.e., \(g \cdot V^N = V^N\) for all \(g \in G\)).

Proof. Let \(R \in \mathbf{Alg}_{/k}\) and \(v \in V^N \otimes_k R\). For any \(R\)-algebra \(R'\) and any \(g \in G(R')\), we must show that: \[ (g \cdot v_R)_{R'} \in V^N \otimes_k R'. \] By Lemma 1, it suffices to show that for any \(n' \in N(R')\): \[ n' \cdot (g \cdot v_R)_{R'} = (g \cdot v_R)_{R'}. \] Writing \(g'\) for the image of \(g\) in \(G(R')\), since \(N \unlhd G\) is normal, \(g'^{-1} n' g' \in N(R')\). Since \(v \in V^N \otimes_k R\), \((g'^{-1} n' g') \cdot v_{R'} = v_{R'}\). Therefore: \[ n' \cdot (g' \cdot v_{R'}) = g' \cdot \big( (g'^{-1} n' g') \cdot v_{R'} \big) = g' \cdot v_{R'} = (g \cdot v_R)_{R'}. \] Thus \((g \cdot v_R) \in V^N \otimes_k R\), so \(V^N\) is stable under \(G\).

Proposition 1 Let \(k = \bar{k}\), let \(G\) be a linear algebraic group, and let \(H \unlhd G\) be a closed normal subgroup. Then there exists a representation \((V, r) \in \mathbf{Rep}_k(G)\) and a \(1\)-dimensional subspace \(L \subseteq V\) such that: \[ H = \operatorname{Stab}_G(L) \quad \text{and } H \text{ acts trivially on } L. \]

To prove Proposition 1, we establish two preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 3 (Lemma B) Let \(G\) be a linear algebraic group over \(k\), and let \((V, r) \in \mathbf{Rep}_k(G)\). If \(V = \sum_{i \in I} S_i\) is a sum of simple subrepresentations, then for every subrepresentation \(W \subseteq V\), there exists a subset \(J \subseteq I\) such that: \[ V = W \oplus \bigoplus_{j \in J} S_j. \] In particular, \(V\) is a direct sum of simple representations, and every subrepresentation \(W\) is a direct summand.

Lemma 4 (Lemma A) Let \(k = \bar{k}\), let \(G\) be a linear algebraic group, and let \(H \unlhd G\) be a closed normal subgroup. If a character \(\chi \in X^*(H)\) occurs in a representation \((V, r)\) of \(G\) (meaning there is an \(H\)-stable line \(L \subseteq V\) on which \(H\) acts via \(\chi\)), then there exists an integer \(m \ge 1\) such that \(-m\chi\) occurs in a representation (possibly different) of \(G\).

Proof. Step 1: Reduction using Lemma B. Suppose \(\chi\) occurs on a line \(L \subseteq V\). It suffices to find a representation \((W, r_W) \in \mathbf{Rep}_k(G)\) containing a \(1\)-dimensional subspace \(L_1 \subseteq W\) such that:

  1. \(H \le \operatorname{Stab}_G(L_1)\) with \(H\) acting on \(L_1\) via \(m\chi\);

  2. \(L_1\) is a direct summand of \(W\) as a representation of \(H\).

If such a \(W\) exists, then in the dual representation \(W^\vee \in \mathbf{Rep}_k(G)\), the dual line \(L_1^\vee\) is an \(H\)-subrepresentation on which \(H\) acts via \(-m\chi\).

Step 2: Case 1 — \(G(k)\) is Zariski dense in \(G\). This occurs, for example, when \(G\) is smooth or reduced over an algebraically closed field. Let \(W \subseteq V\) be the sum of all \(1\)-dimensional \(H\)-stable subspaces of \(V\). We claim that \(W\) is \(G\)-stable: Since \(H \unlhd G\), for any \(g \in G(k)\) and \(h \in H(k)\), \(g^{-1} h g \in H(k)\). For any \(v \in L_\chi \subseteq W\), \[ h \cdot (g \cdot v) = g \cdot (g^{-1} h g \cdot v) = g \cdot \big(\chi(g^{-1} h g) v\big) = \chi(g^{-1} h g) (g \cdot v). \] Hence \(g \cdot v\) is an eigenvector for \(H\), so \(g \cdot W \subseteq W\). Since \(G(k)\) is Zariski dense in \(G\), \(\operatorname{Stab}_G(W) = G\), so \((W, r|_W) \in \mathbf{Rep}_k(G)\). By definition, \(W\) is a sum of \(1\)-dimensional (hence simple) \(H\)-representations. By Lemma 3 (Lemma B), \(L\) is a direct summand of \(W\) as an \(H\)-representation. Thus \(L^\vee \subseteq W^\vee\) is a subrepresentation with character \(-\chi\) (here \(m = 1\)).

Step 3: Case 2 — General case (characteristic \(p > 0\)). If \(\operatorname{char} k = 0\), \(G\) is always smooth, so Case 1 applies. Assume \(\operatorname{char} k = p > 0\). For a sufficiently large integer \(r \ge 1\), let \(F_G^r: G \to G^{(p^r)}\) be the \(r\)-th Frobenius morphism, and let \(N = \ker F_G^r\) be the Frobenius kernel. Consider the representation \(S^{p^r}(V) \in \mathbf{Rep}_k(G)\) and the line \(L^{\otimes p^r} \subseteq S^{p^r}(V)\). Choose a basis \(\{e_1, \dots, e_n\}\) of \(V\) such that \(L = k e_1\). Under \(r: G \to \mathrm{GL}_n\), the elements \(n \in N(R)\) satisfy \(r(n) = (a_{ij})\) with \(a_{ij}^{p^r} = \delta_{ij}\). In the symmetric power \(S^{p^r}(V)\), \[ n \cdot e_1^{\otimes p^r} = \left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_{i1} e_i\right)^{\!\![p^r]} = \sum_{i=1}^n a_{i1}^{p^r} e_i^{[p^r]} = e_1^{[p^r]}. \] Therefore, \(L^{\otimes p^r} \subseteq \big(S^{p^r}(V)\big)^N\). By Lemma 2, \(V_1 := \big(S^{p^r}(V)\big)^N\) is a subrepresentation of \(G\). Since \(N\) acts trivially on \(V_1\), the action of \(G\) on \(V_1\) factors through the quotient \(Z := G/N \cong \operatorname{Im}(F_G^r)\). For \(r \gg 0\), \(Z\) is reduced, and since \(k = \bar{k}\), \(Z(k)\) is Zariski dense in \(Z\). Applying Case 1 to \(Z\), there exists a subrepresentation \(W \subseteq V_1\) containing a line \(L_1\) on which \(H\) acts via \(\chi^{p^r}\), and \(L_1\) is a direct summand of \(W\). Thus the dual representation yields the character \(-p^r \chi\) (with \(m = p^r\)).

Proof. (Proof of Proposition 1) By Chevalley’s theorem (Theorem 5.2.1), there exists a representation \((V, r_V) \in \mathbf{Rep}_k(G)\) and a \(1\)-dimensional subspace \(L \subseteq V\) such that \(H = \operatorname{Stab}_G(L)\). The subgroup \(H\) acts on \(L\) via a character \(\chi: H \to \mathrm{GL}(L) \cong \mathbb{G}_m\). If \(\chi = 1\) is trivial, the statement is already satisfied. If \(\chi \ne 1\), by Lemma 4 (Lemma A), there exists a representation \((W, r_W) \in \mathbf{Rep}_k(G)\) and a line \(L' \subseteq W\) such that \(H \le \operatorname{Stab}_G(L')\) and \(H\) acts on \(L'\) via \(-m\chi\) for some \(m \ge 1\). Consider the representation \[ \widetilde{V} := V^{\otimes m} \otimes_k W \in \mathbf{Rep}_k(G), \] and the \(1\)-dimensional subspace \(\widetilde{L} := L^{\otimes m} \otimes_k L' \subseteq \widetilde{V}\). The character by which \(H\) acts on \(\widetilde{L}\) is: \[ \chi^m \cdot (-m\chi) = 1 \quad (\text{trivial}). \] Furthermore, \(\operatorname{Stab}_G(\widetilde{L}) \ge \operatorname{Stab}_G(L) \cap \operatorname{Stab}_G(L') \ge H\). To ensure the stabilizer is exactly \(H\), we take \(V' = V \oplus \widetilde{V}\) and \(L'' = \widetilde{L}\) inside a suitable direct sum, or simply replace \(V\) with \(V^{\oplus m} \oplus \widetilde{V}\) so that \(\operatorname{Stab}_G(\widetilde{L}) = H\).

Definition 3 (Weil Restriction of Scalars) Let \(A\) be a finite \(k\)-algebra, and let \(X\) be a quasi-projective scheme over \(A\). The Weil restriction of scalars of \(X\) from \(A\) to \(k\), denoted \(\operatorname{Res}_{A/k} X\), is the functor: \[ \operatorname{Res}_{A/k} X: \mathbf{Alg}_{/k} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Sets}, \quad R \longmapsto X(A \otimes_k R). \] The functor \(\operatorname{Res}_{A/k}: \mathbf{Sch}_{/A} \to \mathbf{Sch}_{/k}\) is the right adjoint to base change: \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Sch}_{/A}}(Y_A, X) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Sch}_{/k}}(Y, \operatorname{Res}_{A/k} X). \]

Proposition 2 Let \(A\) be a finite \(k\)-algebra.

  1. If \(G\) is an algebraic group over \(A\), then \(\operatorname{Res}_{A/k} G\) is an algebraic group over \(k\).

  2. If \(G\) is a linear algebraic group over \(A\), then \(\operatorname{Res}_{A/k} G\) is a linear algebraic group over \(k\).

Example 1  

  1. For any \(k\)-algebraic group \(G\), the unit of the adjunction gives a canonical homomorphism: \[ i_G: G \longrightarrow \operatorname{Res}_{A/k}(G_A), \] which on \(R\)-points is given by: \[ i_G(R): G(R) \longrightarrow G_A(A \otimes_k R) = G(A \otimes_k R), \] induced by the natural \(k\)-algebra homomorphism \(R \to A \otimes_k R\), \(r \mapsto 1 \otimes r\).

  2. If \(f: G_A \to H\) is a homomorphism of \(A\)-algebraic groups, the adjunction gives: \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}/A}(G_A, H) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}/k}\big(G, \operatorname{Res}_{A/k} H\big), \quad f \longmapsto \operatorname{Res}_{A/k}(f) \circ i_G. \]

Theorem 2 (Existence of Quotients) Every closed normal subgroup \(H\) of a linear algebraic group \(G\) over a field \(k\) is the kernel of a quotient map \(p: G \to Q\), where \(Q\) is a linear algebraic group.

Proof. Case 1: \(k = \bar{k}\). By Proposition 1, there exists a representation \((V, r_V) \in \mathbf{Rep}_k(G)\) and a line \(L \subseteq V\) such that \(\operatorname{Stab}_G(L) = H\) and \(H\) acts trivially on \(L\). Since \(H\) acts trivially on \(L\), we have \(L \subseteq V^H\). By Lemma 2, \(V^H\) is a \(G\)-subrepresentation of \(V\). Let \(\alpha: G \to \mathrm{GL}(V^H)\) be the restriction of \(r_V\) to \(V^H\). Since \(H\) acts trivially on \(V^H\), we have \(H \le \ker \alpha\). On the other hand, since \(L \subseteq V^H\), any \(g \in \ker \alpha\) fixes \(L\) pointwise, which means: \[ \ker \alpha \le \operatorname{Stab}_G(L) = H. \] Thus \(\ker \alpha = H\). Setting \(Q := \operatorname{Im}(\alpha) \subseteq \mathrm{GL}(V^H)\) (the scheme-theoretic image), the map \(p: G \to Q\) is faithfully flat (fppf), and \(\ker p = H\). Thus \(Q \cong G/H\) is a linear algebraic group.

Case 2: Arbitrary ground field \(k\). Over \(\bar{k}\), by Case 1 there exists a quotient \(G_{\bar{k}} \to Q_{\bar{k}}\) with kernel \(H_{\bar{k}}\), and choosing a faithful representation of \(Q_{\bar{k}}\) yields a representation \(r: G_{\bar{k}} \to \mathrm{GL}_{n,\bar{k}}\) such that \(\ker r = H_{\bar{k}}\). Since \(G\) and \(H\) are defined over \(k\), the representation \(r\) is defined over some finite field extension \(k'/k\): \[ r': G_{k'} \longrightarrow \mathrm{GL}(V') \quad \text{such that} \quad \ker r' = H_{k'}, \] where \(V'\) is a finite-dimensional \(k'\)-vector space. Applying Weil restriction \(\operatorname{Res}_{k'/k}\) to \(r'\) and precomposing with the canonical map \(i_G: G \to \operatorname{Res}_{k'/k} G_{k'}\), we obtain a homomorphism of linear algebraic groups over \(k\): \[ \alpha: G \xrightarrow{\;i_G\;} \operatorname{Res}_{k'/k}(G_{k'}) \xrightarrow{\;\operatorname{Res}_{k'/k}(r')\;} \operatorname{Res}_{k'/k}\big(\mathrm{GL}(V')\big). \] For every \(k\)-algebra \(R \in \mathbf{Alg}_{/k}\), the map on \(R\)-points is: \[ \alpha(R): G(R) \longrightarrow G(R \otimes_k k') \xrightarrow{\;r'(R \otimes_k k')\;} \mathrm{GL}_{V'}(R \otimes_k k'). \] Its kernel is: \[ \ker \alpha(R) = G(R) \cap \ker\big(r'(R \otimes_k k')\big) = G(R) \cap H(R \otimes_k k') = H(R). \] Hence \(\ker \alpha = H\). Defining \(Q := \operatorname{Im} \alpha\) as the scheme-theoretic image of \(\alpha\) inside \(\operatorname{Res}_{k'/k}(\mathrm{GL}(V'))\), we obtain a quotient map \(p: G \to Q\) with \(\ker p = H\). By Proposition 2, \(\operatorname{Res}_{k'/k}(\mathrm{GL}(V'))\) is a linear algebraic group, so its closed subgroup \(Q\) is also a linear algebraic group.

Corollary 1  

  1. For every closed normal subgroup \(H \unlhd G\), there exists a representation \((V, r)\) of \(G\) such that \(H = \ker r\).

  2. Let \(0 \to H \to G \xrightarrow{\pi} Q \to 0\) be a sequence of linear algebraic groups. The following are equivalent:

    1. It is an exact sequence of group sheaves in the fppf topology on \((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\text{fppf}}\);

    2. \(Q\) is the quotient group \(G/H\);

    3. \(\pi\) is fppf and \(H = \ker \pi\).

Proof.

  1. By Theorem 2, \(Q = G/H\) is a linear algebraic group. Choosing a faithful representation \(\rho: Q \hookrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_V\), the composite \(r = \rho \circ \pi: G \to \mathrm{GL}_V\) satisfies \(\ker r = \ker \pi = H\).

  2. The equivalence (b) \(\iff\) (c) is the definition of a quotient map.

  1. \(\implies\) (c): Since \(G \to Q\) is an fppf epimorphism, \(Q\) is an \(H\)-torsor over \(G\), which implies \(\pi\) is fppf and \(H = \ker \pi\).
  2. \(\implies\) (a): For every \(R \in \mathbf{Alg}_{/k}\) and every \(\bar{g} \in Q(R)\), the fiber product \(R' := R \times_Q G\) is an fppf \(R\)-algebra, and the image of \(\bar{g}\) in \(Q(R')\) lifts to \(G(R')\). Hence \(G \to Q\) is an epimorphism of fppf sheaves. Exactness at \(G\) follows from \(H = \ker \pi\).

Proposition 3  

  1. Let \(f: X \to Y\) be a \(G\)-equivariant morphism of \(k\)-schemes of finite type. Then the image \(f(X)\) is locally closed in \(Y\). If \(X\) is reduced, \(f\) factors as: \[ X \xrightarrow{\;\text{fppf}\;} f(X) \hookrightarrow Y. \]

  2. In particular, for any action \(G \times X \to X\) and any \(k\)-point \(x \in X(k)\), the orbit map induces a morphism: \[ G \xrightarrow{\;\text{fppf}\;} G \cdot x \hookrightarrow X. \] If \(G\) is reduced, the orbit \(G \cdot x\) is locally closed and stable under \(G\). The sequence \[ 0 \longrightarrow G_x \longrightarrow G \longrightarrow G \cdot x \longrightarrow 0 \] is an exact sequence of fppf sheaves. If \(G\) is smooth, then \(G \cdot x\) is smooth.

Theorem 3 (Quotient by an Arbitrary Subgroup) Let \(G\) be a smooth linear algebraic group, and let \(H \le G\) be any closed subgroup (not necessarily normal). Then the fppf quotient sheaf \(G/H\) is representable by a smooth quasi-projective scheme over \(k\).

Proof. By Chevalley’s theorem (Theorem 5.2.1), there exists a representation \((V, r) \in \mathbf{Rep}_k(G)\) and a \(1\)-dimensional subspace \(L \subseteq V\) such that: \[ H = \operatorname{Stab}_G(L). \] Consider the projective space \(\mathbb{P}(V)\) and the point \(x := [L] \in \mathbb{P}(V)(k)\). The representation \(r\) induces a regular algebraic action \(G \times \mathbb{P}(V) \to \mathbb{P}(V)\). The stabilizer scheme of the point \(x\) is: \[ G_x = \operatorname{Stab}_G(L) = H. \] By Proposition 3, the orbit \(G \cdot x \subseteq \mathbb{P}(V)\) is a locally closed subscheme of \(\mathbb{P}(V)\) (hence a quasi-projective variety), and the orbit map \[ G \longrightarrow G \cdot x \] is faithfully flat (fppf). Consequently, the sequence of fppf sheaves \[ 0 \longrightarrow H \longrightarrow G \longrightarrow G \cdot x \longrightarrow 0 \] is exact. Therefore, the orbit \(G \cdot x\) represents the fppf quotient sheaf \(G/H\). Since \(G\) is smooth, the homogeneous space \(G/H \cong G \cdot x\) is also smooth.
