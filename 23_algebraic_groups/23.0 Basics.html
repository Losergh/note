23.0 Basics

Published

September 16, 2026

Throughout this chapter:

  • Let \(k\) be a fixed base field.
  • A \(k\)-variety is defined as a geometrically reduced, separated scheme of finite type over \(k\).
  • We denote by \(\mathrm{Sch}/k\) the category of schemes of finite type over \(k\), and by \(\mathrm{Var}/k\) the full subcategory of \(k\)-varieties.
  • For an affine scheme \(X = \operatorname{Spec} A\), \(A\) is a finitely generated \(k\)-algebra.

Definition 1 (Algebraic Group and Abelian Variety)  

  • An algebraic group over \(k\) is a group object in the category \(\mathrm{Sch}/k\). That is, it is a scheme \(G\) of finite type over \(k\) equipped with \(k\)-morphisms:

    • Multiplication: \(m: G \times_k G \to G\),
    • Inverse: \(i: G \to G\),
    • Identity section: \(e: \operatorname{Spec} k \to G\),

    satisfying the usual group axioms.

  • An abelian variety is an algebraic variety \(A \in \mathrm{Var}/k\) which is a proper, geometrically connected group object in \(\mathrm{Sch}/k\).

Lemma 1 (Smoothness of Geometrically Reduced Groups) Any geometrically reduced algebraic group \(G\) over \(k\) is smooth. Consequently, its connected components coincide with its irreducible components.

Proof. Proof sketch. Since \(G_{\bar{k}}\) is reduced and of finite type over an algebraically closed field, its smooth locus \(\operatorname{Sm}(G_{\bar{k}})\) is open and non-empty. For any closed point \(g \in G(\bar{k})\), the group translation \(L_g: G_{\bar{k}} \to G_{\bar{k}}\) is an automorphism mapping smooth points to smooth points. Transitivity of translations implies that every point of \(G_{\bar{k}}\) is smooth. Thus \(G\) is smooth over \(k\). Because smooth schemes are locally irreducible, the connected components must coincide with the irreducible components.

Lemma 2 (Rigidity Lemma) Let \(X, Y, Z\) be varieties over \(k\) with \(X\) proper and geometrically irreducible, and \(Y\) geometrically irreducible. Let \[ f: X \times_k Y \to Z \] be a \(k\)-morphism. Suppose there exists a \(k\)-rational point \(y_0 \in Y(k)\) such that \(f(X \times \{y_0\}) = \{z_0\}\) for some \(z_0 \in Z(k)\).

Then \(f\) factors through the canonical projection \(p_2: X \times_k Y \to Y\). That is, there exists a morphism \(g: Y \to Z\) such that \(f = g \circ p_2\).

Proof. Fix a rational point \(x_0 \in X(k)\) (extending scalars to \(\bar{k}\) if necessary). Define \(g: Y \to Z\) by the composition: \[ Y \xrightarrow{x_0 \times \mathrm{id}_Y} X \times_k Y \xrightarrow{f} Z. \] We show that \(f(x, y) = g(y)\) for all \(x \in X, y \in Y\).

  1. Choose an affine open neighborhood \(U \subseteq Z\) containing \(z_0\). The complement \(Z \setminus U\) is closed in \(Z\), so \(f^{-1}(Z \setminus U)\) is closed in \(X \times_k Y\).

  2. Since \(X\) is proper over \(k\), the projection \(p_2: X \times_k Y \to Y\) is universally closed. Thus: \[ W := p_2\big(f^{-1}(Z \setminus U)\big) = \{ y \in Y \mid \exists x \in X \text{ with } f(x, y) \notin U \} \] is a closed subset of \(Y\).

  3. By hypothesis, \(f(X \times \{y_0\}) = \{z_0\} \subseteq U\), so \(y_0 \notin W\). Hence \(Y \setminus W\) is an open neighborhood of \(y_0\). Pick an affine open neighborhood \(V \subseteq Y \setminus W\) containing \(y_0\).

  4. By construction, \(f(X \times V) \subseteq U\). Since \(U\) is affine, we may choose a closed embedding \(U \hookrightarrow \mathbb{A}^n\). For every closed point \(y \in V\), the fiber \(X \times \{y\} \cong X\) is proper and connected. Its image in the affine space \(\mathbb{A}^n\) under \(f\) must be both proper and affine, hence it must be a single point: \[ f(X \times \{y\}) = \{f(x_0, y)\} = \{g(y)\}. \]

  5. Therefore, \(f|_{X \times V} = (g \circ p_2)|_{X \times V}\). Because \(X \times_k Y\) is reduced and \(Z\) is separated, two morphisms agreeing on a non-empty open subscheme are identical everywhere (Reduced-to-Separated Theorem). Thus \(f = g \circ p_2\).

Proposition 1 (Proper Connected Groups are Abelian) Any proper connected smooth algebraic group \(G\) over \(k\) is abelian. In particular, abelian varieties are commutative.

Proof. Consider the commutator morphism: \[ f: G \times_k G \to G, \quad (x, y) \mapsto x y x^{-1} y^{-1}. \] Observe that \(f(G \times \{e\}) = \{e\}\). Since \(G\) is proper and connected, Lemma 2 implies that \(f\) factors through the projection to the second factor: \[ f(x, y) = f(e, y) = e y e^{-1} y^{-1} = e. \] Hence \(xyx^{-1}y^{-1} = e\) for all \(x, y \in G\), meaning \(xy = yx\). Thus \(G\) is abelian.

Proposition 2 (Separatedness of Group Schemes) Every group scheme \(G\) of finite type over a field \(k\) is separated.

Proof. A morphism \(G \to \operatorname{Spec} k\) is separated if and only if the diagonal \(\Delta: G \to G \times_k G\) is a closed immersion.

Consider the difference morphism \(\mu: G \times_k G \to G\) defined by \((g_1, g_2) \mapsto g_1 g_2^{-1}\). The diagonal \(\Delta(G)\) is the scheme-theoretic pullback of the identity section \(e: \operatorname{Spec} k \to G\) along \(\mu\): \[ \begin{CD} G @>{\Delta}>> G \times_k G \\ @VVV @VV{\mu}V \\ \operatorname{Spec} k @>{e}>> G \end{CD} \] Because base change preserves closed immersions, it suffices to prove that \(e: \operatorname{Spec} k \to G\) is a closed immersion. The image of \(e\) is a single point \(e \in G\). Since \(G\) is of finite type over \(k\), the point \(e\) has residue field \(k(e) \cong k\) and is closed. Thus \(e\) is a closed immersion, which implies \(\Delta\) is a closed immersion. Therefore, \(G\) is separated over \(k\).

Lemma 3 (Open Subgroups are Closed)  

  1. If \(G\) is a group scheme over \(k\) such that \(G_{\mathrm{red}} \times_k G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is reduced, then \(G_{\mathrm{red}} \hookrightarrow G\) is a closed subgroup scheme. In particular, if \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is geometrically reduced (e.g., if \(k\) is perfect), then \(G_{\mathrm{red}} \hookrightarrow G\) is a smooth closed subgroup scheme of \(G\).

  2. Let \(\varphi: G' \to G\) be a homomorphism of group schemes over \(k\). If the image \(\varphi(G')\) is open in \(G\), then \(\varphi(G')\) is also closed in \(G\).

Proof. Proof of (2). Let \(U = \varphi(G') \subseteq G\) be open. Endow the complement \(Z := G \setminus U\) with the reduced subscheme structure. It suffices to show that \(Z\) is open.

Consider the action map \(Z \times_k G' \to G\) given by \((z, g') \mapsto z \cdot \varphi(g')\). For any \(g' \in G'\), the translation \(x \mapsto x \cdot \varphi(g')\) is an automorphism of \(G\). Since \(U = \varphi(G')\) is stable under right multiplication by \(\varphi(G')\), its complement \(Z\) is also stable under multiplication by \(\varphi(G')\).

Because translations are open morphisms, the image of \(Z \times_k G' \to G\) is a union of translates of \(Z\), which is open. But this image is precisely \(Z\) itself. Hence \(Z\) is open, which proves \(U = G \setminus Z\) is closed.

Lemma 4 (Immersions of Group Schemes are Closed) Let \(i: G' \hookrightarrow G\) be an immersion of group schemes over \(k\). Then \(i\) is a closed immersion.

Proof. Without loss of generality, assume \(G\) and \(G'\) are reduced. Let \(\overline{G'}\) denote the scheme-theoretic closure of \(i(G')\) in \(G\) equipped with the reduced structure. Since multiplication \(m: G \times_k G \to G\) is continuous and maps \(G' \times_k G'\) into \(G'\), it maps the closure \(\overline{G'} \times_k \overline{G'}\) into \(\overline{G'}\). Thus \(\overline{G'}\) is a closed subgroup scheme of \(G\).

Since \(i\) is an immersion, \(i(G')\) is an open subscheme of \(\overline{G'}\). By Lemma 3, an open subgroup scheme of \(\overline{G'}\) is closed in \(\overline{G'}\). Hence \(i(G') = \overline{G'}\), so \(i(G')\) is closed in \(G\).

Lemma 5 (Connected Implies Irreducible) Let \(G\) be a group scheme of finite type over \(k\).

  1. If \(G\) is irreducible, then \(G\) is quasi-compact.
  2. If \(G\) is connected, then \(G\) is irreducible.

(Reference: The Stacks Project, Tag 047R, Tag 047S).

Proposition 3 (Subgroup Schemes and Kernels)  

  1. A closed subscheme \(H \subseteq G\) is a normal subgroup scheme (denoted \(H \trianglelefteq G\)) if for all \(T \in \mathrm{Sch}/k\), \(H(T)\) is a normal subgroup of \(G(T)\) (equivalently, \(H(R) \trianglelefteq G(R)\) for all \(R \in \mathrm{Alg}_k\)).

  2. Let \(\varphi: G \to G'\) be a homomorphism of group schemes. The scheme-theoretic kernel is defined as the fiber product: \[ \ker \varphi := \operatorname{Spec} k \times_{e, G', \varphi} G = \varphi^{-1}(e). \] Then \(\ker \varphi\) is a closed normal subgroup scheme of \(G\).

  3. On functor of points, \(\ker(\varphi)(R) = \ker(G(R) \to G'(R))\) for all \(R \in \mathrm{Alg}_k\).

Proposition 4 (Homomorphisms and Dimensions) Let \(\varphi: G \to G'\) be a homomorphism of algebraic groups over \(k\).

  1. The scheme-theoretic image \(\varphi(G)\) is closed in \(G'\). We denote \(\operatorname{Im} \varphi := \varphi(G)_{\mathrm{red}}\).

  2. Dimension Formula: \[ \dim G = \dim \ker \varphi + \dim \operatorname{Im} \varphi. \]

Proof. Idea of Proof. By Chevalley’s Theorem on constructible sets, \(\varphi(G)\) contains a dense open subset of its closure. Group translations then show that \(\varphi(G)\) is open in its closure \(\overline{\varphi(G)}\). By Lemma 3, an open subgroup of \(\overline{\varphi(G)}\) is closed, whence \(\varphi(G) = \overline{\varphi(G)}\).

Proposition 5 (Image Factorization and Flatness)  

  1. If \(G'\) is reduced and \(\varphi: G \to G'\) is dominant, then \(\varphi\) is an fppf morphism (faithfully flat locally of finite presentation).

  2. If \(G\) is smooth, any homomorphism \(\varphi: G \to H\) factors canonically as: \[ G \xrightarrow{p} I \xrightarrow{i} H \] where \(p\) is fppf and \(i\) is a closed immersion into \(H\).

Proof. Set \(I := \varphi(G)\) with reduced subscheme structure. By generic flatness, there exists a dense open subscheme \(U \subseteq I\) over which \(\varphi^{-1}(U) \to U\) is flat. Since translations by rational points (over \(\bar{k}\)) act transitively on \(I\), translates of \(U\) cover \(I\). Therefore, \(\varphi_{\bar{k}}\) is flat everywhere. Being surjective (image is closed) between noetherian schemes, it is fppf.

Remark 1 (Hopf Algebras and Functor of Points). An affine scheme \(G = \operatorname{Spec} A\) is a group scheme if and only if its coordinate ring \(A = \mathcal{O}(G)\) is a commutative Hopf algebra over \(k\), equipped with:

  • Comultiplication \(\Delta: A \to A \otimes_k A\),
  • Counit \(\varepsilon: A \to k\),
  • Antipode \(S: A \to A\).

We have an anti-equivalence of categories: \[ \{\text{Commutative f.g. Hopf } k\text{-algebras}\}^{\mathrm{op}} \xrightarrow{\sim} \{\text{Affine / Linear } k\text{-algebraic groups}\}. \]

Via the functor of points, the Yoneda embedding yields a fully faithful functor: \[ \mathbf{GrpSch}/k \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Alg}_k, \mathbf{Grp}), \quad G \mapsto (R \mapsto G(R) = \operatorname{Hom}_{k\text{-alg}}(A, R)). \]

Example 1 (Basic Examples)  

  1. Finite Constant Group: Let \(\Gamma\) be a finite abstract group. The coordinate algebra is \(A = \prod_{g \in \Gamma} k \cdot e_g\) with orthogonal idempotents \(e_g\).

    • Comultiplication: \(\Delta(e_x) = \sum_{yz = x} e_y \otimes e_z\),
    • Antipode: \(S(e_x) = e_{x^{-1}}\),
    • Counit: \(\varepsilon(e_x) = \delta_{x, e}\).

    On \(R\)-points: \[ G(R) = \left\{ (r_g)_{g \in \Gamma} \in R^\Gamma \;\middle|\; \sum_{g \in \Gamma} r_g = 1, \, r_g r_h = 0 \text{ for } g \neq h \right\}. \]

  2. Standard Matrix Groups (for \(n \in \mathbb{N}\)):

    • General Linear Group \(\mathrm{GL}_n\) (Reductive): \[ \mathrm{GL}_n(R) = \{ M \in \mathrm{Mat}_{n \times n}(R) \mid \det(M) \in R^\times \}. \]

    • Standard Borel Subgroup \(B_n\) (Solvable): \[ B_n(R) = \{ (x_{ij}) \in \mathrm{GL}_n(R) \mid x_{ij} = 0 \text{ for all } i > j \}. \]

    • Standard Unipotent Subgroup \(U_n\) (Unipotent): \[ U_n(R) = \{ (x_{ij}) \in B_n(R) \mid x_{ii} = 1 \text{ for all } 1 \le i \le n \}. \]

    • Standard Maximal Torus \(T_n\) (Split Torus): \[ T_n(R) = \{ (x_{ij}) \in B_n(R) \mid x_{ij} = 0 \text{ for all } i \neq j \} \cong (\mathbb{G}_{m, R})^n. \]

    These satisfy the inclusion hierarchy: \[ U_n \trianglelefteq B_n \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_n, \quad T_n \subseteq B_n, \quad B_n = T_n \ltimes U_n. \]

Proposition 6 (Existence of the Largest Étale Subalgebra) Let \(X\) be a scheme of finite type over \(k\). Then there exists a unique largest étale \(k\)-subalgebra \(\pi_0(X) \subseteq \mathcal{O}(X)\).

Proof.

  1. If \(A_1, A_2 \subseteq \mathcal{O}(X)\) are étale \(k\)-subalgebras, the subring generated by them is the image of the canonical map \(A_1 \otimes_k A_2 \to \mathcal{O}(X)\). Since the tensor product of étale algebras is étale and any quotient of an étale algebra over a field is étale, the composite is étale.

  2. For any étale subalgebra \(A \subseteq \mathcal{O}(X)\), base change to the separable closure satisfies: \[ A \otimes_k k^{\mathrm{sep}} \subseteq \mathcal{O}(X) \otimes_k k^{\mathrm{sep}} = \mathcal{O}(X_{k^{\mathrm{sep}}}). \] Since \(A \otimes_k k^{\mathrm{sep}} \cong \prod_{i=1}^m k^{\mathrm{sep}}\), it admits a complete system of orthogonal idempotents \(e_1, \dots, e_m\). Each \(e_i\) defines a non-empty open and closed subscheme \(V(1 - e_i)\) of \(X_{k^{\mathrm{sep}}}\). Thus \(m \le \#\{\text{connected components of } X_{k^{\mathrm{sep}}}\}\).

  3. Because \(X\) is of finite type, it has finitely many connected components. The dimension \(\dim_k A\) is uniformly bounded, guaranteeing the existence of a unique maximal étale subalgebra.

Definition 2 (The Scheme \(\pi_0(X)\))  

  1. Let \(A \subseteq \mathcal{O}(X)\) be the largest étale \(k\)-subalgebra. We define: \[ \pi_0(X) := \operatorname{Spec} A. \] The inclusion \(A \hookrightarrow \mathcal{O}(X)\) induces a canonical morphism \(X \to \pi_0(X)\).

  2. Universal Property: For any étale \(k\)-scheme \(Y\) and any \(k\)-morphism \(f: X \to Y\), there exists a unique morphism \(\bar{f}: \pi_0(X) \to Y\) such that the following diagram commutes: \[ \begin{CD} X @>{f}>> Y \\ @VVV @| \\ \pi_0(X) @>{\exists! \bar{f}}>> Y \end{CD} \]

  3. Properties:

    • The fibers of \(X \to \pi_0(X)\) are the connected components of \(X\).
    • For every \(y \in \pi_0(X)\), the fiber \(X_y\) is geometrically connected over the residue field \(k(y)\).
    • \(\pi_0\) preserves finite products: \(\pi_0(X \times_k Y) \cong \pi_0(X) \times_k \pi_0(Y)\).
    • The canonical morphism \(X \to \pi_0(X)\) is fppf.

Proposition 7 (Geometric Connectedness Criteria)  

  1. If \(X\) is connected and \(X(k) \neq \emptyset\), then \(X\) is geometrically connected.

  2. If \(X\) is geometrically connected and \(Y\) is connected, then \(X \times_k Y\) is connected.

Proof.

  1. If \(X\) is connected, \(\pi_0(X) = \operatorname{Spec} L\), where \(L/k\) is a finite separable field extension. A rational point \(x \in X(k)\) provides a section \(\operatorname{Spec} k \to X \to \operatorname{Spec} L\), forcing \(L \cong k\). Thus \(\pi_0(X) = \operatorname{Spec} k\), so \(X\) is geometrically connected.

  2. We have \(\pi_0(X) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k\). By product preservation, \[ \pi_0(X \times_k Y) \cong \pi_0(X) \times_k \pi_0(Y) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k \times_k \pi_0(Y) \cong \pi_0(Y). \] Since \(Y\) is connected, \(\pi_0(Y) = \operatorname{Spec} L\) for a field \(L\). Thus \(\pi_0(X \times_k Y) \cong \operatorname{Spec} L\), which shows \(X \times_k Y\) is connected.

Definition 3 (The Identity Component \(G^\circ\))  

  1. The identity component \(G^\circ\) of an algebraic group \(G\) is the connected component containing the identity section \(e\). Endowed with its open subscheme structure, \(G^\circ\) is a smooth, geometrically connected, open and closed normal subgroup scheme of \(G\).

  2. For any open subgroup \(G' \subseteq G\) of finite index, \(G^\circ \subseteq G' \subseteq G\), and \((G^\circ)^\circ = G^\circ\).

Proposition 8 (\(\pi_0(G)\) and the Connected Component) Let \(G\) be an algebraic group over \(k\).

  1. \(\pi_0(G)\) is a finite étale group scheme over \(k\), and the canonical morphism \(G \to \pi_0(G)\) is a homomorphism of algebraic groups.

  2. The identity component \(G^\circ := \ker(G \to \pi_0(G))\) is a geometrically connected, normal closed and open subgroup scheme of \(G\). For any field extension \(L/k\), we have \((G_L)^\circ = (G^\circ)_L\).

  3. \(G\) is connected \(\iff \pi_0(G) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k \iff G\) is geometrically connected.

  4. Formation of the identity component commutes with products: \((G \times_k H)^\circ \cong G^\circ \times_k H^\circ\).

  5. If \(H\) is a connected group scheme over \(k\), any homomorphism \(\psi: H \to G\) factors uniquely through \(G^\circ \hookrightarrow G\).

Proof.

  • Since \(\pi_0\) commutes with finite products, the group morphisms \(m, i, e\) on \(G\) induce group structure morphisms on \(\pi_0(G)\) by functoriality.

  • For (3), since \(e \in G^\circ(k)\), \(G^\circ\) has a \(k\)-rational point. Proposition 7(1) implies that \(G^\circ\) is geometrically connected.

  • For (5), since \(H\) is connected, \(\pi_0(H) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k\). Functoriality yields a commutative diagram: \[ \begin{CD} H @>{\psi}>> G \\ @VVV @VVV \\ \pi_0(H) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k @>{e}>> \pi_0(G) \end{CD} \] Thus the composition \(H \to G \to \pi_0(G)\) is the trivial map, which implies that \(\psi\) factors uniquely through \(G^\circ = \ker(G \to \pi_0(G))\).

Definition 4 (Exact Sequences of Group Schemes) A sequence of homomorphisms of group schemes over \(k\): \[ 1 \longrightarrow N \xrightarrow{\quad i \quad} G \xrightarrow{\quad p \quad} Q \longrightarrow 1 \] is said to be exact if:

  1. \(i: N \hookrightarrow G\) is a closed immersion identifying \(N\) with a normal subgroup scheme of \(G\);
  2. \(p: G \to Q\) is an fppf morphism;
  3. \(i(N) = \ker(p)\).
