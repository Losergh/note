Basics of Algebraic Groups

Algebraic Groups Note x, Section 0

Published

September 15, 2026

Conventions

Throughout this section:

  • Let \(k\) be a fixed base field.
  • A \(k\)-variety is defined as a reduced, separated scheme of finite type over \(k\).
  • We denote by \(\mathrm{Sch}/k\) the category of schemes of finite type over \(k\), and by \(\mathrm{Var}/k\) the full subcategory of \(k\)-varieties.
  • For an affine scheme \(X = \operatorname{Spec} A\), \(A\) is a finitely generated \(k\)-algebra.

1. Group Objects and the Rigidity Lemma

NoteDefinition x.0.0 (Algebraic Group and Abelian Variety)
  • An algebraic group over \(k\) is a group object in the category \(\mathrm{Sch}/k\). That is, it is a scheme \(G\) of finite type over \(k\) equipped with \(k\)-morphisms:
    • Multiplication: \(m: G \times_k G \to G\),
    • Inverse: \(i: G \to G\),
    • Identity section: \(e: \operatorname{Spec} k \to G\), satisfying the usual group axioms.
  • An abelian variety is an algebraic variety \(A \in \mathrm{Var}/k\) which is a proper, geometrically connected group object in \(\mathrm{Sch}/k\).
TipLemma x.0.1 (Smoothness of Geometrically Reduced Groups)

Any geometrically reduced algebraic group \(G\) over \(k\) is smooth. Consequently, its connected components coincide with its irreducible components.

Proof sketch. Since \(G_{\bar{k}}\) is reduced and of finite type over an algebraically closed field, its smooth locus \(\operatorname{Sm}(G_{\bar{k}})\) is open and non-empty. For any closed point \(g \in G(\bar{k})\), the group translation \(L_g: G_{\bar{k}} \to G_{\bar{k}}\) is an automorphism mapping smooth points to smooth points. Transitivity of translations implies that every point of \(G_{\bar{k}}\) is smooth. Thus \(G\) is smooth over \(k\). Because smooth schemes are locally irreducible, the connected components must coincide with the irreducible components. \(\square\)

TipLemma x.0.2 (Rigidity Lemma)

Let \(X, Y, Z\) be varieties over \(k\) with \(X\) proper and geometrically irreducible, and \(Y\) geometrically irreducible. Let \[ f: X \times_k Y \to Z \] be a \(k\)-morphism. Suppose there exists a \(k\)-rational point \(y_0 \in Y(k)\) such that \(f(X \times \{y_0\}) = \{z_0\}\) for some \(z_0 \in Z(k)\).

Then \(f\) factors through the canonical projection \(p_2: X \times_k Y \to Y\). That is, there exists a morphism \(g: Y \to Z\) such that \(f = g \circ p_2\).

Proof. Fix a rational point \(x_0 \in X(k)\) (extending scalars to \(\bar{k}\) if necessary). Define \(g: Y \to Z\) by the composition: \[ Y \xrightarrow{x_0 \times \mathrm{id}_Y} X \times_k Y \xrightarrow{f} Z. \] We show that \(f(x, y) = g(y)\) for all \(x \in X, y \in Y\).

  1. Choose an affine open neighborhood \(U \subseteq Z\) containing \(z_0\). The complement \(Z \setminus U\) is closed in \(Z\), so \(f^{-1}(Z \setminus U)\) is closed in \(X \times_k Y\).
  2. Since \(X\) is proper over \(k\), the projection \(p_2: X \times_k Y \to Y\) is universally closed. Thus: \[ W := p_2\big(f^{-1}(Z \setminus U)\big) = \{ y \in Y \mid \exists x \in X \text{ with } f(x, y) \notin U \} \] is a closed subset of \(Y\).
  3. By hypothesis, \(f(X \times \{y_0\}) = \{z_0\} \subseteq U\), so \(y_0 \notin W\). Hence \(Y \setminus W\) is an open neighborhood of \(y_0\). Pick an affine open neighborhood \(V \subseteq Y \setminus W\) containing \(y_0\).
  4. By construction, \(f(X \times V) \subseteq U\). Since \(U\) is affine, we may choose a closed embedding \(U \hookrightarrow \mathbb{A}^n\). For every closed point \(y \in V\), the fiber \(X \times \{y\} \cong X\) is proper and connected. Its image in the affine space \(\mathbb{A}^n\) under \(f\) must be both proper and affine, hence it must be a single point: \[ f(X \times \{y\}) = \{f(x_0, y)\} = \{g(y)\}. \]
  5. Therefore, \(f|_{X \times V} = (g \circ p_2)|_{X \times V}\). Because \(X \times_k Y\) is reduced and \(Z\) is separated, two morphisms agreeing on a non-empty open subscheme are identical everywhere (Reduced-to-Separated Theorem). Thus \(f = g \circ p_2\). \(\square\)
ImportantProposition x.0.3 (Proper Connected Groups are Abelian)

Any proper connected smooth algebraic group \(G\) over \(k\) is abelian. In particular, abelian varieties are commutative.

Proof. Consider the commutator morphism: \[ f: G \times_k G \to G, \quad (x, y) \mapsto x y x^{-1} y^{-1}. \] Observe that \(f(G \times \{e\}) = \{e\}\). Since \(G\) is proper and connected, Lemma x.0.2 (Rigidity) implies that \(f\) factors through the projection to the second factor: \[ f(x, y) = f(e, y) = e y e^{-1} y^{-1} = e. \] Hence \(xyx^{-1}y^{-1} = e\) for all \(x, y \in G\), meaning \(xy = yx\). Thus \(G\) is abelian. \(\square\)

2. Topological and Subgroup Properties

ImportantProposition x.0.4 (Separatedness of Group Schemes)

Every group scheme \(G\) of finite type over a field \(k\) is separated.

Proof. A morphism \(G \to \operatorname{Spec} k\) is separated if and only if the diagonal \(\Delta: G \to G \times_k G\) is a closed immersion.

Consider the difference morphism \(\mu: G \times_k G \to G\) defined by \((g_1, g_2) \mapsto g_1 g_2^{-1}\). The diagonal \(\Delta(G)\) is the scheme-theoretic pullback of the identity section \(e: \operatorname{Spec} k \to G\) along \(\mu\): \[ \begin{matrix} G & \xrightarrow{\Delta} & G \times_k G \\ \downarrow & \square & \downarrow \mu \\ \operatorname{Spec} k & \xrightarrow{e} & G \end{matrix} \] Because base change preserves closed immersions, it suffices to prove that \(e: \operatorname{Spec} k \to G\) is a closed immersion. The image of \(e\) is a single point \(e \in G\). Since \(G\) is of finite type over \(k\), the point \(e\) has residue field \(k(e) \cong k\) and is closed. Thus \(e\) is a closed immersion, which implies \(\Delta\) is a closed immersion. Therefore, \(G\) is separated over \(k\). \(\square\)

TipLemma x.0.5 (Open Subgroups are Closed)
  1. If \(G\) is a group scheme over \(k\) such that \(G_{\mathrm{red}} \times_k G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is reduced, then \(G_{\mathrm{red}} \hookrightarrow G\) is a closed subgroup scheme.
  2. Let \(\varphi: G' \to G\) be a homomorphism of group schemes over \(k\). If the image \(\varphi(G')\) is open in \(G\), then \(\varphi(G')\) is also closed in \(G\).

Proof of (2). Let \(U = \varphi(G') \subseteq G\) be open. Endow the complement \(Z := G \setminus U\) with the reduced subscheme structure. It suffices to show that \(Z\) is open.

Consider the action map \(Z \times_k G' \to G\) given by \((z, g') \mapsto z \cdot \varphi(g')\). For any \(g' \in G'\), the translation \(x \mapsto x \cdot \varphi(g')\) is an automorphism of \(G\). Since \(U = \varphi(G')\) is stable under right multiplication by \(\varphi(G')\), its complement \(Z\) is also stable under multiplication by \(\varphi(G')\).

Because translations are open morphisms, the image of \(Z \times_k G' \to G\) is a union of translates of \(Z\), which is open. But this image is precisely \(Z\) itself. Hence \(Z\) is open, which proves \(U = G \setminus Z\) is closed. \(\square\)

TipLemma x.0.6 (Immersions of Group Schemes are Closed)

Let \(i: G' \hookrightarrow G\) be an immersion of group schemes over \(k\). Then \(i\) is a closed immersion.

Proof. Without loss of generality, assume \(G\) and \(G'\) are reduced. Let \(\overline{G'}\) denote the scheme-theoretic closure of \(i(G')\) in \(G\) equipped with the reduced structure. Since multiplication \(m: G \times_k G \to G\) is continuous and maps \(G' \times_k G'\) into \(G'\), it maps the closure \(\overline{G'} \times_k \overline{G'}\) into \(\overline{G'}\). Thus \(\overline{G'}\) is a closed subgroup scheme of \(G\).

Since \(i\) is an immersion, \(i(G')\) is an open subscheme of \(\overline{G'}\). By Lemma x.0.5, an open subgroup scheme of \(\overline{G'}\) is closed in \(\overline{G'}\). Hence \(i(G') = \overline{G'}\), so \(i(G')\) is closed in \(G\). \(\square\)

TipLemma x.0.7 (Connected Implies Irreducible)

Let \(G\) be a group scheme of finite type over \(k\). 1. If \(G\) is irreducible, then \(G\) is quasi-compact. 2. If \(G\) is connected, then \(G\) is irreducible.

(Reference: The Stacks Project, Tag 047R, Tag 047S).

3. Kernels, Images, and Homomorphisms

NoteDefinition & Proposition x.0.8 (Subgroup Schemes and Kernels)
  1. A closed subscheme \(H \subseteq G\) is a normal subgroup scheme (denoted \(H \trianglelefteq G\)) if for all \(T \in \mathrm{Sch}/k\), \(H(T)\) is a normal subgroup of \(G(T)\) (equivalently, \(H(R) \trianglelefteq G(R)\) for all \(R \in \mathrm{Alg}_k\)).
  2. Let \(\varphi: G \to G'\) be a homomorphism of group schemes. The scheme-theoretic kernel is defined as the fiber product: \[ \ker \varphi := \operatorname{Spec} k \times_{e, G', \varphi} G = \varphi^{-1}(e). \] Then \(\ker \varphi\) is a closed normal subgroup scheme of \(G\).
  3. On functor of points, \(\ker(\varphi)(R) = \ker(G(R) \to G'(R))\) for all \(R \in \mathrm{Alg}_k\).
ImportantProposition x.0.9 (Homomorphisms and Dimensions)

Let \(\varphi: G \to G'\) be a homomorphism of algebraic groups over \(k\). 1. The scheme-theoretic image \(\varphi(G)\) is closed in \(G'\). We denote \(\operatorname{Im} \varphi := \varphi(G)_{\mathrm{red}}\). 2. Dimension Formula: \[ \dim G = \dim \ker \varphi + \dim \operatorname{Im} \varphi. \] 3. \(\varphi: G \to \operatorname{Im} \varphi\) is smooth if and only if \(\ker \varphi\) is geometrically reduced, if and only if the differential \(d\varphi_e: T_e(G) \to T_e(\operatorname{Im} \varphi)\) is surjective.

Idea of Proof. By Chevalley’s Theorem on constructible sets, \(\varphi(G)\) contains a dense open subset of its closure. Group translations then show that \(\varphi(G)\) is open in its closure \(\overline{\varphi(G)}\). By Lemma x.0.5, an open subgroup of \(\overline{\varphi(G)}\) is closed, whence \(\varphi(G) = \overline{\varphi(G)}\). \(\square\)

ImportantProposition x.0.10 (Image Factorization and Flatness)
  1. If \(G'\) is reduced and \(\varphi: G \to G'\) is dominant, then \(\varphi\) is an fppf morphism (faithfully flat of finite presentation).
  2. If \(G\) is smooth, any homomorphism \(\varphi: G \to H\) factors canonically as: \[ G \xrightarrow{p} I \xrightarrow{i} H \] where \(p\) is fppf and \(i\) is a closed immersion into \(H\).

Proof. Set \(I := \varphi(G)\) with reduced subscheme structure. By generic flatness, there exists a dense open subscheme \(U \subseteq I\) over which \(\varphi^{-1}(U) \to U\) is flat. Since translations by rational points (over \(\bar{k}\)) act transitively on \(I\), translates of \(U\) cover \(I\). Therefore, \(\varphi\) is flat everywhere. Being surjective and of finite type, it is fppf. \(\square\)

NoteDefinition x.0.11 (The Identity Component \(G^\circ\))
  1. The identity component \(G^\circ\) of an algebraic group \(G\) is the connected component containing the identity section \(e\). Endowed with its open subscheme structure, \(G^\circ\) is a smooth, geometrically connected, open and closed normal subgroup scheme of \(G\).
  2. For any open subgroup \(G' \subseteq G\) of finite index, \(G^\circ \subseteq G' \subseteq G\), and \((G^\circ)^\circ = G^\circ\).

4. Affine Algebraic Groups and Hopf Algebras

NoteRemark x.0.12 (Hopf Algebras and Functor of Points)

An affine scheme \(G = \operatorname{Spec} A\) is a group scheme if and only if its coordinate ring \(A = \mathcal{O}(G)\) is a commutative Hopf algebra over \(k\), equipped with: - Comultiplication \(\Delta: A \to A \otimes_k A\), - Counit \(\varepsilon: A \to k\), - Antipode \(S: A \to A\).

We have an anti-equivalence of categories: \[ \{\text{Commutative f.g. Hopf } k\text{-algebras}\}^{\mathrm{op}} \xrightarrow{\sim} \{\text{Affine / Linear } k\text{-algebraic groups}\}. \] Via the functor of points, the Yoneda embedding yields a fully faithful functor: \[ \mathbf{GrpSch}/k \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Alg}_k, \mathbf{Grp}), \quad G \mapsto (R \mapsto G(R) = \operatorname{Hom}_{k\text{-alg}}(A, R)). \]

NoteExample x.0.13 (Basic Examples)
  1. Finite Constant Group: Let \(\Gamma\) be a finite abstract group. The coordinate algebra is \(A = \prod_{g \in \Gamma} k \cdot e_g\) with orthogonal idempotents \(e_g\).
    • Comultiplication: \(\Delta(e_x) = \sum_{yz = x} e_y \otimes e_z\),
    • Antipode: \(S(e_x) = e_{x^{-1}}\),
    • Counit: \(\varepsilon(e_x) = \delta_{x, e}\). On \(R\)-points: \(G(R) = \{ (r_g)_{g \in \Gamma} \in R^\Gamma \mid \sum r_g = 1, \, r_g r_h = 0 \text{ for } g \neq h \}\).
  2. Standard Matrix Groups (for \(n \in \mathbb{N}\)):
    • General Linear Group \(\mathrm{GL}_n\) (Reductive): \[ \mathrm{GL}_n(R) = \{ M \in \mathrm{Mat}_{n \times n}(R) \mid \det(M) \in R^\times \}. \]
    • Standard Borel Subgroup \(B_n\) (Solvable): \[ B_n(R) = \{ (x_{ij}) \in \mathrm{GL}_n(R) \mid x_{ij} = 0 \text{ for all } i > j \}. \]
    • Standard Unipotent Subgroup \(U_n\) (Unipotent): \[ U_n(R) = \{ (x_{ij}) \in B_n(R) \mid x_{ii} = 1 \text{ for all } 1 \le i \le n \}. \]
    • Standard Maximal Torus \(T_n\) (Split Torus): \[ T_n(R) = \{ (x_{ij}) \in B_n(R) \mid x_{ij} = 0 \text{ for all } i \neq j \} \cong (\mathbb{G}_{m, R})^n. \] These satisfy the inclusion hierarchy: \[ U_n \trianglelefteq B_n \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_n, \quad T_n \subseteq B_n, \quad B_n = T_n \ltimes U_n. \]
TipProposition x.0.14 (Reduction of Group Schemes)

Let \(G\) be an algebraic group over \(k\). If \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is geometrically reduced (e.g., if \(k\) is perfect), then \(G_{\mathrm{red}} \hookrightarrow G\) is a smooth closed subgroup scheme of \(G\).

5. The Étale Quotient \(\pi_0(X)\) and Component Groups

ImportantProposition x.0.15 (Existence of the Largest Étale Subalgebra)

Let \(X\) be a scheme of finite type over \(k\). Then there exists a unique largest étale \(k\)-subalgebra \(\pi_0(X) \subseteq \mathcal{O}(X)\).

Proof. 1. If \(A_1, A_2 \subseteq \mathcal{O}(X)\) are étale \(k\)-subalgebras, the subring generated by them is the image of the canonical map \(A_1 \otimes_k A_2 \to \mathcal{O}(X)\). Since the tensor product of étale algebras is étale and any quotient of an étale algebra over a field is étale, the composite is étale. 2. For any étale subalgebra \(A \subseteq \mathcal{O}(X)\), base change to the separable closure satisfies: \[ A \otimes_k k^{\mathrm{sep}} \subseteq \mathcal{O}(X) \otimes_k k^{\mathrm{sep}} = \mathcal{O}(X_{k^{\mathrm{sep}}}). \] Since \(A \otimes_k k^{\mathrm{sep}} \cong \prod_{i=1}^m k^{\mathrm{sep}}\), it admits a complete system of orthogonal idempotents \(e_1, \dots, e_m\). Each \(e_i\) defines a non-empty open and closed subscheme \(V(1 - e_i)\) of \(X_{k^{\mathrm{sep}}}\). Thus \(m \le \#\{\text{connected components of } X_{k^{\mathrm{sep}}}\}\). 3. Because \(X\) is of finite type, it has finitely many connected components. The dimension \(\dim_k A\) is uniformly bounded, guaranteeing the existence of a unique maximal étale subalgebra. \(\square\)

NoteDefinition x.0.16 (The Scheme \(\pi_0(X)\))
  1. Let \(A \subseteq \mathcal{O}(X)\) be the largest étale \(k\)-subalgebra. We define: \[ \pi_0(X) := \operatorname{Spec} A. \] The inclusion \(A \hookrightarrow \mathcal{O}(X)\) induces a canonical morphism \(X \to \pi_0(X)\).
  2. Universal Property: For any étale \(k\)-scheme \(Y\) and any \(k\)-morphism \(f: X \to Y\), there exists a unique morphism \(\bar{f}: \pi_0(X) \to Y\) making the diagram commute: \[ \begin{matrix} X & \xrightarrow{f} & Y \\ \downarrow & \nearrow_{\exists! \bar{f}} & \\ \pi_0(X) & & \end{matrix} \]
  3. Properties:
    • The fibers of \(X \to \pi_0(X)\) are the connected components of \(X\).
    • For every \(y \in \pi_0(X)\), the fiber \(X_y\) is geometrically connected over the residue field \(k(y)\).
    • \(\pi_0\) preserves finite products: \(\pi_0(X \times_k Y) \cong \pi_0(X) \times_k \pi_0(Y)\).
    • The canonical morphism \(X \to \pi_0(X)\) is fppf.
TipProposition x.0.17 (Geometric Connectedness Criteria)
  1. If \(X\) is connected and \(X(k) \neq \emptyset\), then \(X\) is geometrically connected.
  2. If \(X\) is geometrically connected and \(Y\) is connected, then \(X \times_k Y\) is connected.

Proof. 1. If \(X\) is connected, \(\pi_0(X) = \operatorname{Spec} L\), where \(L/k\) is a finite separable field extension. A rational point \(x \in X(k)\) provides a section \(\operatorname{Spec} k \to X \to \operatorname{Spec} L\), forcing \(L \cong k\). Thus \(\pi_0(X) = \operatorname{Spec} k\), so \(X\) is geometrically connected. 2. We have \(\pi_0(X) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k\). By product preservation, \(\pi_0(X \times_k Y) \cong \pi_0(X) \times_k \pi_0(Y) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k \times_k \pi_0(Y) \cong \pi_0(Y)\). Since \(Y\) is connected, \(\pi_0(Y) = \operatorname{Spec} L\) for a field \(L\). Thus \(\pi_0(X \times_k Y) \cong \operatorname{Spec} L\), which shows \(X \times_k Y\) is connected. \(\square\)

ImportantProposition x.0.18 (\(\pi_0(G)\) and the Connected Component)

Let \(G\) be an algebraic group over \(k\). 1. \(\pi_0(G)\) is a finite étale group scheme over \(k\), and the canonical morphism \(G \to \pi_0(G)\) is a homomorphism of algebraic groups. 2. The identity component \(G^\circ := \ker(G \to \pi_0(G))\) is a geometrically connected, normal closed and open subgroup scheme of \(G\). For any field extension \(L/k\), we have \((G_L)^\circ = (G^\circ)_L\). 3. \(G\) is connected \(\iff \pi_0(G) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k \iff G\) is geometrically connected. 4. Formation of the identity component commutes with products: \((G \times_k H)^\circ \cong G^\circ \times_k H^\circ\). 5. If \(H\) is a connected group scheme over \(k\), any homomorphism \(\psi: H \to G\) factors uniquely through \(G^\circ \hookrightarrow G\).

Proof. - Since \(\pi_0\) commutes with finite products, the group morphisms \(m, i, e\) on \(G\) induce group structure morphisms on \(\pi_0(G)\) by functoriality. - For (3), since \(e \in G^\circ(k)\), \(G^\circ\) has a \(k\)-rational point. Proposition x.0.17(1) implies that \(G^\circ\) is geometrically connected. - For (5), since \(H\) is connected, \(\pi_0(H) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k\). Functoriality yields a commutative diagram: \[ \begin{matrix} H & \xrightarrow{\psi} & G \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ \pi_0(H) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k & \xrightarrow{e} & \pi_0(G) \end{matrix} \] Thus the composition \(H \to G \to \pi_0(G)\) is the trivial map, which implies that \(\psi\) factors uniquely through \(G^\circ = \ker(G \to \pi_0(G))\). \(\square\)

NoteDefinition x.0.19 (Exact Sequences of Group Schemes)

A sequence of homomorphisms of group schemes over \(k\): \[ 1 \longrightarrow N \xrightarrow{\quad i \quad} G \xrightarrow{\quad p \quad} Q \longrightarrow 1 \] is said to be exact if: 1. \(i: N \hookrightarrow G\) is a closed immersion identifying \(N\) with a normal subgroup scheme of \(G\); 2. \(p: G \to Q\) is an fppf quotient morphism; 3. \(i(N) = \ker(p)\) scheme-theoretically.
