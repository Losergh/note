26.4 Base Point Free Theorem

Birational Geometry (Chen Jiang, Fall 2026)

Published

November 12, 2026

Theorem 26.4.0 (Base Point Free Theorem)

Let \((X, B)\) be a klt pair and let \(f: X \to S\) be a projective morphism. Let \(D\) and \(E\) be \(\mathbb{Z}\)-Cartier divisors on \(X\) (\(E\) is an auxiliary divisor convenient for induction). Assume that:

  1. \(D\) is \(f\)-nef;
  2. There exists an integer \(m_1 > 0\) such that \(m_1 D + E - (K_X + B)\) is \(f\)-nef and \(f\)-big;
  3. \(E\) is effective, and there exists an integer \(m_2 > 0\) such that for all \(m \ge m_2\), the natural sheaf homomorphism \[ f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \xrightarrow{\sim} f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD + E) \] is an isomorphism.

Then there exists an integer \(m_3 > 0\) such that for all \(m \ge m_3\), \(mD\) is \(f\)-generated (base-point free over \(S\)), i.e., the natural evaluation map \[ f^* f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \] is surjective. That is, \(D\) is relatively semiample over \(S\).

Proof.

Since the statement is local on \(S\), we may assume without loss of generality that \(S\) is affine. Then it suffices to show that the evaluation map on global sections \[ H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \otimes_k \mathcal{O}_X \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \] is surjective for all \(m \gg 0\), i.e., \(\operatorname{Bs}|mD| = \emptyset\).

Step 1: Reduction to \(f\)-ample and \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Weil boundary

First, since \(m_1 D + E - (K_X + B)\) is \(f\)-big, by Kodaira’s lemma we can write: \[ m_1 D + E - (K_X + B) \sim_{\mathbb{R}} A + B', \] where \(A\) is an \(f\)-ample \(\mathbb{R}\)-Cartier divisor and \(B'\) is an effective \(\mathbb{R}\)-Cartier divisor. For any \(0 < \varepsilon \ll 1\), we have: \[ m_1 D + E - (K_X + B + \varepsilon B') = A + B' - \varepsilon B' = \underbrace{(1 - \varepsilon)(A + B')}_{\text{nef}} + \underbrace{\varepsilon A}_{\text{ample}}, \] which is \(f\)-ample. Since \((X, B)\) is klt, for \(\varepsilon \ll 1\), \((X, B + \varepsilon B')\) remains klt. We can then replace \(B\) by \(B + \varepsilon B'\). Thus, \(m_1 D + E - (K_X + B) =: A\) is an \(\mathbb{R}\)-Cartier ample divisor. Since ampleness is an open condition in \(N^1(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}}\), we may perturb the coefficients to assume without loss of generality that \(B\) is a \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Weil divisor.

Step 2: Elimination of base points via strong log resolution

Input the Nonvanishing Theorem (?@thm-nonvanishing): then there exists \(m \gg 0\) such that \(f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \ne 0\) (which is weaker; since \(S\) is affine, this means \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \ne 0\)).

Assume that \(|m_3' D|\) has a base point for some \(m_3' \gg 0\) (otherwise, the theorem holds). Take a general member \(M \in |m_3' D|\) and a strong log resolution: \[ \phi: (Y, C) \longrightarrow (X, B + E + M), \] and let \(h := f \circ \phi\). We may assume that the pullback linear system decomposes as: \[ \phi^* |m_3' D| = |M_1| + M_2, \] where \(|M_1|\) is free and \(M_2 \ne 0\) is the fixed part (an effective \(\mathbb{Z}\)-divisor). Notice that \(\operatorname{Bs}|m_3' D| \subseteq \operatorname{Supp} M\), so \(\operatorname{Bs}|\phi^* m_3' D| \subseteq \operatorname{Supp} \phi^* M\), which is of pure codimension \(1\) and contained in \(M_2\).

Step 3: Determining \(t\), tie-breaking, and Kawamata vanishing

Idea: Find a prime component \(Z \preceq M_2\) (which corresponds to \(E\)) such that the restriction map: \[ H^0\big(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(m\phi^* D + \lceil -B_Y \rceil)\big) \twoheadrightarrow H^0\big(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z((m\phi^* D|_Z - \lfloor B_Y \rfloor|_Z))\big) \] is surjective. It suffices to have \(H^1(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(m\phi^* D - \lfloor B_Y \rfloor - Z)) = 0\), for which we want \(m\phi^* D - B_Y - Z - K_Y\) to be \(h\)-ample (or \(h\)-nef and big).

First, there exists an effective \(\mathbb{Z}\)-divisor \(C'\) such that \(\operatorname{Supp} C' = \operatorname{Exc}(\phi)\) and \(-C'\) is \(\phi\)-ample. For \(0 < \varepsilon \ll 1\), we have that \(\phi^*(m_1 D + E) - K_Y - C - \varepsilon C'\) is ample over \(S\), where \(0 \le B_Y < 1\). We want \(m\phi^* D - Z = K_Y + B_Y + A_Y\) with \(A_Y\) ample, i.e., \(m\phi^* D - K_Y = Z + B_Y + A_Y\). We can expand the left-hand side as: \[ \begin{aligned} \text{LHS} &= (m - t m_3')\phi^* D + t M_1 + t M_2 - K_Y \\ &= \underbrace{(m - t m_3' - m_1)\phi^* D}_{\text{nef}} + \underbrace{t M_1}_{\text{free}} + \underbrace{t M_2 - \phi^* E + C + \varepsilon C'}_{Z + B_Y} + \underbrace{\phi^*(m_1 D + E) - K_Y - C - \varepsilon C'}_{\text{ample}}. \end{aligned} \] We require: 1. There exists a unique component of \(t M_2 - \phi^* E + C + \varepsilon C'\) with coefficient \(1\) (which will be our prime divisor \(Z\)); 2. \((m - t m_3' - m_1)\phi^* D + t M_1\) is free.

Then we set: \[ A_Y := (m - t m_3' - m_1)\phi^* D + t M_1 + \big(\phi^*(m_1 D + E) - K_Y - C - \varepsilon C'\big), \] and \(Z + B_Y = t M_2 - \phi^* E + C + \varepsilon C'\), so that \(m\phi^* D - B_Y - Z - K_Y = A_Y\) is ample.

Determining \(t\): First, we may assume \(C + \varepsilon C' < 1\). Now define: \[ t := \sup \big\{ s \in \mathbb{R}_{> 0} \;\big|\; \lfloor s M_2 - \phi^* E + C + \varepsilon C' \rfloor \le 0 \big\}. \] Then: \[ t M_2 - \phi^* E + C + \varepsilon C' = Z + F, \] where \(Z\) has coefficient \(1\) (coming from \(t M_2\)) and \(\lfloor F \rfloor \le 0\). Goal: \(Z\) is a prime divisor. Since ampleness is an open condition, we perturb \(C'\) so that the coefficient \(1\) is attained at a unique prime component. Then by Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing: \[ H^1(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(m\phi^* D - Z - \lfloor F \rfloor)) = 0, \] which gives the surjection: \[ H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(m\phi^* D - \lfloor F \rfloor)) \twoheadrightarrow H^0(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z((m\phi^* D|_Z - \lfloor F \rfloor|_Z))). \] Note that: \[ 0 \le F - \lfloor F \rfloor \le \lceil \phi^* E \rceil + \lceil -(t M_2 + C + \varepsilon C') \rceil - Z \le \phi^* E \quad \text{is effective}. \]

Diagram Chasing: Consider the commutative diagram: \[ \begin{CD} H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(m\phi^* D - \lfloor F \rfloor)) @>>> H^0(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z((m\phi^* D|_Z - \lfloor F \rfloor|_Z))) \\ @VVV @VVV \\ H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(m\phi^* D + \phi^* E)) @>>> H^0(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z(m\phi^* D|_Z)) \\ @V \sim V \text{by (3)} V @. \\ H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(m\phi^* D)) @>>> H^0(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z(m\phi^* D|_Z)) \end{CD} \] where the isomorphism in the lower-left vertical column is induced by \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \cong H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD + E))\).

Step 4: Induction on dimension

First, \(\operatorname{Supp} M_2 \cup \operatorname{Supp} E \cup \operatorname{Supp} C\) is SNC, which implies that \(Z\) is smooth. By the adjunction formula: \[ (K_Y + Z)|_Z = K_Z. \] Then \((m\phi^* D|_Z - K_Z - F|_Z) = (m\phi^* D - K_Y - Z - F)|_Z\) is ample. Set: \[ B_Z := (-\lfloor F \rfloor + F)|_Z, \] then \(0 \le B_Z < 1\), \(B_Z\) is SNC, and \((Z, B_Z)\) is a klt pair. Set \(E_Z := \lceil -F \rceil|_Z \ge 0\). Note that: \[ H^0(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z(m\phi^* D|_Z)) \longhookrightarrow H^0(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z(m\phi^* D|_Z + \lceil -F \rceil|_Z)). \] The triple \((\phi^* D|_Z, E_Z)\) on \((Z, B_Z)\) satisfies conditions (1), (2), and (3): 1. \(\phi^* D|_Z\) is nef; 2. There exists \(m_1 > 0\) such that \(m_1 \phi^* D|_Z + E_Z - (K_Z + B_Z)\) is nef and big; 3. \(E_Z\) is effective, and for \(m \ge m_2\), the sections stabilize.

By induction on dimension, \(\mathcal{O}_Z(\phi^* D|_Z)\) is semiample. For \(m \gg 0\), there exists a section \(s \in H^0(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z(m\phi^* D|_Z))\) such that \(V(s) \subsetneq Z\). By the surjection from Step 3, lift \(s\) to a section \(\tilde{s} \in H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(m\phi^* D))\); then \(Z \not\subseteq V(\tilde{s})\). Since \(Z \subseteq M_2\), this implies \(Z \not\subseteq \operatorname{Bs}|\phi^* m D|\). Thus, there exists \(m \gg 0\) such that: \[ \operatorname{Bs}|\phi^*(m m_3' D)| \subsetneq \operatorname{Bs}|\phi^* m_3' D| \quad (\dim \operatorname{Bs} \downarrow). \] By Noetherian induction on the base locus, there exists \(m \gg 0\) such that: \[ \operatorname{Bs}|\phi^* m D| = \emptyset \implies \operatorname{Bs}|mD| = \phi(\operatorname{Bs}|\phi^* m D|) = \emptyset. \] Therefore, \(D\) is semiample, which implies \(\kappa(D) = \nu(D)\), and for all \(m \gg 0\), \(\operatorname{Bs}|mD| = \emptyset\).

Theorem 26.4.1 (Nonvanishing Theorem)

Let \((X, B)\) be a klt pair, \(f: X \to S\) a projective morphism, and \(D, E\) \(\mathbb{Z}\)-Cartier divisors on \(X\). Assume:

  1. \(D\) is \(f\)-nef;
  2. There exists \(m_1 > 0\) such that \(m_1 D + E - (K_X + B)\) is \(f\)-nef and \(f\)-big;
  3. \(E\) is effective, and there exists \(m_2 > 0\) such that for all \(m \ge m_2\), \[ f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \xrightarrow{\sim} f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD + E). \]

Then there exists \(m_3 > 0\) such that for all \(m \ge m_3\), \[ f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \ne 0. \]

Proof.

We have: \[ f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \ne 0 \iff \big(f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD)\big)_{k(s)} \ne 0 \iff H^0(X_{k(s)}, \mathcal{O}_{X_{k(s)}}(mD)) \ne 0. \] Thus, we may assume without loss of generality that \(X/k\) where \(k\) is a field of characteristic \(0\) and \(S = \operatorname{Spec} k\). Then \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \cong H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD + E))\).

By Kawamata–Viehweg Vanishing, for all \(p > 0\) and \(m \ge m_1\), we have \(H^p(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD + E)) = 0\). Then: \[ h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD + E)) = \chi(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD + E)) =: P(m) \quad \text{is a polynomial in } m. \]

  • Case 1: If \(D \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} 0\). Then \(E - (K_X + B) \equiv m_1 D + E - (K_X + B)\) is nef and big. For all \(p \ge 1\), \(H^p(X, \mathcal{O}_X(E)) = 0\). But \(E\) is effective, so \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(E)) \ne 0\). Then \(\chi(X, \mathcal{O}_X(E)) \ne 0\), i.e., \(P(0) \ne 0\). Since \(P(m)\) is not identically zero, \(h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = P(m) \ne 0\) for all \(m \gg 0\).

  • Case 2: In general. We may assume \(m_1 D + E - (K_X + B) =: A\) is an ample \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier divisor. Then there exists an integer \(a \in \mathbb{Z}_{> 0}\) such that \(a A\) is a \(\mathbb{Z}\)-Cartier very ample divisor. Since \(D \not\equiv_{\mathrm{num}} 0\) and \(D\) is nef, there exists a curve \(C\) such that \((D \cdot C) > 0\). We may assume \(\mathcal{I}_C(aA)\) is generated by global sections. Let \(d := \dim X\). Take \(d - 1\) general sections \(s_1, \dots, s_{d-1}\) of \(\mathcal{I}_C(aA)\): \[ a^{d-1} A^{d-1} \sim \big(V(s_1) \cap \dots \cap V(s_{d-1})\big) = t C + C', \] where \(C \in V(s)\) and \(C'\) is effective and distinct from \(C\) in \(|aA|\). Then \((D \cdot A^{d-1}) > 0\), and: \[ (mD + aA)^d \ge m a^{d-1} (D \cdot A^{d-1}) \longrightarrow +\infty \quad (m \to +\infty). \] There exists \(k_1\) such that for all \(k \ge k_1\) and \(p \ge 1\), \(H^p(X, \mathcal{O}_X(k(mD + aA))) = 0\) by ampleness. Then \(h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(k(mD + aA))) = \chi(X, \mathcal{O}_X(k(mD + aA)))\) is a polynomial in \(k\) of degree \(d\), with leading coefficient \(\frac{(mD + aA)^d}{d!}\).

    Fix a smooth point \(P \in X\) not contained in \(\operatorname{Supp}(E + B)\), and let \(\mathfrak{m}_P\) be its maximal ideal. Then \(\operatorname{length}(\mathcal{O}_P / \mathfrak{m}_P^{a(d+1)k})\) is a polynomial in \(k\) of degree \(d\), given by: \[ \binom{d + a(d+1)k - 1}{d}, \quad \text{with leading coefficient } \frac{(a(d+1))^d}{d!}. \] Now choose \(m \gg 0\) such that \((mD + aA)^d > a^d(d+1)^d\). Then for all \(k \gg 0\): \[ H^0\big(X, \mathcal{O}_X(k(mD + aA)) \otimes \mathfrak{m}_P^{a(d+1)k}\big) \ne 0. \] That is, there exists \(M \in |k(mD + aA)|\) such that \(\operatorname{mult}_P M \ge a(d+1)k\).

    First blow up \(P\), let \(\pi: \mathrm{Bl}_P X \to X\), where \(K_{\mathrm{Bl}_P X} = \pi^* K_X + (d-1)C_0\), and \(C_0\) is the exceptional divisor. Take a strong log resolution \(\phi: Y \to X\) of \((\mathrm{Bl}_P X, B + E + M)\), and let \(C_0\) denote the strict transform of the exceptional divisor. Define: \[ t := \sup \big\{ s \in \mathbb{R}_{> 0} \;\big|\; \lfloor s \phi^* M - \phi^* E + C + \varepsilon C' \rfloor \le 0 \big\}. \] Perturb \(C'\) such that there exists a unique component \(Z\) of coefficient \(1\) in \(t \phi^* M - \phi^* E + C + \varepsilon C'\). Since \(\operatorname{Supp} C_0 \not\subseteq \operatorname{Supp} E \cup \operatorname{Supp} \phi^* B\), we have: \[ 1 > \operatorname{coeff}_{C_0}(t \phi^* M - \phi^* E + C + \varepsilon C') \ge t a(d+1)k - (d - 1). \] Then \(t \le \frac{d}{a(d+1)k}\). Write \(t \phi^* M - \phi^* E + C + \varepsilon C' = F + Z\) with \(\lfloor F \rfloor \le 0\). Then: \[ \begin{aligned} m' \phi^* D - F - Z - K_Y &= (m' - m_1 - t k m)\phi^* D - \phi^* M + \phi^* E - C - \varepsilon C' - K_Y + m_1 \phi^* D + t k m \phi^* D \\ &= (m' - m_1 - t k m)\phi^* D + (1 - t k a)\big(\phi^*(m_1 D + E) - K_Y - C\big) - \varepsilon C'. \end{aligned} \] Since: \[ 1 - t k a \ge 1 - k a \cdot \frac{d}{a(d+1)k} = 1 - \frac{d}{d+1} = \frac{1}{d+1} > 0, \] the divisor \(m' \phi^* D - F - Z - K_Y\) is ample. Kawamata vanishing yields: \[ H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(m'\phi^* D - \lfloor F \rfloor)) \twoheadrightarrow H^0(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z((m'\phi^* D|_Z - \lfloor F \rfloor|_Z))). \] By the same induction on dimension, \(H^0(Z, \mathcal{O}_Z((m'\phi^* D|_Z - \lfloor F \rfloor|_Z))) \ne 0\) for all \(m' \gg 0\), which completes the proof.

Corollary 26.4.3 (Semiample Fibration)

Suppose \(f: (X, B) \to S\) and \(D, E\) satisfy conditions (1), (2), and (3) in ?@thm-bpf. Then there exists a unique normal variety \(Z \to S\) projective over \(S\) and a surjective projective morphism: \[ h: X \longrightarrow Z \quad \text{with } h_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Z \text{ and } h^* H \sim_{\mathbb{Q}} D, \] where \(H\) is an \(S\)-ample divisor on \(Z\), and \(Z \cong \operatorname{Proj}_S \bigoplus_{m \ge 0} f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD)\).

Moreover: 1. Theorem 26.4.0 \(\iff\) Corollary 26.4.3. 2. In this case, for any relative curve \(C \subseteq X/S\), \(h(C) = \text{pt} \iff (C \cdot D) = 0\). 3. \(Z \cong X\) if and only if \(D\) is \(f\)-ample. 4. In particular, if \(D\) is not \(f\)-ample, there exists an irreducible effective curve \(C\) such that \((C \cdot D) = 0\) (which is not true for a general nef divisor).

Lemma 26.4.4 (Perturbation of Boundary for DLT Pairs)

Let \((X, B)\) be a DLT pair, let \(f: X \to S\) be projective, \(H\) an \(f\)-ample divisor, and \(\varepsilon > 0\). Assume \(S\) is quasi-projective. Then there exists an ample divisor \(A\) over \(S\) and an effective \(\mathbb{R}\)-Weil divisor \(B'\) on \(X\) such that: \[ B + \varepsilon(H + f^* A) \sim_{\mathbb{R}} B' \quad \text{and } (X, B') \text{ is a KLT pair}. \]

Proof.

Choose an ample divisor \(A\) on \(S\) such that \(H + f^* A\) is ample on \(X\). Take a strong log resolution \(g: (Y, C) \to (X, B)\), let \(h := f \circ g\), and we may assume \(C \le 1\). Take a small \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier effective divisor \(E\) such that \(-E\) is \(g\)-ample, \(\operatorname{Supp} E = \operatorname{Exc}(g)\), \(\lfloor C + E \rfloor = \lfloor C \rfloor\), and \(g^*(H + f^* A) - E\) is ample on \(Y\).

Assume \(B = \sum b_i B_i\) and \(g^{-1}_* B = \sum b_i B_i'\). For \(m \gg 0\), \(\mathcal{O}_Y(B_i' + m(g^*(H + f^* A) - E))\) is generated by global sections for all \(i\). Take general effective divisors \(D_i' \sim B_i' + m(g^*(H + f^* A) - E)\) and choose \(0 \le \delta \ll 1\) such that: \[ C' := C - \delta \sum b_i B_i' + \delta \sum b_i D_i' + m \delta \sum b_i E \sim_{\mathbb{R}} C + \delta m \sum b_i g^*(H + f^* A), \] such that \((\bigcup \operatorname{Supp} D_i') \cup g^*(\operatorname{Supp} B) \cup \operatorname{Exc}(g)\) is SNC. Then \(C'\) is still SNC and \(C' < 1\) for \(\delta \ll 1\). Define \(B' := g_* C' \sim_{\mathbb{R}} B + \delta m \sum b_i (H + f^* A)\). Then \(K_X + B'\) is \(\mathbb{R}\)-Cartier and \(g^*(K_X + B') = K_Y + C'\), which implies \((X, B')\) is a KLT pair.

Theorem 26.4.5 (BPF for DLT)

Let \((X, B)\) be a DLT pair, \(f: X \to S\) a projective morphism, and \(D, E\) Cartier divisors on \(X\). Assume:

  1. \(D\) is \(f\)-nef;
  2. There exists \(m_1 > 0\) such that \(m_1 D + E - (K_X + B)\) is \(f\)-ample;
  3. \(E\) is effective, and there exists \(m_2 > 0\) such that for all \(m \ge m_2\), \[ f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \xrightarrow{\sim} f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD + E). \]

Then \(D\) is \(f\)-semiample.

Proof.

We may assume \(S\) is affine. Take \(B' \sim_{\mathbb{R}} B + \varepsilon(H + f^* A)\) as in ?@lem-dlt-perturbation. If \(\varepsilon\) is small, \(m_1 D + E - (K_X + B')\) remains relatively ample. Then the result follows by ?@thm-bpf.

Remark 26.4.6 (Effective BPF Theorem)

Let \((X, B)/S\) be a KLT pair with \(\dim X = n\), and let \(D, E\) be \(\mathbb{Z}\)-Cartier divisors with \(E\) effective. If: 1. \(D\) is \(f\)-nef; 2. There exists \(m_1 > 0\) such that \(m_1 D + E - (K_X + B)\) is \(f\)-nef and \(f\)-big; 3. \(E\) is effective, and there exists \(m_2 > 0\) such that for all \(m \ge m_2\), \(f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \xrightarrow{\sim} f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD + E)\);

then for all integers: \[ m \ge \max\{m_1, m_2\} \cdot 2(n + 2)^{n+2}, \] the linear system \(|mD|\) is free.

Proposition 26.4.7 (Special Case: Abundance for Big Divisors)

Let \((X, B)\) be a KLT pair and \(f: X \to S\) a projective morphism. Assume \(K_X + B\) is \(f\)-nef, and \(B\) is an \(f\)-big \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier divisor. Then there exists a normal variety \(Z\) and a morphism \(h: X \to Z\) with \(h_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Z\), and an \(S\)-ample divisor \(H\) such that: \[ h^* H \sim_{\mathbb{Q}} K_X + B. \]

Proof.

We may assume \(S\) is affine. Since \(B\) is \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier and \(K_X + B\) is \(\mathbb{R}\)-Cartier, \(K_X\) is \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier. There exists \(m_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{> 0}\) such that \(m_1(K_X + B)\) is \(\mathbb{Z}\)-Cartier and \(f\)-nef; set \(D := m_1(K_X + B)\). Since \(B\) is big, there exists an ample \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier divisor \(A\) and an effective \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier divisor \(E\) such that \(B = A + E\). Then \((X, (1 - \varepsilon)B + \varepsilon E)\) is KLT for \(\varepsilon \ll 1\), and: \[ D - (K_X + B') = D - \big(K_X + (1 - \varepsilon)B + \varepsilon E\big) = (m_1 - 1)(K_X + B) + \varepsilon A \quad \text{is } f\text{-ample}. \] The proposition then follows by ?@cor-semiample-fibration.
