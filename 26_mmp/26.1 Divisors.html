Definition 26.1.0 (Linear Systems, Fixed Part, and Movable Part)

Let \(X\) be a normal variety and \(D \in Z^1(X)\).

  1. Complete Linear System: \[ |D| := \big\{ D' \in Z^1(X) \;\big|\; D' \sim D, \, D' \ge 0 \big\} \cong \big(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(D)) \setminus \{0\}\big)/k^*. \] The base locus of \(|D|\) is the closed algebraic set: \[ \operatorname{Bs}|D| := \bigcap_{D' \in |D|} \operatorname{Supp} D'. \] \(|D|\) is called free (or base-point free) if \(\operatorname{Bs}|D| = \emptyset \iff \mathcal{O}_X(D)\) is generated by global sections.

  2. Linear System: A finite-dimensional subspace \(V \subseteq H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(D))\) determines a linear system: \[ \Lambda := \big\{ \operatorname{div}(s) \;\big|\; s \in V \setminus \{0\} \big\} \cong \mathbb{P} V^* = (V \setminus \{0\})/k^*. \] Its base locus is \(\operatorname{Bs}\Lambda := \bigcap_{D' \in \Lambda} \operatorname{Supp} D'\). \(\Lambda\) is free if \(\operatorname{Bs}\Lambda = \emptyset\).

  3. Fixed Part and Movable Part: The fixed part of \(\Lambda\) is the effective Weil divisor: \[ F := \operatorname{Fix}(\Lambda) := \operatorname{gcd}_{D' \in \Lambda} D' = \min_{D' \in \Lambda} D'. \] Then \(V \subseteq H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(D - F)) \hookrightarrow H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(D))\). The linear system: \[ \Lambda - F := \big\{ D' - F \;\big|\; D' \in \Lambda \big\} =: \operatorname{Mov}(\Lambda) \] is called the movable part of \(\Lambda\). \(\operatorname{Supp} F\) is precisely the codimension \(1\) component of \(\operatorname{Bs}\Lambda\).

  4. Rational Map Associated to a Linear System: \(\Lambda\) induces a rational map: \[ \Phi_\Lambda: X \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P} V \cong \mathbb{P}^n, \quad x \longmapsto [s_0(x) : s_1(x) : \dots : s_n(x)], \] where \(\{s_0, \dots, s_n\}\) is a basis of \(V\). The domain of definition satisfies \(\operatorname{dom}(\Phi_\Lambda) \supseteq X \setminus \operatorname{Bs}\Lambda\). By construction, \(\Phi_\Lambda = \Phi_{\Lambda - F}\).

Proposition 26.1.1 (Base-Point Freeness Implies Cartier)

\[ \operatorname{Bs}|D| = \emptyset \iff D \text{ is Cartier and } \mathcal{O}_X(D) \text{ is generated by global sections}. \]

Proof.

\((\Leftarrow)\) Trivial.

\((\Rightarrow)\) For each \(x \in X\), since \(\operatorname{Bs}|D| = \emptyset\), there exists \(D' \in |D|\) such that \(x \notin \operatorname{Supp} D'\). Write \(D' = D + \operatorname{div} f\) for some \(f \in K(X)^*\). On the open neighborhood \(U := X \setminus \operatorname{Supp} D'\), we have: \[ D|_U = -(\operatorname{div} f)|_U = \operatorname{div}(f^{-1})|_U. \] Covering \(X\) by such open sets \(\{U_i\}\) with local equations \(f_i^{-1}\), on overlaps \(U_i \cap U_j\) we have \(\operatorname{div}(f_i^{-1}) = \operatorname{div}(f_j^{-1})\), so \(f_i f_j^{-1} \in \mathcal{O}_X(U_i \cap U_j)^*\). Thus \(D\) is locally principal (Cartier), and the sections generate \(\mathcal{O}_X(D)\).

Theorem 26.1.2 (Elimination of Base Locus)

Let \(X\) be a normal variety and \(\Lambda\) a linear system of Cartier divisors on \(X\). Then there exists a birational projective morphism \(f: Y \to X\) from a normal variety \(Y\) such that: \[ f^* \Lambda = \Lambda_1 + F, \] where \(F = \operatorname{Fix}(f^* \Lambda)\) is the fixed part and \(\Lambda_1\) is free.

(Warning: This theorem fails in general if the elements of \(\Lambda\) are only Weil divisors).

Proof.

Let \(V \subseteq H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(D))\) be the subspace defining \(\Lambda\). The obstruction to \(\Lambda - F\) being free is the locus of codimension \(\ge 2\) in \(\operatorname{Bs}\Lambda\). Define the ideal sheaf \(\mathcal{I} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X\) by: \[ \operatorname{Im}\big(V \otimes \mathcal{O}_X \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(D)\big) = \mathcal{I} \cdot \mathcal{O}_X(D). \] Let \(Y := \widetilde{\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathcal{I}} X}\) be the normalization of the blowup of \(X\) along \(\mathcal{I}\), with morphism \(f: Y \to X\). The inverse image ideal sheaf \(\mathcal{I}\mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_Y(-F)\) is an invertible ideal sheaf defining an effective Cartier divisor \(F\). Thus: \[ \operatorname{Im}\big(V \otimes \mathcal{O}_Y \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_Y(f^* D)\big) = \mathcal{I}\mathcal{O}_Y \cdot \mathcal{O}_Y(f^* D) = \mathcal{O}_Y(f^* D - F). \] Factoring out \(\mathcal{O}_Y(-F)\) gives a surjective morphism: \[ V \otimes \mathcal{O}_Y \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}_Y(f^* D - F), \] which implies that the linear system \(\Lambda_1\) corresponding to \(V \subseteq H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(f^* D - F))\) is free.

Definition 26.1.3 (Iitaka Dimension and Bigness)

  1. Let \(X\) be a normal proper variety and \(D \in Z^1_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\). The section ring of \(D\) is: \[ R(X, D) := \bigoplus_{m=0}^\infty H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(\lfloor mD \rfloor)). \] The Iitaka dimension of \(D\) is: \[ \kappa(X, D) := \begin{cases} \operatorname{tr.deg}_k R(X, D) - 1, & \text{if } R(X, D) \ne k, \\ -\infty, & \text{if } R(X, D) = k. \end{cases} \] \(D\) is called big if \(\kappa(X, D) = \dim X\).

  2. Relative Version: Let \(f: X \to S\) be a proper morphism and \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\) an \(\mathbb{R}\)-Cartier divisor.

    • \(D\) is relatively free if the natural evaluation map \(f^* f_* \mathcal{O}_X(D) \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(D)\) is surjective.
    • The relative section ring is \(R(X/S, D) := \bigoplus_{m=0}^\infty f_* \mathcal{O}_X(\lfloor mD \rfloor)\), which is a graded \(\mathcal{O}_S\)-algebra.
    • The relative Iitaka dimension is \(\kappa(X/S, D) := \kappa(X_{\bar{\eta}}, D|_{X_{\bar{\eta}}})\), where \(\bar{\eta}\) is the geometric generic point of \(S\).
    • \(D\) is relatively big (or \(f\)-big) if \(\kappa(X/S, D) = \dim X_{\bar{\eta}}\).
Remark 26.1.4 (Logical Hierarchy of Divisors)

\[\text{Removing fixed parts} \implies \text{Weil divisors} \implies \text{Base locus removal needs Cartier} \implies \text{Pullbacks need } \mathbb{Q}\text{-Cartier}.\]

Definition 26.1.5 (Canonical Divisor and Morphisms of Pairs)

Let \(X\) be a normal variety of dimension \(n\), and \(U = \operatorname{Reg}(X)\) its smooth locus (\(\operatorname{codim}_X(X \setminus U) \ge 2\)).

  1. The sheaf of Kähler differentials \(\Omega_{U/\mathbb{C}}^1\) is locally free of rank \(n\). The canonical sheaf on \(U\) is: \[ \omega_U := \det(\Omega_{U/\mathbb{C}}^1) = \bigwedge^n \Omega_{U/\mathbb{C}}^1 \cong \mathcal{O}_U(K_U). \]
  2. Since \(Z^1(X) \cong Z^1(U)\), the closure \(K_X := \overline{K_U}\) defines the canonical divisor of \(X\), well-defined in \(\operatorname{WCl}(X)\). The canonical sheaf on \(X\) is defined as the reflexive pushforward: \[ \omega_X := \mathcal{O}_X(K_X) = j_* \omega_U. \]
  3. If \(f: Y \to X\) is a birational projective morphism between normal varieties, there exists an open set \(U \subseteq X\) with \(\operatorname{codim}(X \setminus U) \ge 2\) such that \(f|_{f^{-1}(U)}: f^{-1}(U) \xrightarrow{\sim} U\). Thus \(K_Y|_{f^{-1}(U)} \cong K_X|_U\), which implies: \[ f_* K_Y = K_X. \]
  4. Morphisms of Pairs: A morphism \(f: (Y, C) \to (X, B)\) between pairs with \(K_X + B \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\) satisfies: \[ K_Y + C = f^*(K_X + B). \] Comparing on the isomorphism locus in codimension \(1\), we obtain: \[ C = f_*^{-1} B + E, \] where \(E\) is an \(f\)-exceptional \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor.
Definition 26.1.6 (Log Canonical Pairs and Adjunction)
  1. Let \(X\) be a normal variety and \(B \ge 0\) an effective \(\mathbb{R}\)-Weil divisor. The divisor \(K_X + B\) is called a log canonical divisor if \(K_X + B\) is \(\mathbb{R}\)-Cartier.
  2. Adjunction Formula: If \(X\) and \(S \subseteq X\) are smooth prime divisors, then: \[ (K_X + S)|_S = K_S. \] More generally, if \(X\) and \(S\) are normal and \(\operatorname{codim}_S(\operatorname{Sing}(X) \cap S) \ge 2\), adjunction still holds: \[ (K_X + S)|_S = K_S. \]
  3. The log canonical ring is \(R(X, K_X + B)\), and the log Kodaira dimension is \(\kappa(X, K_X + B)\).
Remark 26.1.7 (Logarithmic Differentials)

If \(X\) is smooth and \(B = \sum_{i=1}^r B_i\) is an SNC divisor with local equations \(x_1 \cdots x_r = 0\), the sheaf of logarithmic differentials \(\Omega_X^1(\log B)\) is locally generated by: \[ \frac{dx_1}{x_1}, \, \dots, \, \frac{dx_r}{x_r}, \, dx_{r+1}, \, \dots, \, dx_n. \] Its determinant line bundle satisfies: \[ \det \Omega_X^1(\log B) \cong \omega_X(B) = \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + B). \]

Example 26.1.8 (Ramification Formula and Pairs)

Let \(f: Y \to X\) be a finite surjective morphism between smooth varieties with branch locus \(D \subseteq X\) and ramification divisor \(E = (f^* D)_{\mathrm{red}}\). Assume \(E\) is smooth and \(f\) is a cyclic covering with ramification index \(m\). The Hurwitz ramification formula reads: \[ K_Y = f^* K_X + (m - 1)E \iff K_Y + E = f^*(K_X + D). \] Thus, \(f: (Y, E) \to (X, D)\) is a morphism of pairs.

Definition 26.1.9 (Relative Numerical Equivalence and Cycles)

Let \(f: X \to S\) be a projective morphism.

  1. A subvariety \(Z \subseteq X\) is a relative subvariety if \(f(Z) = \text{pt}\). A relative curve is a \(1\)-dimensional relative subvariety.
  2. Two line bundles \(\mathcal{L}, \mathcal{L}' \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)\) are relatively numerically equivalent (\(\mathcal{L} \equiv_S \mathcal{L}'\)) if: \[ (\mathcal{L} \cdot C) = (\mathcal{L}' \cdot C) \quad \text{for every relative curve } C \subseteq X. \] The quotient by numerically trivial bundles defines the relative Néron–Severi group: \[ \operatorname{NS}(X/S) := \operatorname{Pic}(X) / \operatorname{Pic}^\tau(X/S), \qquad \rho(X/S) := \operatorname{rank} \operatorname{NS}(X/S) < \infty. \] We set \(N^1(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}} := \operatorname{NS}(X/S) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{R}\).
  3. Two relative \(1\)-cycles \(C, C'\) are numerically equivalent (\(C \equiv_S C'\)) if: \[ (D \cdot C) = (D \cdot C') \quad \text{for every } D \in \operatorname{Div}(X). \] The quotient space is denoted \(N_1(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}}\). The intersection pairing: \[ N^1(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}} \times N_1(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R} \] is a perfect pairing, identifying \(N^1(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}} \cong N_1(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}}^\vee\).
Proposition 26.1.10 (Projection Formula)

Let \(g: Y \to X\) be a proper morphism of varieties over \(S\). For any relative subvariety \(Z \subseteq Y\), define: \[ g_* Z := \begin{cases} [k(Z) : k(g(Z))] \, g(Z), & \text{if } \dim g(Z) = \dim Z, \\ 0, & \text{if } \dim g(Z) < \dim Z. \end{cases} \] Extending linearly gives \(g_*: Z_r(Y) \to Z_r(X)\). Then: \[ (g^* L_1 \cdots g^* L_t \cdot Z) = (L_1 \cdots L_t \cdot g_* Z). \]

Proposition 26.1.11 (Pullback of Numerical Classes)

Let \(f: X \to S\) and \(g: Y \to X\) be proper morphisms. 1. If \(L \equiv_S 0\), then \(g^* L \equiv_S 0\), inducing \(g^*: N^1(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}} \to N^1(Y/S)_{\mathbb{R}}\). 2. If \(g\) is surjective, then \(g^* L \equiv_S 0 \implies L \equiv_S 0\), so \(g^*\) is injective.

Definition 26.1.12 (Relative Positivity: Ample, Nef, and Effective Cones)

Let \(f: X \to S\) be a projective morphism.

  1. Relative Ampleness: A line bundle \(\mathcal{L}\) on \(X\) is \(f\)-ample (relatively ample) if there exists an open cover \(S = \bigcup U_i\), integers \(m, N > 0\), and immersions \(g_i: f^{-1}(U_i) \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}_{U_i}^N\) over \(U_i\) such that \(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}|_{f^{-1}(U_i)} \cong g_i^* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^N}(1)\).
  2. Relative Nefness: \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\) is \(f\)-nef if \((D \cdot C) \ge 0\) for every relative curve \(C \subseteq X\).
    • \(\operatorname{Nef}(X/S) \subseteq N^1(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}}\) is the closed convex cone of nef classes.
    • \(\operatorname{Amp}(X/S) := \operatorname{Nef}(X/S)^\circ\) is the open cone of ample classes, and \(\overline{\operatorname{Amp}(X/S)} = \operatorname{Nef}(X/S)\).
  3. Mori Cone of Curves: \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) \subseteq N_1(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}}\) is the closed cone generated by classes of effective relative curves. By Kleiman’s criterion, the nef cone and Mori cone are dual cones: \[ \operatorname{Nef}(X/S) = \big(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)\big)^\vee. \]
Remark 26.1.13 (Polarization)

When \(X/S\) is projective, \(\operatorname{Nef}(X/S)\) and \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)\) contain no linear subspaces. Indeed, fixing an \(f\)-ample divisor \(A\), for every nonzero \([C] \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)\) we have \((A \cdot C) > 0\). Hence \((A \cdot -C) < 0\), so \(-[C] \notin \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)\). This strict positivity is the origin of the term polarization.

Proposition 26.1.14 (Pullback of Cones under Morphisms)

Let \(f: X \to S\) and \(g: Y \to X\) be projective morphisms, and \(L \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)\). 1. If \(L\) is \(f\)-nef, then \(g^* L\) is \((f \circ g)\)-nef. 2. If \(g\) is surjective, then \(g^* \operatorname{Nef}(X/S) = \operatorname{Nef}(Y/S) \cap g^* N^1(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}}\). 3. If \(g\) is surjective and finite, then \(g^* \operatorname{Amp}(X/S) = \operatorname{Amp}(Y/S) \cap g^* N^1(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}}\). (If \(g\) is not finite, \(g^* \operatorname{Amp}(X/S) \not\subseteq \operatorname{Amp}(Y/S)\) because \(g\) contracts curves, so \(g^* L\) vanishes on them).

Theorem 26.1.15 (Kodaira’s Lemma)

Let \(f: X \to S\) be a surjective projective morphism with \(S\) quasi-projective, and let \(D \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\). The following statements are equivalent:

  • (A) \(D\) is \(f\)-big.
  • (B) There exists an integer \(m > 0\), an \(f\)-ample Cartier divisor \(A\), and an effective divisor \(E \ge 0\) such that: \[ mD \sim A + E. \]
  • (C) \([D] \in \operatorname{Big}(X/S) := \operatorname{Nef}(X/S)^\circ + \text{effective classes}\).
Proof.

The equivalence \((\mathrm{B}) \iff (\mathrm{C})\) is clear.

  • \((\mathrm{B}) \implies (\mathrm{A})\): Restricting to the generic fiber \(X_\eta\), we have \(mD|_{X_\eta} = A|_{X_\eta} + E|_{X_\eta}\). Since \(A|_{X_\eta}\) is ample and \(E|_{X_\eta} \ge 0\), sections of multiples of \(A\) inject into sections of multiples of \(mD\). Thus: \[ h^0(X_\eta, k m D|_{X_\eta}) \ge h^0(X_\eta, k A|_{X_\eta}) \sim c \cdot k^{\dim X_\eta}, \] which proves \(D\) is \(f\)-big.

  • \((\mathrm{A}) \implies (\mathrm{B})\): Fix an \(f\)-very ample divisor \(A\). For a general element \(Y \in |A|\), consider the exact sequence: \[ 0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD - Y) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_Y(mD|_Y) \longrightarrow 0. \] Pushing forward to \(S\) yields: \[ 0 \longrightarrow f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD - Y) \longrightarrow f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \longrightarrow f_* \mathcal{O}_Y(mD|_Y). \] At the generic point \(\eta \in S\), by flat base change: \[ \dim_{k(\eta)} (f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD))_\eta \sim c_1 m^{\dim X_\eta}, \qquad \dim_{k(\eta)} (f_* \mathcal{O}_Y(mD|_Y))_\eta \le c_2 m^{\dim X_\eta - 1}. \] For \(m \gg 0\), \(\dim (f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD))_\eta > \dim (f_* \mathcal{O}_Y(mD|_Y))_\eta\), so \(f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD - A) \ne 0\). Choose an ample divisor \(B \in \operatorname{Div}(S)\) such that \(f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD - A) \otimes \mathcal{O}_S(B)\) is generated by global sections. Then: \[ H^0\big(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - A + f^* B)\big) = H^0\big(S, f_* \mathcal{O}_X(mD - A) \otimes \mathcal{O}_S(B)\big) \ne 0. \] Thus, there exists an effective divisor \(E \ge 0\) linearly equivalent to \(mD - A + f^* B\). Since \(f^* B \equiv_S 0\), it follows that \(mD - E \equiv_S A\) is relatively ample.

Proposition 26.1.16 (Birational Invariance of Bigness)

Let \(f: Y \to X\) be a birational morphism between projective normal varieties, and let \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\). Then: \[ D \text{ is big} \iff f^* D \text{ is big}. \]

Proof.

Since \(f\) is birational, \(K(X) \cong K(Y)\). For any rational function \(h \in K(X)^*\): \[ \operatorname{div} h + mD \ge 0 \iff \operatorname{div} h + \lfloor mD \rfloor \ge 0 \iff \operatorname{div}(f^* h) + f^* \lfloor mD \rfloor \ge 0 \iff \operatorname{div}(f^* h) + \lfloor m f^* D \rfloor \ge 0. \] Consequently, for every \(m \ge 0\): \[ H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(\lfloor mD \rfloor)) \cong H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(\lfloor m f^* D \rfloor)). \] The section rings \(R(X, D)\) and \(R(Y, f^* D)\) are isomorphic, hence \(\kappa(X, D) = \kappa(Y, f^* D)\).
