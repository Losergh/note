NoteIdea: How to Use the BPF Theorem?

Assume \(K_X + B\) is not nef. Take \(H\) ample over \(S\), then \(K_X + B + t H\) is ample for all \(t \gg 0\). Define the threshold: \[ t_0 := \inf \big\{ t \in \mathbb{R} \;\big|\; K_X + B + t H \text{ is ample over } S \big\}. \] Then \(D := K_X + B + t_0 H\) is nef, and: \[ D - (K_X + B) = t_0 H \quad \text{is } f\text{-ample}. \] Main Issue: Is \(K_X + B + t_0 H\) a \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier divisor or not?

Theorem 26.5.0 (Rationality Theorem)
  1. Let \((X, B)/S\) be a KLT pair, \(B\) a \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier divisor, and \(A\) a \(\mathbb{Z}\)-Cartier \(f\)-nef and \(f\)-big divisor. Assume \(K_X + B\) is not relatively nef, and define: \[ r := \max \big\{ t \in \mathbb{R} \;\big|\; A + t(K_X + B) \text{ is } f\text{-nef} \big\}. \] Then \(r \in \mathbb{Q}_{> 0}\).
  2. Let \(a\) be the minimal positive integer such that \(a(K_X + B)\) is Cartier, and let \(b\) be the maximal dimension of the fibers of \(f\). If \(\frac{r}{a} = \frac{p}{q}\) with \(\gcd(p, q) = 1\) (writing \(\left. A + r(K_X + B) = A + \frac{r}{a} \cdot a(K_X + B) \right)\), then: \[ q \le a(b + 1). \]
Proof.

(Idea of Proof) Consider the divisor class \((x, y) \sim x A + y a(K_X + B)\) in the \((x, y)\)-plane. The nef boundary line is given by \(a y = r x\).

  1. There exist infinitely many points \((x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2\) in the strip between \(a y = r x\) and \(a y = r x + \delta\).
  2. There exist infinitely many \(\mathbb{Z}^2\)-points in this region satisfying the Nonvanishing Theorem.
  3. By the BPF argument, fix such a point \((x_0, y_0)\); then: \[ \operatorname{Bs}|(x_0, y_0)| \supsetneq \operatorname{Bs}|(m x_0, m y_0)| \quad \text{for some } m \gg 0. \] Then there exists \((x_0', y_0') \in \{ (x, y) \mid a y = r x \} \cap \mathbb{Z}^2\) such that: \[ (x_2, y_2) := (m x_0 + x_0', m y_0 + y_0') \] remains in the strip. By the BPF theorem, we may assume \((x_0', y_0')\) is free (enlarging \(m\)), so: \[ \operatorname{Bs}|(x_1, y_1)| \subsetneq \operatorname{Bs}|(x_0, y_0)|. \] Then for \(n \gg 0\), \((x_n, y_n)\) is a \(\mathbb{Z}\)-Cartier free divisor, which implies it is nef, yielding a contradiction.
Definition 26.5.1 (Face, Extremal Ray, and Contraction)

Let \(C \subseteq V\) be a closed convex cone in a finite-dimensional \(\mathbb{R}\)-vector space \(V\).

  1. For any \(u \in V^*\), define: \[ C_{u \ge 0} := \{ v \in C \mid \langle u, v \rangle \ge 0 \}, \qquad C_{u = 0} := \{ v \in C \mid \langle u, v \rangle = 0 \}. \]
  2. A subset \(F \subseteq C\) is a face if there exists \(u \in C^*\) such that \(C \subseteq V_{u \ge 0}\) (i.e., \(C = C_{u \ge 0}\)) and \(F = C_{u = 0}\). The element \(u\) is called a supporting function.
  3. \(R \subseteq C\) is an extremal ray if \(R\) is a \(1\)-dimensional face.
  4. Let \(F\) be a face of \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)\). A morphism \(h: X \to Y\) is a contraction with respect to \(F\) if and only if:
    • \(X, Y\) are normal and \(h_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y\) over \(S\);
    • For every relative curve \(C \subseteq X/S\), \([C] \in F \iff h(C) = \text{pt}\);
    • \(F\) is the smallest closed convex cone containing \(\{ [C] \mid h(C) = \text{pt} \}\).
    In this case, \(F = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/Y) \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)\).
Theorem 26.5.2 (Contraction Theorem)

Let \((X, B)\) be a KLT pair, \(f: X \to S\) a projective morphism, and \(F\) a face of \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)\). Assume that there exists a rational supporting function \(u \in N^1(X/S)_{\mathbb{Q}}\) of \(F\), and for all nonzero \(C \in F \setminus \{0\}\), \(C \cdot (K_X + B) < 0\). Then:

  1. There exists a contraction morphism \(h: X \to Y\) with respect to \(F\);
  2. \(F = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/Y)\) via the inclusion \(N_1(X/Y) \hookrightarrow N_1(X/S)\);
  3. \(-(K_X + B)\) is \(h\)-ample;
  4. For every \(\mathbb{Z}\)-Cartier divisor \(D\), if \(D \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} 0\) in \(N^1(X/Y)\) (i.e., \(D \sim_Y 0\)), then there exists \(E \in \operatorname{Div}(Y)\) such that: \[ D \sim_{\mathbb{Q}} h^* E \quad (\text{numerical triviality } \implies \mathbb{Q}\text{-linear equivalence}); \]
  5. \(\rho(X/S) = \rho(Y/S) + \rho(X/Y)\).
Proof.
  • (1) \(\implies\) (2): Holds by definition.
  • (1) \(\implies\) (3): Since \((K_X + B) \cdot [C] < 0\) for all \([C] \in F \setminus \{0\} = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/Y) \setminus \{0\}\), \(-(K_X + B)\) is \(h\)-ample.
  • (4) + (1) \(\implies\) (5): The sequence \(0 \to N^1(Y/S) \xrightarrow{h^*} N^1(X/S) \to N^1(X/Y) \to 0\) where \(E \mapsto h^* E\) is exact by (4).

It therefore suffices to prove (1) and (4):

Fix \(L \in N^1(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\) supporting \(F\). Taking \(m L\) for \(m \gg 0\), we may assume \(L \in N^1(X)_{\mathbb{Z}}\). Then \(F = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{L=0}\), and \(L \ge 0\) on \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) \implies L\) is nef. We want to apply the BPF theorem to \(L\). It suffices to show that \(m L - (K_X + B)\) is ample. Since \(K_X + B < 0\) on \(F \setminus \{0\}\), take the unit sphere \(S_1 \subseteq N_1(X/S)\), and set \(S' := S_1 \cap \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)\). Define: \[ P := \{ x \in S' \mid (K_X + B) \cdot x \ge 0 \}. \] Then \(P\) is closed and compact, and \(P \cap F = \emptyset\). Thus \(\delta := \min_P L > 0\). Take \(m \gg 0\) such that \(m \delta > \max_{S'} (K_X + B)\). For any \(x \in S'\): - If \(x \notin P\), \((m L - K_X - B) \cdot x \ge m L \cdot x \ge 0\); - If \(x \in P\), \((m L - K_X - B) \cdot x \ge m \delta - \max_{S'} (K_X + B) > 0\).

Hence \(m L - K_X - B > 0\) on \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) \setminus \{0\} \implies m L - K_X - B\) is ample. By the BPF Theorem (?@thm-bpf), there exists a contraction \(h: X \to Y\) over \(S\) and an ample divisor \(H\) on \(Y/S\) such that \(L = h^* H\). Consider the sequence: \[ 0 \longrightarrow N_1(X/Y) \longrightarrow N_1(X/S) \xrightarrow{h_*} N_1(Y/S) \longrightarrow 0. \] Then for any \(\alpha \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)\), \[ h_* \alpha = 0 \iff (h_* \alpha \cdot H) = 0 \iff (\alpha \cdot L) = 0 \iff \alpha \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{L=0} = F. \] Thus \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/Y) \subseteq F\).

Assume \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/Y) =: F' \subsetneq F\). By separating convex sets, there exists a Cartier divisor \(D\) such that: \[ D > 0 \quad \text{on } F' \setminus \{0\}, \quad \text{and } \exists \alpha \in F \text{ such that } (D \cdot \alpha) < 0. \] Then \(D\) is \(h\)-ample, which implies \(D + m L\) is \(f\)-ample for all \(m \gg 0\). But: \[ (D + m L) \cdot \alpha = D \cdot \alpha < 0, \] a contradiction! Thus \(F = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/Y)\).

Assume \(D \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} 0\) in \(N^1(X/Y)\). By the BPF theorem over \(Y\), there exists \(m > 0\) such that \(|mD|\) is free over \(Y\). This gives a contraction \(h': X \to Y'\) over \(Y\) and an ample divisor \(H'/Y'\) over \(Y\) such that \(h'^* H' \sim D\): \[ \begin{CD} X @>h'>> Y' \\ @V h VV @VV \phi V \\ Y @= Y \end{CD} \] For any curve \(C\), \(h(C) = \text{pt} \implies (D \cdot C) = 0 \implies h'(C) = \text{pt}\); conversely, \(h'(C) = \text{pt} \implies h(C) = \text{pt}\). Hence \(\phi: Y' \to Y\) is an isomorphism (\(\phi_* \mathcal{O}_{Y'} = \mathcal{O}_Y\) and \(\phi\) is a bijection, which implies isomorphism). Thus \(D \sim_{\mathbb{Q}} h^* H'\), i.e., \(D \sim_Y 0\).

Theorem 26.5.3 (Cone Theorem)
  1. Let \((X, B)\) be a KLT pair and \(f: X \to S\) a projective morphism. Fix a relative ample divisor \(A\) and \(\varepsilon > 0\). Then there exist finitely many extremal rays \(R_i\) of the closed cone of curves such that: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B + \varepsilon A \ge 0} + \sum_{i=1}^k R_i. \] Consequently, \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B \ge 0} + \sum_{\text{countable}} R_i. \]
  2. For each \(R_i \not\subseteq \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B + \varepsilon A \ge 0}\), \(K_X + B\) is negative on \(R_i \setminus \{0\}\), and there exists a contraction morphism associated to \(R_i\).
  3. Each \(R_i = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} [C_i]\) where \(C_i\) is an effective rational curve such that: \[ 0 < -(K_X + B) \cdot C_i \le 2 \dim X \quad (\text{Fact: } \le \dim X + 1). \]
Proof.

Perturb \(\varepsilon A\) so that \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B + \varepsilon A \ge 0} \supsetneq \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B + \varepsilon' A' \ge 0}\) (considering \(S_1 \cap \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)\)). Then we may assume \(A\) is \(\mathbb{Z}\)-Cartier, \(\varepsilon \in \mathbb{Q}_{> 0}\), and \(B\) is \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier (hence \(K_X + B\) is \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier). We proceed by induction on \(\rho(X/S)\). If \(\rho(X/S) = 1\), the theorem is obvious.

We may assume \(\rho(X/S) \ge 2\) and \(K_X + B\) is not nef over \(S\). Take an \(f\)-ample \(\mathbb{Z}\)-Cartier divisor \(H\) on \(X\). By the Rationality Theorem (?@thm-rationality), \[ r_H := \max \big\{ t \in \mathbb{R} \;\big|\; H + t(K_X + B) \text{ is } f\text{-nef} \big\} \in \mathbb{Q}_{> 0}. \] The divisor \(L_H := H + r_H(K_X + B)\) is \(f\)-nef and \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier. Then by ?@thm-contraction, we have a contraction \(h_H: X \to Y_H\) for \(L_H\) with respect to the face \(F_H = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/Y_H)\). Note that \(F_H = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) \iff L_H \equiv 0 \iff Y_H = S\).

Goal: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B \ge 0} + \sum_{H \text{ ample}, L_H \not\equiv 0} F_H \quad (\exists H \text{ such that } L_H \not\equiv 0 \text{ since } \rho \ge 2). \] If this Goal is true, by induction we can show: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B \ge 0} + \sum_{\dim F_H = 1} F_H. \] Indeed, on \(X/Y_H\): \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/Y_H) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/Y_H)_{K_X + B \ge 0} + \sum_{H'} F_{H'}, \] but: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/Y_H) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{L_H = 0} = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{H + r_H(K_X + B) = 0} \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B < 0}. \] Thus by induction: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/Y_H) = \sum_{\substack{H' \text{ } h_H\text{-ample} \\ \dim F_{H'} = 1}} F_{H'}. \] Such \(H'\) is \(h_H\)-ample; we now construct an \(S\)-ample divisor: Since \(L_H = H + r_H(K_X + B)\), there exists \(A_H\) ample on \(Y/S\) such that \(h_H^* A_H = L_H\). For any \(H'\) that is \(h_H\)-ample, let \(L_{H'} = H' + r'(K_X + B)\). Take \(k \gg 0\), then: \[ \widetilde{H} := H' + k L_H = H' + k h_H^* A_H \quad \text{is } S\text{-ample}. \] Then: \[ \widetilde{H} + t(K_X + B) = H' + k L_H + t(K_X + B) = H' + t(K_X + B) + k L_H \] is nef but not ample. Thus: \[ \widetilde{H} + r'(K_X + B) = L_{H'} + k L_H =: L_{\widetilde{H}}. \] Notice that: - \(L_H = 0\) on \(F_H\), and \(L_H > 0\) on \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) \setminus F_H\); - \(L_{H'} = 0\) on \(F_{H'} \subseteq F_H\), and \(L_{H'} > 0\) on \(F_H \setminus F_{H'}\); - \(L_{\widetilde{H}} = 0\) on \(F_{H'} \implies L_{\widetilde{H}}\) is not ample.

Thus \(h_H\) contracts more curves than \(h_{H'}\). Let \(A_k := A_{H'} + k g^* A_H\), then: \[ L_{\widetilde{H}} = L_{H'} + k L_H = h_{H'}^* A_{H'} + k h_H^* A_H = h_{H'}^* A_k. \] Taking \(k \gg 0\) such that \(A_k\) is \(S\)-ample, we have that \(L_{\widetilde{H}}\) is \(S\)-nef. Then \(F_{\widetilde{H}} = F_{H'}\), so \(F_H\) decomposes into smaller faces: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B \ge 0} + \sum F_H = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B \ge 0} + \sum_H \sum_{\substack{H' \\ \dim F = 1}} \text{rays}. \]

Let \(\mathcal{C} := \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B \ge 0} + \sum_{L_H \not\equiv 0} F_H\). Suppose \(\mathcal{C} \subsetneq \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)\). There exists \(M \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\) such that: \[ M \cdot v > 0 \quad \forall v \in \mathcal{C} \setminus \{0\}, \quad \text{but } \exists v_0 \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) \setminus \mathcal{C} \text{ such that } M \cdot v_0 < 0. \] Since \(M > 0\) on \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B \ge 0}\), \[ M \in \big(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B \ge 0}\big)^\vee = \operatorname{Nef}(X/S) + (K_X + B)\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}. \] Notice that \([M] \notin (K_X + B)\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), because if \([M] = t(K_X + B)\), taking \(H\) and \(L_H = H + s(K_X + B)\) and \(v \in F_H\), we would have: \[ M \cdot v = t\big((K_X + B) \cdot v\big) = -\frac{t}{s}(H \cdot v) \le 0, \] contradicting \(M \cdot v > 0\). Hence: \[ M \in \operatorname{Int}\big(\operatorname{Nef}(X/S) + (K_X + B)\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\big) = \operatorname{Amp}(X/S) + (K_X + B)\mathbb{R}_{> 0}. \] Thus \(M = H + t(K_X + B)\) for some ample divisor \(H\) and \(t > 0\). Since \(M \cdot v_0 < 0\), let \(L_H := H + s(K_X + B)\) with maximal \(s \le t\). Since \(M \notin (K_X + B)\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), we have \(L_H \not\equiv 0\), so \(F_H \subseteq \mathcal{C}\). Then \(M\) is positive on \(F_H\). But \(L_H = 0\) on \(F_H \implies K_X + B\) is positive on \(F_H\), which implies \(L_H = H + s(K_X + B)\) is positive on \(F_H\), a contradiction!

Denote: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B + \varepsilon A \ge 0} + \sum_{i \in I} R_i, \] where \(R_i \not\subseteq \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B + \varepsilon A \ge 0}\). Let \(h_i: X \to Y_i\) be the associated contraction morphism, so \(R_i = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}[C_i]\) for a curve \(C_i\).

Let \(a\) be the minimal positive integer such that \(a(K_X + B)\) is Cartier, and let \(b_i\) be the maximal dimension of the fibers of \(h_i: (X, B) \to Y_i\). Since \(-(K_X + B)\) is \(h_i\)-ample, there exists a unique \(v_i \in R_i\) such that: \[ a(K_X + B) \cdot v_i = -1. \] Take \(f\)-ample divisors \(H_1, \dots, H_{\rho-1}\) such that \(\{H_1, \dots, H_{\rho-1}, a(K_X + B)\}\) forms a basis of \(N^1(X/S)\). Since \(\dim N_1(X/Y_i) \ge 1\), there exists a unique \(r_{ij}\) such that: \[ H_j + r_{ij}(K_X + B) \equiv_{Y_i} 0. \] Here \(r_{ij}\) is the maximal \(t\) such that \(H_j + t(K_X + B)\) is \(Y_i\)-nef. By the Rationality Theorem (?@thm-rationality), writing \(\frac{r_{ij}}{a} = \frac{p_{ij}}{q_{ij}}\), we have \(q_{ij} \le a(b_i + 1)\). Since \(q_{ij} \mid (a(b_i + 1))!\), we have: \[ \big(a(b_i + 1)\big)! \, (H_j \cdot v_i) \in \mathbb{Z}. \] Now take \(N \gg 0\) such that \(N A - H_j\) is \(f\)-ample for all \(j\). Then: \[ (H_j \cdot v_i) \le (N A \cdot v_i) < -\frac{N}{\varepsilon} (K_X + B) \cdot v_i = \frac{N}{a \varepsilon}, \] since \((K_X + B + \varepsilon A) \cdot v_i < 0\). Thus: \[ \big(a(b_i + 1)\big)! \, (H_j \cdot v_i) \in \mathbb{Z} \cap \left[0, \frac{\big(a(b_i + 1)\big)! N}{a \varepsilon}\right). \] Now consider the map: \[ \Phi: I \longhookrightarrow N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^\rho, \quad i \longmapsto v_i \longmapsto \big( (H_1 \cdot v_i), \dots, (H_{\rho-1} \cdot v_i), (K_X + B) \cdot v_i \big). \] Since \(b_i \le \dim X =: n\), the image satisfies: \[ \Phi(I) \subseteq \prod_{j=1}^{\rho-1} \left( \frac{1}{(a(n+1))!} \mathbb{Z} \cap \left[0, \frac{N}{a \varepsilon}\right) \right) \times \left\{ -\frac{1}{a} \right\}, \] which is a finite set. Since \(\Phi\) is injective on extremal rays, \(I\) is finite, which completes the proof of (1) and (2).

Corollary 26.5.4 (Finite Extremal Rays for Big Boundary)

Let \((X, B)\) be a KLT pair and \(f: X \to S\) a projective morphism. Assume \(S\) is quasi-projective and \(B\) is an \(f\)-big \(\mathbb{R}\)-Cartier divisor. Then there are only finitely many extremal rays in \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B < 0}\).

Proof.

Write \(B = A + E\) with \(A\) ample and \(E\) effective. Take \(\varepsilon \ll 1\) such that \((X, (1 - \varepsilon)B + \varepsilon E)\) is KLT. Then there are only finitely many extremal rays in: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + (1-\varepsilon)B + \varepsilon E + \varepsilon A < 0} = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B < 0}, \] by ?@thm-cone.

Corollary 26.5.5 (DLT Case)

Let \((X, B)\) be a DLT pair, \(f: X \to S\) a projective morphism, and assume \(S\) is quasi-projective. Fix a relative ample divisor \(A\) and \(\varepsilon > 0\). Then there are only finitely many extremal rays \(R_i\) such that: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B + \varepsilon A \ge 0} + \sum R_i. \] For each \(R_i \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B + \varepsilon A < 0}\), there exists a contraction morphism with respect to \(R_i\).

Proof.

By Lemma 26.4.4, there exists an ample divisor \(H/S\) and an effective \(\mathbb{R}\)-Weil divisor \(B'/X\) such that: \[ B' \sim_{\mathbb{R}} B + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}(f^* H + A) \quad \text{and } (X, B') \text{ is KLT}. \] Then \(B' \equiv_S B + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} A\), and the corollary reduces directly to the KLT Cone Theorem (?@thm-cone).

Theorem 26.5.6 (Dual Version of Cone Theorem)

Let \((X, B)\) be a KLT pair and \(f: X \to S\) a projective morphism. Fix a relative ample divisor \(A\) and \(\varepsilon > 0\), and assume \(K_X + B + \varepsilon A\) is not \(f\)-nef. There are finitely many extremal rays \(R_i\) such that \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B + \varepsilon A \ge 0} + \sum R_i\). For \(R_i \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B + \varepsilon A < 0}\), let \(h_i: X \to Y_i\) be the contractions. Let \(f_i \in N^1(X/S)^\vee \cong N_1(X/S)\) be defined by \(v_i\), where \(v_i \in R_i\) is a rational point. Then:

  1. \(f_i \ge 0\) on \(\operatorname{Nef}(X/S)\), and \(f_i > 0\) on \(\operatorname{Amp}(X/S)\).
  2. \(G_i := \{ u \in \operatorname{Nef}(X/S) \mid f_i(u) = 0 \}\) is a face of codimension \(1\) in \(\operatorname{Nef}(X/S)\), which coincides with \(h_i^* \operatorname{Nef}(Y_i/S)\).
  3. If \(F = R_{i_1} + \dots + R_{i_r}\) is a face, let \(h: X \to Y\) be its contraction. Then: \[ G := \bigcap_{j=1}^r G_{i_j} = \{ u \in \operatorname{Nef}(X/S) \mid \forall v \in F, \, \langle u, v \rangle = 0 \} = h^* \operatorname{Nef}(Y/S). \]
  4. For any \(f\)-ample divisor \(H\), let \(t := \max \{ s \in \mathbb{R} \mid s(K_X + B + \varepsilon A) + H \text{ is } f\text{-nef} \}\). Then there exists a face \(G = \bigcap G_{i_j}\) such that \([t(K_X + B + \varepsilon A) + H]\) is contained in: \[ \operatorname{int}(G) = h^* \operatorname{Amp}(Y/S). \]
Proof.
  • Proof of (2): If \(D\) is \(f\)-nef and \(D \cdot v_i = 0\), then: \[ D \in \ker\big(N^1(X/S) \to N^1(X/Y_i)\big) = \operatorname{Im}\big(N^1(Y_i/S) \to N^1(X/S)\big). \] Then there exists \(E\) on \(Y_i\) such that \(h_i^* E = D\). Since \(D\) is \(f\)-nef, \(E \in \operatorname{Nef}(Y_i/S)\), so \(G_i = h_i^* \operatorname{Nef}(Y_i/S)\).
  • Proof of (3): Take effective irreducible curves as a basis of \(N_1(X/S)\) and \(\mathbb{Z}\)-Cartier ample divisors as a basis of \(N^1(X/S)\). Then \(v_i\) is a rational point, which implies \(G_{i_j}\) is defined by rational linear equations, and hence \(G\) is defined by rational linear equations. Since \(F\) is a face, \(G \setminus \{0\} \ne \emptyset\). Take a rational point \(L \in \operatorname{rel.int}(G)\). Then \(L\) is a rational supporting function of \(F\). The same proof as in (2) shows that \(G = h^* \operatorname{Nef}(Y/S)\).
  • Proof of (4): The divisor \(t(K_X + B + \varepsilon A) + H\) is the supporting function of a face \(F\). Since \(F \cap \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/S)_{K_X + B + \varepsilon A \ge 0} = \emptyset\), we have \(F = R_{i_1} + \dots + R_{i_r}\), and then \(u \in \operatorname{int}(G) = h^* \operatorname{Amp}(Y/S)\).
NoteGeometric Intuition: The Satellite and the Planet

Consider the nef cone \(\operatorname{Nef}(X/S)\) as a celestial body (“planet”), and the canonical divisor \(K_X + B \notin \operatorname{Nef}(X/S)\) as an observing “satellite”:

  • If \(K_X + B \notin \operatorname{Nef}(X/S)\), an observer at \(K_X + B\) can observe the front side \(U\) of the boundary surface \(\partial \operatorname{Nef}(X/S)\); the back side \(\partial \operatorname{Nef}(X/S) \setminus U\) cannot be observed.
  • If we move a little bit closer (considering \(K_X + B + \varepsilon A\)), the visible boundary \(U_\varepsilon\) looks like a polyhedron with finitely many faces.
  • As \(\varepsilon \to 0\), there may be infinitely many faces converging towards the horizon defined by \((K_X + B) \cdot v = 0\).
Corollary 26.5.7 (BPF for ℝ-Divisors)

Let \((X, B)\) be a KLT pair and \(f: X \to S\) a projective morphism. Let \(D\) be an \(\mathbb{R}\)-Cartier divisor on \(X\). Assume:

  1. \(D\) is \(f\)-nef;
  2. \(D - (K_X + B)\) is \(f\)-nef and \(f\)-big.

Then there exists a projective morphism \(g: Z \to S\) with \(Z\) normal and a contraction \(h: X \to Z\) such that: \[ h_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Z, \quad f = g \circ h, \] and there exists a \(g\)-ample \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor \(H\) on \(Z\) such that: \[ h^* H \sim_{\mathbb{R}} D. \]

Proof.

We may assume \(S\) is affine (then glue to obtain \(Z\)). We may also assume \(D\) is not ample (otherwise \(Z = X\)). Write \(D - (K_X + B) = A + E\) with \(A\) ample and \(E\) effective. Then for \(\varepsilon \ll 1\), \[ L := D - (K_X + B) - \varepsilon E = (1 - \varepsilon)(A + E) + \varepsilon A. \] Then \(L\) is \(f\)-ample, and for \(\varepsilon \ll 1\) we may assume \((X, B + \varepsilon E)\) is KLT. Then \(K_X + B + \varepsilon E = D - L\) is not \(f\)-nef since \(D\) is not \(f\)-ample. Define: \[ t_0 := \min \big\{ t \in \mathbb{R} \;\big|\; (K_X + B + \varepsilon E + \delta L) + t L \text{ is } f\text{-nef} \big\}. \] We compute: \[ (K_X + B + \varepsilon E + \delta L) + t L = D - L + \delta L + t L = D + (t + \delta - 1) L. \] Since \(D\) is nef but not ample, \(t_0 = 1 - \delta\). The result then follows from ?@thm-cone-dual (4).

Corollary 26.5.8 (Canonical Model)

Let \((X, B)\) be a KLT pair and \(f: X \to S\) a projective morphism. Assume \(K_X + B\) is \(f\)-nef, and \(B\) is an \(f\)-big \(\mathbb{R}\)-Cartier divisor. Then there exists a projective morphism \(g: Z \to S\) from a normal variety \(Z\) and a surjective morphism \(h: X \to Z\) such that: \[ h_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Z, \] and there exists a \(g\)-ample divisor \(H\) on \(Z\) such that: \[ h^* H \sim_{\mathbb{R}} K_X + B. \] (\(Z\) is called the canonical model of \((X, B)\) over \(S\)).

Proof.

Write \(B = A + E\) with \(A\) ample and \(E\) effective. Then \(B - \varepsilon E = (1 - \varepsilon)B + \varepsilon A\) is nef and big, and \((X, B + \varepsilon E)\) is KLT for \(\varepsilon \ll 1\). Then: \[ (K_X + B) - \big(K_X + (1 - \varepsilon)B + \varepsilon E\big) = \varepsilon A \quad \text{is nef and big (in fact, ample)}, \] and \(K_X + B\) is nef. The assertion then follows directly by applying ?@cor-bpf-r-div.
