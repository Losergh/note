22.0 Preliminaries

Divisors and Intersection Theory

Published

September 20, 2026

Definition 1 (Cartier Divisor) Let \(X\) be an irreducible complex variety. Let \(\mathcal{M}_X := \mathcal{K}_X\) be the constant sheaf of rational functions on \(X\), and \(\mathcal{O}_X \subseteq \mathcal{M}_X\) the structure sheaf.

  1. A Cartier divisor on \(X\) is a global section of the quotient sheaf of units \(\mathcal{M}_X^* / \mathcal{O}_X^*\). The abelian group of Cartier divisors on \(X\) is denoted by: \[ \operatorname{Div}(X) := \Gamma(X, \mathcal{M}_X^* / \mathcal{O}_X^*). \]

  2. In terms of local data, a Cartier divisor \(D \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\) is represented by a collection of pairs \(\{(U_i, f_i)\}_{i \in I}\), where \(\{U_i\}\) is an open covering of \(X\), \(f_i \in \Gamma(U_i, \mathcal{M}_X^*)\), and on overlaps \(U_{ij} = U_i \cap U_j\), \[ \frac{f_i}{f_j} = g_{ij} \in \Gamma(U_{ij}, \mathcal{O}_X^*). \] Two such collections \(\{(U_i, f_i)\}\) and \(\{(V_j, g_j)\}\) determine the same Cartier divisor if they agree on a common refinement \(\{W_k\}\), that is, \(f_k / g_k \in \Gamma(W_k, \mathcal{O}_X^*)\).

  3. The support of \(D\) is the closed subset: \[ \operatorname{Supp}(D) := \big\{ x \in X \;\big|\; (f_i)_x \notin \mathcal{O}_{X, x}^* \text{ for } x \in U_i \big\}. \]

  4. The group operation is written additively: for \(D = \{(U_i, f_i)\}\) and \(D' = \{(U_i, f_i')\}\), \[ D + D' = \{(U_i, f_i f_i')\}. \]

  5. A Cartier divisor \(D\) is effective (denoted \(D \ge 0\)) if it can be represented by local equations \(f_i \in \Gamma(U_i, \mathcal{O}_X)\) for all \(i\). We write \(D \ge D'\) if and only if \(D - D' \ge 0\).

Remark 1 (Sheaf of Meromorphic Functions on Non-Reduced Schemes). If \(X\) is not necessarily reduced, \(\mathcal{M}_X\) is defined as the sheaf of total quotient rings: for an affine open \(U = \operatorname{Spec} A\), \[ \mathcal{M}_X(U) := A_{\mathrm{tot}}, \] where \(A_{\mathrm{tot}} = S^{-1}A\) is the localization of \(A\) at the multiplicatively closed set \(S \subseteq A\) of all non-zero-divisors. Then \(\mathcal{O}_X^* \subseteq \mathcal{M}_X^*\), and Cartier divisors are defined identically.

Definition 2 (Cycles and Weil Divisors)  

  1. Let \(X\) be a variety (or scheme) of pure dimension \(n\). A \(k\)-cycle on \(X\) is a formal \(\mathbb{Z}\)-linear combination of \(k\)-dimensional irreducible subvarieties of \(X\). The abelian group of \(k\)-cycles is denoted by \(Z_k(X)\).

  2. A Weil divisor on \(X\) is an \((n-1)\)-cycle, so the group of Weil divisors is \(\operatorname{WDiv}(X) := Z_{n-1}(X)\).

  3. If \(X\) is irreducible, for any \(f \in \mathcal{K}(X)^*\) and any prime divisor \(V \in Z_{n-1}(X)\), we define: \[ \operatorname{ord}_V(f) := \operatorname{ord}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{X, V}}(f), \] where \(\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{X, V}\) is the normalization of the local ring \(\mathcal{O}_{X, V}\) in \(\mathcal{K}(X)\). The rational function \(f\) defines a principal Weil divisor: \[ \operatorname{div}(f) = \sum_{V \subseteq X} \operatorname{ord}_V(f) [V]. \]

Proposition 1 (Cycle Map) There is a canonical cycle homomorphism: \[ \operatorname{cyc}: \operatorname{Div}(X) \longrightarrow \operatorname{WDiv}(X), \quad D \longmapsto [D] := \sum_{V \subseteq X} \operatorname{ord}_V(D) \cdot [V], \] where \(\operatorname{ord}_V(D) = \operatorname{ord}_V(f_i)\) whenever the generic point \(\eta_V \in U_i\).

  • The map \(\operatorname{cyc}\) is injective if \(X\) is normal.
  • The map \(\operatorname{cyc}\) is an isomorphism if \(X\) is non-singular (regular).

Definition 3 (Principal Divisors and Rational Sections)  

  1. For any global section \(f \in \Gamma(X, \mathcal{M}_X^*)\), \(f\) defines a principal Cartier divisor: \[ \operatorname{div}(f) \in \Gamma(X, \mathcal{M}_X^* / \mathcal{O}_X^*) = \operatorname{Div}(X). \] The subgroup of principal divisors is denoted by \(\operatorname{PDiv}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Div}(X)\).

  2. Two Cartier divisors \(D_1, D_2\) are linearly equivalent (denoted \(D_1 \sim D_2\) or \(D_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{lin}} D_2\)) if: \[ D_1 - D_2 \in \operatorname{PDiv}(X). \]

  3. Let \(L\) be a line bundle on \(X\). For any global section \(s \in \Gamma(X, L)\), choosing local trivializations \(\psi_i: L|_{U_i} \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{O}_{U_i}\) defines an effective Cartier divisor \(\{ (U_i, \psi_i(s)) \} \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\), denoted \(\operatorname{div}(s)_0\).

  4. More generally, any non-zero rational section \(s \in \Gamma(X, L \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{M}_X)\) determines a Cartier divisor \(\operatorname{div}(s) \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\).

Definition 4 (The Sheaf \(\mathcal{O}_X(D)\))  

  1. Every Cartier divisor \(D = \{(U_i, f_i)\} \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\) determines an invertible sheaf \(\mathcal{O}_X(D)\) (or \(\mathcal{L}(D)\)) whose local sections are: \[ \mathcal{O}_X(D)(U) := \big\{ s \in \Gamma(U, \mathcal{M}_X) \;\big|\; \operatorname{div}(s)|_U + D|_U \ge 0 \big\}. \] Locally on each open set \(U_i\), multiplication by \(f_i\) yields an isomorphism: \[ \mathcal{O}_X(D)(U_i) \xrightarrow{\quad \times f_i \quad} \mathcal{O}_X(U_i), \quad s \longmapsto f_i \cdot s, \] since \(\operatorname{div}(f_i s) = \operatorname{div}(f_i) + \operatorname{div}(s) \ge 0 \implies f_i s \in \mathcal{O}_X(U_i)\).

  2. From the short exact sequence of sheaves on \(X\): \[ 0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X^* \longrightarrow \mathcal{M}_X^* \longrightarrow \mathcal{M}_X^* / \mathcal{O}_X^* \longrightarrow 0, \] the connecting homomorphism gives: \[ \delta: \operatorname{Div}(X) = \Gamma(X, \mathcal{M}_X^* / \mathcal{O}_X^*) \longrightarrow H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^*) = \operatorname{Pic}(X). \] Furthermore, \(\mathcal{O}_X(D_1) \cong \mathcal{O}_X(D_2) \iff D_1 \sim D_2\).

  3. This construction induces an isomorphism: \[ \operatorname{CaCl}(X) := \operatorname{Div}(X) / \operatorname{PDiv}(X) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \operatorname{Pic}(X) \] whenever \(X\) is reduced or projective.

Definition 5 (Linear Series and Base Locus)  

  1. Let \(X\) be a variety and \(L\) a line bundle on \(X\). Let \(V \subseteq H^0(X, L)\) be a non-zero finite-dimensional subspace. The linear series associated with \(V\) is: \[ |V| := \mathbb{P}(V^*) = \mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sub}}(V), \] the projective space of 1-dimensional subspaces in \(V\). If \(H^0(X, L)\) is finite-dimensional, \(|H^0(X, L)|\) is denoted by \(|L|\) and called the complete linear series.

  2. The evaluation homomorphism is given by: \[ \mathrm{eval}_V: V \otimes_k \mathcal{O}_X \longrightarrow L, \quad s \otimes f \longmapsto f \cdot s|_U. \]

  3. The base ideal \(\mathfrak{b}(|V|) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X\) of \(|V|\) is the image of the dualized evaluation: \[ V \otimes_k L^\vee \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X, \quad s \otimes \phi \longmapsto \phi(s). \] The base scheme is the closed subscheme \(\operatorname{Bs}(|V|) \subseteq X\) defined by \(\mathfrak{b}(|V|)\). For a divisor \(D\), we write \(\mathfrak{b}(|D|)\) and \(\operatorname{Bs}(|D|)\) for the base ideal and locus of \(|\mathcal{O}_X(D)|\).

Definition 6 (Base-Point-Free Linear Series)  

  • A linear series \(|V|\) is free (or base-point-free) if its base ideal is trivial: \(\mathfrak{b}(|V|) = \mathcal{O}_X\) (equivalently, \(\operatorname{Bs}(|V|) = \emptyset\)).
  • A divisor \(D\) or a line bundle \(L\) is free if its complete linear series is base-point-free.
  • A line bundle \(L\) is free if and only if \(L\) is generated by its global sections.

Proposition 2 (Maps to Projective Space)  

  1. If \(\dim_k V \ge 1\) and \(B = \operatorname{Bs}(|V|)\), then \(|V|\) defines a morphism: \[ \phi_{|V|}: X \setminus B \longrightarrow \mathbb{P} V = \operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{Sym} V). \]

  2. If \(W \subseteq V\) is a linear subspace, we have a commutative naturality diagram: \[ \begin{CD} X \setminus \operatorname{Bs}(|V|) @>{\phi_{|V|}}>> \mathbb{P} V \setminus \mathbb{P}(V/W) \\ @VVV @VV{\pi}V \\ X \setminus \operatorname{Bs}(|W|) @>{\phi_{|W|}}>> \mathbb{P} W \end{CD} \] where \(\pi\) is the linear projection with center \(\mathbb{P}(V/W)\).

Definition 7 (Intersection Number (Snapper’s Formulation))  

  1. Let \(X\) be a proper variety over \(k\). Let \(Y \hookrightarrow X\) be an irreducible subvariety of dimension \(m = \dim Y\). For any line bundles \(L_1, \dots, L_m \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)\), the intersection number is defined by: \[ (L_1 \cdots L_m \cdot Y) := \sum_{I \subseteq \{1, \dots, m\}} (-1)^{|I|} \chi\left(X, \bigotimes_{i \in I} L_i^\vee \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} i_* \mathcal{O}_Y\right) = \sum_{I \subseteq \{1, \dots, m\}} (-1)^{|I|} \chi\left(Y, \bigotimes_{i \in I} L_i^\vee|_Y\right). \]

  2. If \(Y = X\) with \(\dim X = n\), we write \((L_1 \cdots L_n)\) instead of \((L_1 \cdots L_n \cdot X)\). Then: \[ (L_1 \cdots L_m \cdot Y) = (L_1|_Y \cdots L_m|_Y). \]

  3. For a general subscheme \(Y \subseteq X\) of pure dimension \(m\), associated with the cycle \([Y] = \sum_j \operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y, \eta_j}}(\mathcal{O}_{Y, \eta_j}) [Y_j]\), the intersection number is defined by linearity: \[ (L_1 \cdots L_m \cdot Y) := \sum_j \operatorname{length}(\mathcal{O}_{Y, \eta_j}) (L_1 \cdots L_m \cdot Y_j). \]

  4. If \(L_i \cong \mathcal{O}_X(D_i)\), we also write \((D_1 \cdots D_m \cdot Y)\) for \((L_1 \cdots L_m \cdot Y)\).

Convention: From now on, unless otherwise stated, every scheme is assumed to be separated, proper, and of finite type over an algebraically closed field \(k = \bar{k}\) with \(\operatorname{char}(k) = 0\). A variety is defined as an irreducible and reduced scheme.

Proposition 3 (Basic Properties of Intersection Numbers)  

  1. Let \(X\) be a proper curve and \(L \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)\). Then \((L \cdot X) = \deg L\). If \(X\) is a proper variety and \(C \subseteq X\) is a curve, \((L \cdot C) = \deg(L|_C)\).

  2. Let \(D \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\) be an effective Cartier divisor. If \(Y \subseteq X\) is an irreducible subvariety of dimension \(n = \dim Y\) such that \(D\) intersects \(Y\) properly (\(Y \not\subseteq \operatorname{Supp} D\)), then \(D|_Y\) is of pure dimension \(n - 1\) and: \[ (L_1 \cdots L_{n-1} \cdot \mathcal{O}_X(D) \cdot Y) = (L_1 \cdots L_{n-1} \cdot D|_Y). \]

  3. The intersection product is \(\mathbb{Z}\)-multilinear and symmetric: \[ \operatorname{Pic}(X)^m \times A_m(X) \longrightarrow \mathbb{Z}, \quad (L_1, \dots, L_m, [Y]) \longmapsto (L_1 \cdots L_m \cdot [Y]). \] In particular, if \(Y_1 \sim_{\mathrm{rat}} Y_2\) are rationally equivalent cycles, then: \[ (L_1 \cdots L_m \cdot Y_1) = (L_1 \cdots L_m \cdot Y_2). \]

Definition 8 (Numerical Equivalence)  

  1. Two line bundles \(L_1, L_2 \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)\) are numerically equivalent (denoted \(L_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} L_2\)) if for every irreducible curve \(C \subseteq X\), \[ (L_1 \cdot C) = (L_2 \cdot C). \] Two Cartier divisors \(D_1, D_2\) are numerically equivalent (denoted \(D_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} D_2\)) if \(\mathcal{O}_X(D_1) \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} \mathcal{O}_X(D_2)\).

  2. The quotient group: \[ N^1(X) := \operatorname{Pic}(X) / \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} \] is called the Néron-Severi group of \(X\) (modulo numerical equivalence).

Proposition 4 (The Néron-Severi Group and Picard Number)  

  1. \(N^1(X)\) is torsion-free.
  2. \(N^1(X)\) is a finitely generated free abelian group.
  3. The rank \(\rho(X) := \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}} N^1(X)\) is called the Picard number of \(X\).

Lemma 1 (Numerical Invariance of Intersection) If \(L_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} L_1', \dots, L_k \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} L_k'\), then for any subvariety \(V \subseteq X\) of pure dimension \(k\), \[ (L_1 \cdots L_k \cdot [V]) = (L_1' \cdots L_k' \cdot [V]). \]

Theorem 1 (Asymptotic Riemann-Roch I)  

  1. Let \(X\) be an irreducible proper variety of dimension \(n = \dim X\), and \(D \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\). As \(m \to \infty\), \[ \chi(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = \frac{(D^n)}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1}). \] More generally, for any coherent sheaf \(\mathcal{F} \in \operatorname{Coh}(X)\), \[ \chi(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{F}) \cdot \frac{(D^n)}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1}). \]

  2. If \(D\) is ample, then by Serre vanishing \(H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = 0\) for all \(i > 0\) and \(m \gg 0\), hence: \[ h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = \frac{(D^n)}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1}), \] and for any \(\mathcal{F} \in \operatorname{Coh}(X)\), \[ h^0(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{F}) \cdot \frac{(D^n)}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1}). \]

Proposition 5 (Amplitude Checked on Curves and Reduction)  

  1. Let \(f: Y \to X\) be a finite morphism of proper schemes. If \(L \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)\) is ample, then \(f^* L \in \operatorname{Pic}(Y)\) is ample.

  2. A line bundle \(L \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)\) is ample if and only if \(L|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}\) is ample, if and only if \(L|_{X_{i, \mathrm{red}}}\) is ample for every irreducible component \(X_i\) of \(X\).

  3. Let \(L\) be a globally generated line bundle on a proper scheme \(X\). Then \(L\) is ample if and only if \((L \cdot C) > 0\) for every irreducible curve \(C \subseteq X\).

Proof. Proof of (3). Consider the following three assertions: - (a) \(L\) is ample; - (b) The morphism \(\phi = \phi_{|L|}: X \to \mathbb{P} H^0(X, L)\) is finite; - (c) \((L \cdot C) > 0\) for all irreducible curves \(C \subseteq X\).

We show \((b) \implies (a) \implies (c) \implies (b)\):

  • \((b) \implies (a)\): Since \(\phi\) is finite and \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\) is ample, \(L \cong \phi^* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\) is ample by (1).

  • \((a) \implies (c)\): If \(L\) is ample, its restriction to any curve \(C \subseteq X\) is ample, hence \((L \cdot C) = \deg(L|_C) > 0\).

  • \((c) \implies (b)\): Suppose \(\phi\) is not finite. Since \(\phi\) is a proper morphism, it cannot be quasi-finite. Hence there exists a point \(p \in \phi(X)\) such that \(\dim \phi^{-1}(p) \ge 1\). Choose an irreducible curve \(Z \subseteq \phi^{-1}(p) \subseteq X\). Endow \(Z\) with the reduced structure.

    The restriction \(\phi|_Z: Z \to \mathbb{P}^N\) has set-theoretic image \(\{p\}\). Because \(Z\) is reduced, the scheme-theoretic image \(\operatorname{Im}(\phi|_Z)\) is reduced, meaning \(\operatorname{Im}(\phi|_Z) = \operatorname{Spec} k(p) \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^N\). Thus the pullback bundle: \[ L|_Z = (\phi|_Z)^* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^N}(1) \cong \mathcal{O}_Z \] is trivial. Consequently, \((L \cdot Z) = \deg(\mathcal{O}_Z) = 0\), contradicting assumption (c). Thus \(\phi\) is finite, completing the proof.

Proposition 6 (Asymptotic Riemann-Roch II) Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety of dimension \(n\), and let \(D \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\) be an ample Cartier divisor. Then: \[ h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = \frac{(D^n)}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1}). \] For any coherent sheaf \(\mathcal{F} \in \operatorname{Coh}(X)\), \[ h^0(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{F}) \cdot \frac{(D^n)}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1}). \]

Proposition 7 (Intersection with Complete Intersections in \(\mathbb{P}^N\)) Let \(X = \mathbb{P}^N\), and let \(Y \subseteq X\) be an irreducible subvariety of dimension \(n\). If \(H_1, \dots, H_n\) are effective hypersurfaces of degrees \(d_1, \dots, d_n\) in \(\mathbb{P}^N\), then: \[ (\mathcal{O}_X(H_1) \cdots \mathcal{O}_X(H_n) \cdot Y) = d_1 \cdots d_n \deg(Y). \]

Proposition 8 (Projection Formula for Intersections) Let \(\pi: X_1 \to X_2\) be a morphism of integral proper schemes of the same dimension \(n\). For any line bundles \(L_1, \dots, L_n \in \operatorname{Pic}(X_2)\), \[ (\pi^* L_1 \cdots \pi^* L_n) = \deg(X_1 / X_2) \cdot (L_1 \cdots L_n). \]

Theorem 2 (Cartier Class Group and Picard Group) If \(X\) is a reduced Noetherian scheme, then: \[ \operatorname{CaCl}(X) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \operatorname{Pic}(X). \]

Proof. Proof idea. We show that \(H^1(X, \mathcal{K}_X^*) = 0\). Let \(X = \bigcup_{j=1}^m \overline{\{\eta_j\}}\) be the decomposition into irreducible components with generic points \(\eta_j\). Then: \[ \mathcal{K}_X^* = \bigoplus_{j=1}^m (i_{\eta_j})_* K_j^*, \] which is a direct sum of pushforwards of constant sheaves from points, hence \(\mathcal{K}_X^*\) is flasque. Therefore \(H^1(X, \mathcal{K}_X^*) = 0\). Taking cohomology of the short exact sequence \(0 \to \mathcal{O}_X^* \to \mathcal{K}_X^* \to \mathcal{K}_X^* / \mathcal{O}_X^* \to 0\) yields: \[ H^0(X, \mathcal{K}_X^*) \xrightarrow{\;\operatorname{div}\;} H^0(X, \mathcal{K}_X^* / \mathcal{O}_X^*) \longrightarrow H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^*) \longrightarrow 0, \] which implies: \[ \operatorname{coker}(\operatorname{div}) = \operatorname{Div}(X) / \operatorname{PDiv}(X) = \operatorname{CaCl}(X) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \operatorname{Pic}(X). \]

Theorem 3 (Cartier Divisors vs. Weil Divisors) Let \(X\) be an irreducible variety that is regular in codimension 1. Then the cycle map induces injective homomorphisms: \[ \operatorname{Div}(X) \hookrightarrow \operatorname{WDiv}(X) \quad \text{and} \quad \operatorname{CaCl}(X) \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Cl}(X). \] In particular, if \(X\) is smooth (or regular), these maps are isomorphisms.
