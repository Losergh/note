22.3 Iitaka Fibration

Iitaka Dimension, Algebraic Fiber Spaces, and the Fundamental Theorem

Published

September 20, 2026

Definition 1 (Semigroup of a Line Bundle and Exponent) Let \(X\) be a variety and \(L\) a line bundle on \(X\).

  1. The set: \[ N(L) = N(X, L) := \big\{ m \ge 0 \;\big|\; H^0(X, L^{\otimes m}) \neq 0 \big\} \] forms a sub-semigroup of \((\mathbb{N}, +)\).
  2. If \(N(L) \neq \{0\}\), there exists a unique integer \(e = e(L) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}\) such that: \[ N(L) \subseteq e \mathbb{N} \quad \text{and} \quad e\mathbb{N} \setminus N(L) \text{ is finite}. \] The integer \(e(L)\) is called the exponent of \(L\).

Proposition 1 (Scheme-Theoretic Image of a Rational Map) Let \(f: X \dashrightarrow Y\) be a rational map between irreducible varieties. For any representative morphism \((U, f_U)\) of \(f\) defined on a dense open set \(U \subseteq X\), the closed subvariety: \[ \operatorname{Im}(f) := \overline{f_U(U)} \subseteq Y \] is independent of the chosen representative and is called the image of \(f\).

Proof. If \((V, f_V)\) is another representative, then \(f_U|_{U \cap V} = f_V|_{U \cap V}\). Setting \(W = U \cap V\), the pullback \(\mathcal{O}_Y \to (f_U)_* \mathcal{O}_U \to (f_W)_* \mathcal{O}_W\) has the same kernel because \(W\) is dense in \(U\) and \(X\) is reduced. The kernel defines the vanishing ideal of the scheme-theoretic closure, so \(\overline{f_U(U)} = \overline{f_W(W)} = \overline{f_V(V)}\).

Definition 2 (Iitaka Dimension) Let \(X\) be a normal variety and \(L\) a line bundle on \(X\).

  1. For each \(m \in N(X, L)\), the complete linear system \(|L^{\otimes m}|\) determines a rational map: \[ \phi_m := \phi_{|L^{\otimes m}|}: X \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P} H^0(X, L^{\otimes m}). \]
  2. The Iitaka dimension (or \(L\)-dimension) of \(L\) is defined by: \[ \kappa(L) = \kappa(X, L) := \begin{cases} \max_{m \in N(X, L)} \big\{ \dim \operatorname{Im}(\phi_m) \big\}, & N(X, L) \neq \{0\}, \\ -\infty, & N(X, L) = \{0\}. \end{cases} \]
  3. For an arbitrary non-normal variety \(X\), let \(\nu: \widetilde{X} \to X\) be the normalization. The Iitaka dimension is defined by: \[ \kappa(X, L) := \kappa(\widetilde{X}, \nu^* L). \]

Definition 3 (Algebraic Fiber Space) An algebraic fiber space (AFS) is a surjective projective morphism \(f: X \to Y\) between irreducible varieties such that: \[ f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y. \] By Zariski’s Connectedness Principle, every fiber of an algebraic fiber space is connected.

Convention: From now on, \(X\) is assumed to be an irreducible normal projective variety over \(k\).

Proposition 2 (Powers Achieving the Iitaka Dimension) Let \(X\) be a normal variety and \(\kappa = \kappa(X, L) \ge 0\). Then: \[ \dim \operatorname{Im}(\phi_m) = \kappa \quad \text{for all } m \gg 0 \text{ with } m \in N(X, L). \]

Proof. Replacing \(L\) by \(L^{\otimes e(L)}\), we may assume \(e(L) = 1\). Then there exists \(p_0\) such that \(H^0(X, L^{\otimes p}) \neq 0\) for all \(p \ge p_0\). Fix a non-zero section \(s \in H^0(X, L^{\otimes p})\).

Choose \(m\) such that \(\dim \operatorname{Im}(\phi_m) = \kappa\). Multiplication by \(s\) induces an injection \(H^0(X, L^{\otimes m}) \hookrightarrow H^0(X, L^{\otimes(m+p)})\). This linear inclusion determines a rational projection of projective spaces: \[ \begin{CD} X @>{\phi_{m+p}}>> Y_{m+p} \\ @| @VV{\pi}V \\ X @>{\phi_m}>> Y_m \end{CD} \] Because \(\pi\) is a dominant rational projection, we have: \[ \kappa = \dim Y_m \le \dim Y_{m+p} \le \kappa \implies \dim Y_{m+p} = \kappa. \] Thus \(\dim \operatorname{Im}(\phi_n) = \kappa\) for all \(n \ge m + p_0\).

Proposition 3 (Criterion for Algebraic Fiber Spaces) Let \(f: X \to Y\) be a surjective projective morphism between normal varieties, inducing an inclusion of function fields \(K(Y) \subseteq K(X)\). Then \(f\) is an algebraic fiber space if and only if \(K(Y)\) is algebraically closed in \(K(X)\).

In particular, every projective surjective birational morphism between normal varieties is an algebraic fiber space.

Proof. \((\implies)\): Let \(K(Y) \subseteq \mathbb{L} \subseteq K(X)\) be the algebraic closure of \(K(Y)\) in \(K(X)\). Normalizing \(Y\) in \(\mathbb{L}\) produces a factorization: \[ X \xrightarrow{\quad h \quad} Z \xrightarrow{\quad g \quad} Y, \] where \(g: Z \to Y\) is finite and \(h\) is proper. Since \(f\) is surjective and projective, \(h(X) = Z\), so \(\dim Z = \dim Y\). The condition \(f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y\) implies: \[ \mathcal{O}_Y \subseteq g_* \mathcal{O}_Z \subseteq g_* h_* \mathcal{O}_X = f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y \implies g_* \mathcal{O}_Z = \mathcal{O}_Y. \] Since \(Y\) is normal, \(g\) must be an isomorphism, so \(\mathbb{L} = K(Y)\).

\((\impliedby)\): Consider the Stein factorization of \(f\): \[ X \xrightarrow{\quad \phi \quad} Z \xrightarrow{\quad g \quad} Y, \] where \(\phi_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Z\) and \(g\) is finite. Since \(X\) is normal and \(\phi_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Z\), the scheme \(Z\) is normal. Furthermore, \(K(Z) \subseteq K(X)\) is a finite algebraic extension of \(K(Y)\). Since \(K(Y)\) is algebraically closed in \(K(X)\), we must have \(K(Z) = K(Y)\). Because \(Y\) is normal and \(g\) is birational and finite, Zariski’s Main Theorem implies that \(g: Z \xrightarrow{\sim} Y\) is an isomorphism. Thus \(f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y\).

Lemma 1 (Invariance of Sections and Picard Group) Let \(\phi: X \to Y\) be an algebraic fiber space.

  1. For every line bundle \(L\) on \(Y\), the natural map is an isomorphism: \[ H^0(Y, L) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} H^0(X, \phi^* L). \]
  2. The pullback homomorphism \(\phi^*: \operatorname{Pic}(Y) \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Pic}(X)\) is injective.

Proof.

  1. By the projection formula, \(\phi_* \phi^* L \cong L \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_Y} \phi_* \mathcal{O}_X \cong L\). Taking global sections yields \(H^0(X, \phi^* L) \cong H^0(Y, \phi_* \phi^* L) \cong H^0(Y, L)\).
  2. If \(\phi^* L \cong \mathcal{O}_X\), then by (1), \(H^0(Y, L) \cong H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X) \cong k\) and \(H^0(Y, L^\vee) \cong H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X) \cong k\). Choose non-zero sections \(s \in H^0(Y, L)\) and \(t \in H^0(Y, L^\vee)\). The product \(s \otimes t \in H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y) \cong k\) is non-zero, hence a non-zero constant \(c \in k^\times\). Thus \(s\) vanishes nowhere, which implies \(L \cong \mathcal{O}_Y\).

Definition 4 (Stable Base Locus) Let \(D\) be a Cartier divisor on \(X\).

  1. The stable base locus of \(D\) is the closed set: \[ \mathbf{B}(D) := \bigcap_{m \ge 1} \operatorname{Bs}(|mD|). \]
  2. Since the family of closed sets satisfies \(\operatorname{Bs}(|m_1 D|) \cap \operatorname{Bs}(|m_2 D|) \supseteq \operatorname{Bs}(|(m_1 + m_2)D|)\), by the descending chain condition on closed subsets of \(X\), there exists \(m_0 \ge 1\) such that: \[ \mathbf{B}(D) = \operatorname{Bs}(|k m_0 D|) \quad \text{for all } k \gg 0. \]

Definition 5 (Semiample Line Bundles)  

  1. Define the semigroup of free multiples: \[ M(X, L) := \big\{ m \in \mathbb{N} \;\big|\; L^{\otimes m} \text{ is base-point-free} \big\}. \] Let \(f = f(L)\) be the exponent of the semigroup \(M(X, L)\).
  2. A line bundle \(L\) on \(X\) is semiample if \(M(X, L) \neq \{0\}\), that is, some positive power \(L^{\otimes m}\) is generated by its global sections.

Theorem 1 (Semiample Fibration) Let \(X\) be a normal projective variety and \(L\) a semiample line bundle on \(X\). Then there exists an algebraic fiber space \(\phi: X \to Y\) and an ample line bundle \(A\) on \(Y\) such that:

  1. For all \(m \in M(X, L)\) sufficiently large, the morphism \(\phi_m: X \to \mathbb{P} H^0(X, L^{\otimes m})\) has image \(Y_m \cong Y\), and factors through \(\phi\).
  2. \(\phi^* A \cong L^{\otimes f(L)}\), and for \(k \gg 0\), the very ample embeddings \(\iota_{A^{\otimes k}}: Y \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P} H^0(Y, A^{\otimes k})\) make the following diagram commute: \[ \begin{CD} X @>{\phi}>> Y \\ @| @VV{\cong}V \\ X @>{\phi_{kf}}>> Y_{kf} \end{CD} \] In particular, the target variety \(Y\) is normal and projective.

Definition 6 (Big Line Bundles and Divisors) A line bundle \(L\) on an irreducible projective variety \(X\) is big if: \[ \kappa(X, L) = \dim X. \] A Cartier divisor \(D\) is big if \(\mathcal{O}_X(D)\) is big.

Theorem 2 (The Iitaka Fibration Theorem) Let \(X\) be a normal projective variety and \(L\) a line bundle on \(X\) with \(\kappa(X, L) > 0\). Then there exists an algebraic fiber space between normal varieties: \[ \phi_\infty: X_\infty \longrightarrow Y_\infty, \] unique up to birational equivalence, and a birational morphism \(u_\infty: X_\infty \to X\), such that for all \(k \in N(X, L)\) sufficiently large, there exists a birational map \(v_k: Y_\infty \dashrightarrow Y_k\) making the following diagram commute: \[ \begin{CD} X_\infty @>{u_\infty}>> X \\ @V{\phi_\infty}VV @VV{\phi_k}V \\ Y_\infty @>{v_k}>{\approx}> Y_k \end{CD} \] where \(Y_k := \operatorname{Im}(\phi_k) \subseteq \mathbb{P} H^0(X, L^{\otimes k})\).

Proof. Replacing \(L\) by \(L^{\otimes e(L)}\), we may assume without loss of generality that \(e(L) = 1\).

Step 1: The stable fibration for a sub-semigroup \(m\mathbb{N}\). Fix \(m \in N(X, L)\) such that \(\dim Y_m = \kappa(X, L)\). Let \(\mathfrak{b} = \mathfrak{b}(|L^{\otimes m}|) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X\) be the base ideal. Blow up \(\mathfrak{b}\) and normalize to obtain a projective birational morphism: \[ u_m: X_{(m)} := \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathfrak{b}}(X)^\nu \longrightarrow X. \] Let \(E_m\) be the exceptional divisor on the blow-up, and \(F_m\) its pullback to \(X_{(m)}\). Define: \[ M_m := u_m^* L^{\otimes m} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{(m)}}} \mathcal{O}_{X_{(m)}}(-F_m). \] Because \(u_m: X_{(m)} \to X\) is a birational morphism between normal varieties, Proposition 3 implies that \(u_m\) is an algebraic fiber space. By Lemma 1, \(H^0(X, L^{\otimes m}) \cong H^0(X_{(m)}, u_m^* L^{\otimes m})\). Since all sections vanish along \(F_m\) by construction of the blow-up, restriction to \(F_m\) is zero, yielding an isomorphism: \[ H^0(X_{(m)}, M_m) \cong H^0(X, L^{\otimes m}). \] Consequently, \(M_m\) is generated by its global sections and defines a morphism \(\phi_{M_m}: X_{(m)} \to \mathbb{P} H^0(X_{(m)}, M_m)\). By Theorem 1, the Stein factorization of \(\phi_{M_m^{\otimes k}}\) stabilizes for \(k \gg 0\) to an algebraic fiber space: \[ \phi_{(m)}: X_{(m)} \longrightarrow Y_{(m)}. \] For every \(k \ge 1\), we have \(H^0(X_{(m)}, M_m^{\otimes k}) \subseteq H^0(X_{(m)}, u_m^* L^{\otimes mk})\), inducing a dominant rational projection \(\mu_k: Y_{mk} \dashrightarrow Y_{(m)}\). Since \(X_{(m)} \to Y_{(m)}\) is an AFS, \(K(Y_{(m)})\) is algebraically closed in \(K(X_{(m)})\). The extension \(K(Y_{mk})/K(Y_{(m)})\) is algebraic and contained in \(K(X_{(m)})\), forcing: \[ K(Y_{mk}) = K(Y_{(m)}). \] Hence \(\mu_k: Y_{mk} \dashrightarrow Y_{(m)}\) is birational for all \(k \gg 0\).

Step 2: Construction of \(X_\infty\) and \(Y_\infty\). Choose two coprime integers \(p, q \in N(X, L)\) such that \(\dim Y_p = \dim Y_q = \kappa(X, L)\) (which is possible since \(H^0(X, L) \neq 0\)). Apply Step 1 to \(M_p\) and \(M_q\), obtaining: \[ \phi_{(p)}: X_{(p)} \longrightarrow Y_{(p)}, \qquad \phi_{(q)}: X_{(q)} \longrightarrow Y_{(q)}. \] Let \(\widetilde{U}\) be the closure of the diagonal embedding of a common dense open set \(U \subseteq X\) into \(X_{(p)} \times_X X_{(q)}\). Let \(X_\infty\) be the normalization of \(\widetilde{U}\). The projection maps \(v_p: X_\infty \to X_{(p)}\) and \(v_q: X_\infty \to X_{(q)}\) are projective, birational morphisms between normal varieties, hence algebraic fiber spaces.

Define the semiample line bundle: \[ M_{pq} := v_p^* M_p^{\otimes m_1} \otimes v_q^* M_q^{\otimes m_2}. \] The morphism induced by \(|M_{pq}|\) factors as: \[ X_\infty \longrightarrow Y_\infty \xrightarrow{\quad w \quad} Y_{(p)} \times Y_{(q)}, \] where \(w\) is a finite morphism onto its image. Thus \(K(Y_\infty) = K(Y_{(p)}) = K(Y_{(q)})\), and \(K(Y_\infty)\) is algebraically closed in \(K(X_\infty)\). Hence \(\phi_\infty: X_\infty \to Y_\infty\) is an algebraic fiber space with \(\dim Y_\infty = \kappa(X, L)\).

For any \(k \gg 0\), the Frobenius coin problem ensures that \(k = c p^{m_1} + d q^{m_2}\) for integers \(c, d \ge 1\). The sheaf \(u_p^* M_p^{\otimes(c-1)p^{m_1}} \otimes u_q^* M_q^{\otimes(d-1)q^{m_2}}\) has a non-zero section, inducing an inclusion: \[ H^0(X_\infty, M_{pq}) \hookrightarrow H^0(X_\infty, u_\infty^* L^{\otimes k}). \] This gives an induced dominant rational map \(v_k: Y_\infty \dashrightarrow Y_k\). Since \(\dim Y_k = \kappa(X, L) = \dim Y_\infty\) and \(K(Y_\infty)\) is algebraically closed in \(K(X_\infty)\), \(v_k\) is birational. Uniqueness follows because any two such targets are birational to \(Y_k\).

Corollary 1 (Iitaka Dimension of the Fibers) Let \(\phi_\infty: X_\infty \to Y_\infty\) be the Iitaka fibration associated with \(L\), and let \(L_\infty := u_\infty^* L\). For a very general closed point \(y \in Y_\infty\) (that is, \(y \in Y_\infty \setminus \bigcup_{i=1}^\infty F_i\) for countable proper closed subsets \(F_i \subsetneq Y_\infty\)), the fiber \(F := X_{\infty, y} = \phi_\infty^{-1}(y)\) satisfies: \[ \kappa(F, L_\infty|_F) = 0. \]

Proof. By generic smoothness, a very general fiber \(F = X_{\infty, y}\) is smooth and connected (by Zariski’s Main Theorem), hence integral.

For all \(k \gg 0\), the rational map \(\phi_k \circ u_\infty\) is defined along \(F\) and contracts \(F\) to a single point \(\phi_k(u_\infty(F)) = \{p_k\} \subseteq Y_k\). Thus the image of the restriction map: \[ \rho_k: H^0(X_\infty, L_\infty^{\otimes k}) \longrightarrow H^0(F, L_\infty^{\otimes k}|_F) \] has dimension \(\le 1\) for all \(k \ge 1\).

Claim: Fix a very ample line bundle \(B\) on \(Y_\infty\). 1. There exists \(m_0 > 0\) such that \(H^0(X_\infty, L_\infty^{\otimes m_0} \otimes \phi_\infty^* B^\vee) \neq 0\). 2. For any fixed \(k\) and all \(r \gg 0\), the restriction: \[ \beta_{k, r}: H^0(X_\infty, L_\infty^{\otimes k} \otimes \phi_\infty^* B^{\otimes r}) \longrightarrow H^0(F, L_\infty^{\otimes k} \otimes \phi_\infty^* B^{\otimes r}|_F) \cong H^0(F, L_\infty^{\otimes k}|_F) \] is surjective.

Proof of Claim: For (1), by the proof of Theorem 2, there exists an ample line bundle \(A\) on \(Y_\infty\) such that \(\phi_\infty^* A \subseteq L_\infty^{\otimes m_0}\). Because \(A\) is ample, \(A^{\otimes m_0} \otimes B^\vee\) has a non-zero section for \(m_0 \gg 0\), yielding a non-zero section \(s \in H^0(X_\infty, L_\infty^{\otimes m_0} \otimes \phi_\infty^* B^\vee)\). For a very general fiber \(F\), \(s|_F \neq 0\).

For (2), by generic flatness, \(X_\infty \to Y_\infty\) is flat over a neighborhood of the generic point. By Grauert’s Semicontinuity Theorem, \((\phi_\infty)_* L_\infty^{\otimes k}\) is locally free near \(y\), and the fiber cohomology satisfies: \[ H^0(F, L_\infty^{\otimes k}|_F) \cong (\phi_\infty)_* L_\infty^{\otimes k} \otimes k(y). \] Since \(B\) is very ample on \(Y_\infty\), the sheaf \((\phi_\infty)_* L_\infty^{\otimes k} \otimes B^{\otimes r}\) is generated by its global sections for \(r \gg 0\). Hence the evaluation map \(\beta_{k, r}\) is surjective.

Now, for any \(r \ge 1\), multiplication by \(s^{\otimes r}\) yields an injection: \[ H^0(X_\infty, L_\infty^{\otimes k} \otimes \phi_\infty^* B^{\otimes r}) \xrightarrow{\quad \times s^{\otimes r} \quad} H^0(X_\infty, L_\infty^{\otimes(k + r m_0)}). \] Restricting to the fiber \(F\), we obtain a commutative diagram: \[ \begin{CD} H^0(X_\infty, L_\infty^{\otimes k} \otimes \phi_\infty^* B^{\otimes r}) @>>> H^0(X_\infty, L_\infty^{\otimes(k + r m_0)}) \\ @VV{\beta_{k, r}}V @VV{\rho_{k + r m_0}}V \\ H^0(F, L_\infty^{\otimes k}|_F) @>{\times (s|_F)^{\otimes r}}>> H^0(F, L_\infty^{\otimes(k + r m_0)}|_F) \end{CD} \] Because \(F\) is integral and \(s|_F \neq 0\), the bottom map is injective. Since \(\beta_{k, r}\) is surjective, the image of the bottom map is contained in \(\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{k + r m_0})\). However, \(\operatorname{rank}(\rho_{k + r m_0}) \le 1\), which forces: \[ h^0(F, L_\infty^{\otimes k}|_F) \le 1 \quad \text{for all } k \ge 1. \] Since \(h^0(F, L_\infty^{\otimes k}|_F) = 1\) for all sufficiently large divisible \(k\), we conclude: \[ \kappa(F, L_\infty|_F) = 0. \]
