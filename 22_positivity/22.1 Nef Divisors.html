22.1 Nef-Divisors

Nakai-Moishezon Criterion, \(\mathbb{R}\)-Divisors, and Kleiman’s Theorem

Published

September 20, 2026

Theorem 1 (Nakai-Moishezon Criterion) Let \(X\) be a projective scheme and \(L\) a line bundle on \(X\). Then \(L\) is ample if and only if for every irreducible subvariety \(V \subseteq X\) with \(\dim V \ge 1\), \[ (L^{\dim V} \cdot V) > 0. \]

Proof. \((\implies)\): Suppose \(L\) is ample. Then \(L^{\otimes m}\) is very ample for some \(m \ge 1\). For any irreducible subvariety \(V \subseteq X\) of dimension \(k = \dim V \ge 1\), \[ ((L^{\otimes m})^k \cdot V) = m^k (L^k \cdot V). \] The very ample line bundle \(L^{\otimes m}\) defines an embedding \(X \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^N\) such that \(L^{\otimes m} \cong \mathcal{O}_X(1)\). Then: \[ ((L^{\otimes m})^k \cdot V) = (\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^N}(1)^k \cdot V) = \deg(V) > 0. \] Since \(m^k > 0\), we conclude that \((L^k \cdot V) > 0\).

\((\impliedby)\): By ?@prp-amplitude-checked-curves(2), we may assume without loss of generality that \(X\) is integral. We proceed by induction on \(n = \dim X\):

  • If \(\dim X = 1\), \(L\) is ample if and only if \(\deg L = (L \cdot X) > 0\), which holds by hypothesis.
  • Assume the criterion holds for all schemes of dimension \(\le n - 1\).
  • Let \(L = \mathcal{O}_X(D)\) for some Cartier divisor \(D \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\).

Step 1: \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \neq 0\) for \(m \gg 0\). By Asymptotic Riemann-Roch (?@thm-asymptotic-rr-1), \[ \chi(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = \frac{(D^n)}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1}). \] Since \((D^n) = (D^n \cdot X) > 0\), the leading coefficient is strictly positive.

Write \(L = M \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} N^\vee\) with \(M, N\) very ample line bundles on \(X\). Choosing effective divisors \(A, B\) such that \(M \cong \mathcal{O}_X(A)\) and \(N \cong \mathcal{O}_X(B)\), we have \(D \sim A - B\).

Tensoring the short exact sequence \(0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(-A) \to \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{O}_A \to 0\) with \(\mathcal{O}_X((m+1)D)\) yields: \[ 0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD - B) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X((m+1)D) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_A((m+1)D) \longrightarrow 0, \] since \((m+1)D - A = mD + (D - A) \sim mD - B\). Similarly, tensoring \(0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(-B) \to \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{O}_B \to 0\) with \(\mathcal{O}_X(mD)\) yields: \[ 0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD - B) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_B(mD) \longrightarrow 0. \] Because \(\dim A, \dim B \le n-1\), the induction hypothesis applies to the restricted bundles on \(A\) and \(B\), implying that for \(m \gg 0\), their higher cohomology stabilizes. Consequently: \[ H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - B)) \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X((m+1)D)) \] for \(m \gg 0\), meaning \(\dim H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))\) is bounded. Therefore: \[ \chi(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) - h^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) + O(1) \longrightarrow +\infty \quad \text{as } m \to \infty, \] which forces \(h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) > 0\) for all \(m \gg 0\).

Step 2: \(\mathcal{O}_X(mD)\) is globally generated for \(m \gg 0\). Replacing \(D\) by a linearly equivalent effective divisor \(D' \sim mD\) (noting that \(D\) is ample if and only if \(mD\) is ample), we may assume \(D \ge 0\).

To show \(\mathcal{O}_X(mD)\) is globally generated, it suffices to show that the evaluation map: \[ H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \otimes_k \mathcal{O}_{X, P} \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD)_P \] is surjective for every point \(P \in X\). - If \(P \notin \operatorname{Supp}(D)\), the canonical section \(s_D^{\otimes m}\) does not vanish at \(P\), so surjectivity holds trivially. - For \(P \in \operatorname{Supp}(D)\), consider the exact sequence: \[ 0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X((m-1)D) \xrightarrow{\;\times D\;} \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_D(mD) \longrightarrow 0. \] Since \(\dim D = n - 1\), the induction hypothesis implies \(\mathcal{O}_D(D)\) is ample on \(D\). Hence \(\mathcal{O}_D(mD)\) is globally generated and \(H^1(D, \mathcal{O}_D(mD)) = 0\) for \(m \gg 0\). The long exact sequence shows: \[ H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X((m-1)D)) \longrightarrow H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \] is surjective for all large \(m\). Thus the dimension sequence \(\dim_k H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))\) is non-increasing, hence constant for \(m \ge m_0\): \[ H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X((m-1)D)) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)). \] This yields a surjection on global sections: \[ H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \twoheadrightarrow H^0(D, \mathcal{O}_D(mD)). \] Considering the commutative diagram: \[ \begin{CD} H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) @>>> H^0(D, \mathcal{O}_D(mD)) \\ @VVV @VVV \\ \mathcal{O}_X(mD)_P @>>> \mathcal{O}_D(mD) \otimes k(P) \cong \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \otimes k(P) \end{CD} \] Since \(\mathcal{O}_D(mD)\) is globally generated, the right vertical arrow is surjective, which implies that \(\mathcal{O}_X(mD)\) is generated by global sections at every \(P \in \operatorname{Supp}(D)\).

Finally, because \(\mathcal{O}_X(mD)\) is globally generated and satisfies \((mD \cdot C) > 0\) for all irreducible curves \(C \subseteq X\), ?@prp-amplitude-checked-curves(3) implies that \(\mathcal{O}_X(mD)\) is ample. Hence \(L\) is ample.

Definition 1 (\(\mathbb{Q}\)-Divisors)  

  1. Let \(X\) be a scheme over \(k\). The group of \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Cartier divisors is: \[ \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X) := \operatorname{Div}(X) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}. \] Every \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\) can be written as \(D = \frac{1}{n} A\) for some \(n \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}\) and \(A \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\).

  2. A \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor \(D\) is integral if it lies in the image of the natural map \(\operatorname{Div}(X) \to \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\).

  3. A \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor \(D\) is effective (\(D \ge 0\)) if it can be written as \(D = \sum c_i A_i\) with rational coefficients \(c_i > 0\) and effective Cartier divisors \(A_i\) (equivalently, \(D = c A\) for some \(c \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}\) and effective \(A \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\)).

Definition 2 (Support of \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Divisors) Let \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\). A codimension 1 closed subscheme \(E \subseteq X\) is said to support \(D\) if there exists a representation \(D = \sum c_i A_i\) such that \(\bigcup_i \operatorname{Supp}(A_i) \subseteq E\).

Definition 3 (Intersection and Equivalence of \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Divisors) Let \(X\) be a proper scheme.

  1. Let \(V \subseteq X\) be a subscheme of pure dimension \(k\). The \(\mathbb{Q}\)-valued intersection product is defined by multi-linear extension: \[ \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)^k \longrightarrow \mathbb{Q}, \quad (D_1, \dots, D_k) \longmapsto \prod_{i=1}^k c_i \cdot (A_1 \cdots A_k \cdot [V]), \] where \(D_i = c_i A_i\) with \(c_i \in \mathbb{Q}\) and \(A_i \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\).

  2. Two \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisors \(D_1, D_2\) are numerically equivalent (denoted \(D_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} D_2\)) if \((D_1 \cdot C) = (D_2 \cdot C)\) for every irreducible curve \(C \subseteq X\). We set: \[ N^1(X)_{\mathbb{Q}} := N^1(X) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}. \]

  3. Two \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisors \(D_1, D_2\) are \(\mathbb{Q}\)-linearly equivalent (denoted \(D_1 \sim_{\mathbb{Q}} D_2\) or \(D_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{lin}} D_2\)) if there exists an integer \(r > 0\) such that \(r D_1, r D_2\) are integral and \(r D_1 \sim r D_2\). We set: \[ \operatorname{CaCl}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X) := \operatorname{CaCl}(X) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}. \]

Definition 4 (Ample \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Divisors) A \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\) is ample if it satisfies any of the following three equivalent conditions:

  1. \(D = \sum c_i A_i\) with \(c_i \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}\) and each \(A_i \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\) ample;
  2. There exists an integer \(r > 0\) such that \(r D\) is an integral ample Cartier divisor;
  3. \((D^{\dim V} \cdot V) > 0\) for all irreducible subvarieties \(V \subseteq X\) with \(\dim V \ge 1\).

Proposition 1 (Openness of Amplitude for \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Divisors) Let \(X\) be a projective variety, and let \(H\) be an ample \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor on \(X\). For any \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisors \(E_1, \dots, E_r\), the perturbed divisor: \[ H + \varepsilon_1 E_1 + \dots + \varepsilon_r E_r \] is ample for all rational numbers \(\varepsilon_i\) satisfying \(0 \le |\varepsilon_i| \ll 1\).

Proof. Clearing denominators, we may assume without loss of generality that \(H\) and each \(E_i\) are integral Cartier divisors. Because \(H\) is ample, there exists an integer \(m \gg 0\) such that \(m H \pm E_i\) is ample for each \(i = 1, \dots, r\). Rewriting the perturbation as a convex linear combination: \[ H + \sum_{i=1}^r \varepsilon_i E_i = \sum_{i=1}^r |\varepsilon_i| \cdot \frac{1}{m}\big(m H + \operatorname{sgn}(\varepsilon_i) E_i\big) + \left(1 - \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^r |\varepsilon_i|\right) H. \] For \(\sum_{i=1}^r |\varepsilon_i| < m\), this is a positive rational linear combination of ample divisors, hence ample.

Definition 5 (\(\mathbb{R}\)-Divisors)  

  1. The real vector space of \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisors is: \[ \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X) := \operatorname{Div}(X) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{R}. \] The intersection product extends multi-linearly to \((D_1 \cdots D_k \cdot V) \in \mathbb{R}\). Two \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisors \(D_1, D_2\) are numerically equivalent (\(D_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} D_2\)) if \((D_1 \cdot C) = (D_2 \cdot C)\) for every curve \(C\).

  2. The real Néron-Severi space is defined by: \[ N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} := \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X) / \equiv_{\mathrm{num}}. \]

  3. An \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor \(D\) is effective if \(D = \sum c_i A_i\) with \(c_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\) and \(A_i\) effective Cartier divisors.

  4. An \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor \(D\) is ample if \(D = \sum c_i A_i\) with \(c_i \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}\) and \(A_i\) ample integral Cartier divisors.

Proposition 2 (Flat Base Change for \(N^1(X)\)) There is a canonical identification: \[ N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} = N^1(X) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{R} = N^1(X)_{\mathbb{Q}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{R}. \]

Proof. Since \(\mathbb{R}\) is a flat \(\mathbb{Z}\)-module, it suffices to show that: \[ \big\{ D \in \operatorname{Div}(X) \mid D \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} 0 \big\} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{R} = \big\{ D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X) \mid D \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} 0 \big\}. \] The inclusion \(\subseteq\) is evident. Conversely, let \(D = \sum_{i=1}^k c_i A_i\) with \(c_i \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(A_i \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\) satisfy \((D \cdot C) = 0\) for all curves \(C \subseteq X\). The intersection pairing defines a linear map: \[ \phi: \mathbb{Z}^k \longrightarrow \mathbb{Z}^{A_1(X)}, \quad (a_1, \dots, a_k) \longmapsto \left( C \mapsto \sum_{i=1}^k a_i (A_i \cdot C) \right). \] Since \((c_1, \dots, c_k) \in \ker(\phi \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{R})\), we have \(\ker(\phi \otimes \mathbb{Q}) \neq 0\), so \(\ker(\phi) \neq 0\). Choose a non-zero vector \((a_1, \dots, a_k) \in \mathbb{Z}^k \cap \ker(\phi)\) with \(a_1 \neq 0\). The divisor \(A_1' := \sum a_i A_i\) satisfies \(A_1' \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} 0\). We then decompose: \[ D = \frac{c_1}{a_1} A_1' + \sum_{i=2}^k \left(c_i - \frac{c_1 a_i}{a_1}\right) A_i. \] The second term involves only \(k - 1\) divisors and is numerically trivial. Induction on \(k\) proves \(D \in \{ D \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} 0 \} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{R}\).

Definition 6 (1-Cycles Modulo Numerical Equivalence)  

  1. Let \(X\) be a proper scheme. Define the real vector space of 1-cycles by: \[ Z_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} := \bigoplus_{C \subseteq X \text{ curve}} \mathbb{R} \cdot C. \] Two 1-cycles \(\gamma_1, \gamma_2\) are numerically equivalent (\(\gamma_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} \gamma_2\)) if \((D \cdot \gamma_1) = (D \cdot \gamma_2)\) for all \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\). We define: \[ N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} := Z_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} / \equiv_{\mathrm{num}}. \] Then the intersection pairing induces a perfect pairing of finite-dimensional real vector spaces: \[ N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \times N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}, \quad (\delta, \gamma) \longmapsto (\delta \cdot \gamma). \]

  2. Both \(N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\) and \(N_1(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\) are finite-dimensional, and \(N_1(X)\) is a finitely generated free abelian group.

Proposition 3 (Openness of Amplitude for \(\mathbb{R}\)-Divisors)  

  1. If \(D_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} D_2\), then \(D_1\) is ample if and only if \(D_2\) is ample.
  2. If \(H \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\) is ample, then \(H + \varepsilon_1 E_1 + \dots + \varepsilon_r E_r\) is ample for all \(0 \le |\varepsilon_i| \ll 1\). In particular, the ample cone \(\operatorname{Amp}(X) \subseteq N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\) is open.

Definition 7 (Nef Divisors)  

  1. Let \(X\) be a proper scheme. A line bundle \(L \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)\) is numerically effective (nef) if for every irreducible curve \(C \subseteq X\), \[ (L \cdot C) \ge 0. \] An \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\) is nef if \((D \cdot C) \ge 0\) for all irreducible curves \(C \subseteq X\).

  2. Nefness depends only on the numerical class: if \(D_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} D_2\), then \(D_1\) is nef if and only if \(D_2\) is nef.

Proposition 4 (Functorial Properties of Nef Bundles)  

  1. Let \(f: X \to Y\) be a proper morphism. If \(L \in \operatorname{Pic}(Y)\) is nef, then \(f^* L\) is nef on \(X\). If \(f\) is additionally surjective, then \(L\) is nef if and only if \(f^* L\) is nef.

  2. If \(L\) is globally generated, then \(L\) is nef. In particular, the pullback of \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\) under any morphism \(\phi_{|L|}: X \to \mathbb{P}^N\) is nef.

  3. \(L\) is nef if and only if \(L|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}\) is nef, if and only if \(L|_{X_i}\) is nef for every irreducible component \(X_i \subseteq X\).

Theorem 2 (Kleiman’s Theorem) Let \(X\) be a proper scheme and \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\) a nef \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor. Then for every irreducible subvariety \(V \subseteq X\) of dimension \(k = \dim V\), \[ (D^k \cdot V) \ge 0. \]

Proof. By Proposition 4(3), we may assume \(X\) is irreducible and reduced.

Reduction to the projective case: By Chow’s Lemma, there exists a projective variety \(\widetilde{X}\) and a birational surjective morphism \(f: \widetilde{X} \to X\). For any curve \(C \subseteq \widetilde{X}\), by the projection formula: \[ (f^* D \cdot C) = (D \cdot f_* C) \ge 0, \] so \(f^* D\) is nef on \(\widetilde{X}\). If the theorem holds for projective varieties, then for any \(V \subseteq X\) of dimension \(k\), choosing \(V' \subseteq \widetilde{X}\) such that \(f_* V' = \deg(V'/V) V\), we have: \[ (D^k \cdot V) = \frac{1}{\deg(V'/V)} ((f^* D)^k \cdot V') \ge 0. \] Thus we may assume \(X\) is projective.

We induct on \(n = \dim X\): - If \(n = 1\), \((D \cdot X) \ge 0\) by the definition of nefness. - Assume the result holds in dimensions \(\le n - 1\). For any proper subvariety \(V \subsetneq X\) of dimension \(k \le n - 1\), \(D|_V\) is nef, so \((D^k \cdot V) \ge 0\) by induction. It remains to show that \((D^n) = (D^n \cdot X) \ge 0\).

Case 1: \(D\) is a \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor. Fix an ample divisor \(H\) on \(X\), and consider the polynomial: \[ P(t) := ((D + t H)^n) = \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} (D^{n-i} \cdot H^i) t^i = a_n t^n + a_{n-1} t^{n-1} + \dots + a_0. \] By induction, for \(1 \le i \le n\), the cycle \(H^i\) is represented by a positive combination of \((n-i)\)-dimensional subvarieties, so \(a_i = \binom{n}{i} (D^{n-i} \cdot H^i) \ge 0\), and \(a_n = (H^n) > 0\).

Suppose toward a contradiction that \(a_0 = (D^n) < 0\). By Descartes’ rule of signs, \(P(t)\) has a unique positive root \(t_0 > 0\).

Claim: For any rational \(t > t_0\), the divisor \(D + t H\) is ample. We verify the Nakai-Moishezon criterion (Theorem 1): - For \(V = X\), \(((D + t H)^n \cdot X) = P(t) > 0\) because \(t > t_0\) and \(a_n > 0\). - For any subvariety \(V \subsetneq X\) of dimension \(k < n\), the expansion: \[ ((D + t H)^k \cdot V) = (D^k \cdot V) + \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} \binom{k}{i} (D^{k-i} \cdot H^i \cdot V) t^i + (H^k \cdot V) t^k \] has non-negative coefficients for \(i < k\) by the induction hypothesis, and the leading coefficient satisfies \((H^k \cdot V) > 0\). Thus \(((D + t H)^k \cdot V) > 0\) for all \(t > 0\).

This proves \(D + t H\) is ample for all rational \(t > t_0\). Now decompose \(P(t)\) as: \[ P(t) = Q(t) + R(t), \quad \text{where } Q(t) = (D \cdot (D + t H)^{n-1}), \quad R(t) = t(H \cdot (D + t H)^{n-1}). \] For \(t > t_0\), \(D + t H\) is ample, so \((D + t H)^{n-1}\) is represented by an effective 1-cycle. Since \(D\) is nef, \(Q(t) \ge 0\) for all \(t > t_0\), and by continuity \(Q(t_0) \ge 0\). Furthermore, every coefficient of \(R(t)\) is non-negative and its leading term \((H^n) t^n\) is strictly positive, so \(R(t_0) > 0\). Hence: \[ P(t_0) = Q(t_0) + R(t_0) > 0, \] contradicting \(P(t_0) = 0\). Thus \((D^n) \ge 0\).

Case 2: \(D\) is an \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor. Choose ample divisors \(H_1, \dots, H_r\) whose classes span \(N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\). For \(\varepsilon = (\varepsilon_1, \dots, \varepsilon_r)\) with \(\varepsilon_i > 0\), the perturbation: \[ D(\varepsilon) := D + \sum_{i=1}^r \varepsilon_i H_i \] is nef, and the cone of such perturbations forms an open neighborhood. For any \(\delta > 0\), we may choose \(0 < \varepsilon_i < \delta\) such that \(D(\varepsilon) \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} E\) for some \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor \(E\). By Case 1, \((D(\varepsilon)^k \cdot V) = (E^k \cdot V) \ge 0\). Taking the limit as \(\varepsilon_i \to 0\) yields: \[ (D^k \cdot V) \ge 0. \] This completes the proof.

Corollary 1 (Kleiman’s Perturbation and Nefness Criterion) Let \(X\) be a projective scheme.

  1. If \(D\) is a nef \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor and \(H\) is an ample \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor, then \(D + \varepsilon H\) is ample for every \(\varepsilon > 0\).
  2. Conversely, if \(D, H\) are \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisors such that \(D + \varepsilon H\) is ample for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon > 0\), then \(D\) is nef.

Proof. Proof of (2): If \(D + \varepsilon H\) is ample for all \(0 < \varepsilon \ll 1\), then for every irreducible curve \(C \subseteq X\), \[ ((D + \varepsilon H) \cdot C) = (D \cdot C) + \varepsilon (H \cdot C) > 0. \] Taking the limit as \(\varepsilon \to 0^+\) gives \((D \cdot C) \ge 0\), so \(D\) is nef.

Proof of (1): Replacing \(\varepsilon H\) by \(H\), it suffices to prove that \(D + H\) is ample. Let \(V \subseteq X\) be an irreducible subvariety of dimension \(k \ge 1\). Expanding the intersection: \[ ((D + H)^k \cdot V) = \sum_{s=0}^k \binom{k}{s} (H^s \cdot D^{k-s} \cdot V). \] Because \(H\) is ample, \(H^s\) is represented by an effective cycle of dimension \(k - s\). By Kleiman’s Theorem (Theorem 2), \((H^s \cdot D^{k-s} \cdot V) \ge 0\) for all \(s < k\). For \(s = k\), \((H^k \cdot V) > 0\). Hence: \[ ((D + H)^k \cdot V) \ge (H^k \cdot V) > 0. \] If \(D + H\) is a \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor, Nakai-Moishezon (Theorem 1) implies directly that \(D + H\) is ample.

If \(D + H\) is not rational, choose ample divisors \(H_1, \dots, H_r\) spanning \(N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\). By openness of the ample cone, there exist small \(\varepsilon_i > 0\) such that \(H - \sum \varepsilon_i H_i\) remains ample, and: \[ E := D + H - \sum_{i=1}^r \varepsilon_i H_i \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} E_{\mathbb{Q}} \] is a rational class. Then \(E\) is ample by the rational case, and: \[ D + H = E + \sum_{i=1}^r \varepsilon_i H_i \] is a sum of ample \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisors, hence ample.
