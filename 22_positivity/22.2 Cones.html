22.2 Cones

Ample and Nef Cones, Kleiman’s Criterion, Openness, and Higher Cohomology

Published

November 12, 2026

Definition 22.2.0 (Ample and Nef Cones)

Let \(X\) be a complete variety over \(k\).

  1. The ample cone of \(X\), denoted by \(\operatorname{Amp}(X) \subseteq N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\), is the open convex cone generated by all ample \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor classes on \(X\).
  2. The nef cone of \(X\), denoted by \(\operatorname{Nef}(X) \subseteq N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\), is the closed convex cone consisting of all nef \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor classes on \(X\).
  3. By Kleiman’s criterion and openness of amplitude, the nef cone is the closure of the ample cone, and the ample cone is the interior of the nef cone: \[ \operatorname{Nef}(X) = \overline{\operatorname{Amp}(X)}, \qquad \operatorname{Amp}(X) = \operatorname{Nef}(X)^\circ. \]
Definition 22.2.1 (The Cone of Effective Curves NE (X))

Let \(X\) be a complete variety.

  1. The cone of effective curves is the convex cone: \[ \operatorname{NE}(X) := \sum_{C \subseteq X} \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} [C] \subseteq N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}, \] where \(C\) runs over all irreducible curves on \(X\).
  2. The closed cone of curves is denoted by \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)\). Under the perfect pairing \(N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \times N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \to \mathbb{R}\), it is dual to the nef cone: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) = \big\{ \gamma \in N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \;\big|\; (\delta \cdot \gamma) \ge 0, \; \forall \delta \in \operatorname{Nef}(X) \big\}. \]
  3. For an \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\), let \(\phi_D: N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \to \mathbb{R}\) be the linear functional \(\gamma \mapsto (\gamma \cdot D)\). We write: \[ D^\perp := \ker(\phi_D), \qquad D_{\ge 0} := \phi_D^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}), \qquad D_{> 0} := \phi_D^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{> 0}). \]
Lemma 22.2.2 (Slicing Criterion for Amplitude)

Let \(X\) be a projective variety (or scheme) and \(H\) an ample \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor on \(X\). An \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor \(D\) on \(X\) is ample if and only if there exists \(\varepsilon > 0\) such that: \[ \frac{(D \cdot C)}{(H \cdot C)} \ge \varepsilon \quad \text{for all irreducible curves } C \subseteq X. \]

Proof.

\((\impliedby)\): We write \(D = (D - \varepsilon H) + \varepsilon H\). By assumption, for every irreducible curve \(C \subseteq X\), \[ ((D - \varepsilon H) \cdot C) = (D \cdot C) - \varepsilon (H \cdot C) \ge 0, \] which means \(D - \varepsilon H\) is nef. Since \(\varepsilon H\) is ample, Kleiman’s perturbation theorem implies that \(D = (D - \varepsilon H) + \varepsilon H\) is ample.

\((\implies)\): If \(D\) is ample, by the openness of the ample cone, \(D - \varepsilon H\) remains ample for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon > 0\). In particular, for any curve \(C \subseteq X\), \[ ((D - \varepsilon H) \cdot C) > 0 \implies (D \cdot C) > \varepsilon (H \cdot C), \] whence \(\frac{(D \cdot C)}{(H \cdot C)} \ge \varepsilon\).

Theorem 22.2.3 (Amplitude via Cones (Kleiman’s Criterion))

Let \(X\) be a projective variety, and let \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\). Then \(D\) is ample if and only if: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) \setminus \{0\} \subseteq D_{> 0}, \] that is, \((\gamma \cdot D) > 0\) for every non-zero class \(\gamma \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) \setminus \{0\}\).

Proof.

\((\impliedby)\): Consider the linear functional \(\phi_D: N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \to \mathbb{R}\), \(\gamma \mapsto (\gamma \cdot D)\). By hypothesis, \(\phi_D(\gamma) > 0\) for all \(\gamma \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) \setminus \{0\}\).

Choose ample divisors \(H_1, \dots, H_r\) on \(X\) whose classes form a basis of \(N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\). Since the intersection pairing is non-degenerate, \[ \|\gamma\| := \sum_{i=1}^r |(H_i \cdot \gamma)| \] defines a norm on \(N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\). Consider the compact unit sphere \(S := \{ \gamma \in N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \mid \|\gamma\| = 1 \}\). The intersection \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) \cap S\) is compact and does not contain \(0\). Therefore, \(\phi_D\) attains a strictly positive minimum on this set: \[ \phi_D(\gamma) \ge \varepsilon > 0 \quad \text{for all } \gamma \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) \cap S. \] By linearity, \((D \cdot \gamma) \ge \varepsilon \|\gamma\|\) for all \(\gamma \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)\). For any curve \(C \subseteq X\), since each \(H_i\) is ample, \((H_i \cdot C) > 0\), so \(\|[C]\| = \sum_{i=1}^r (H_i \cdot C) = (H \cdot C)\), where \(H = \sum_{i=1}^r H_i\) is ample. Thus: \[ (D \cdot C) \ge \varepsilon (H \cdot C) \implies \frac{(D \cdot C)}{(H \cdot C)} \ge \varepsilon. \] By ?@lem-slicing-amplitude, \(D\) is ample.

\((\implies)\): Suppose \(D\) is ample. Fix \(H = \sum_{i=1}^r H_i\) as above. By openness of amplitude, \(D - \varepsilon H\) is ample for some \(\varepsilon > 0\). Hence \(D - \varepsilon H\) is nef, which implies: \[ (\gamma \cdot (D - \varepsilon H)) \ge 0 \quad \text{for all } \gamma \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X). \] For every \(\gamma \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) \cap S\), we have \((\gamma \cdot H) = \|\gamma\| = 1\), hence: \[ (\gamma \cdot D) \ge \varepsilon (\gamma \cdot H) = \varepsilon > 0. \] Thus \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) \setminus \{0\} \subseteq D_{> 0}\).

Remark 22.2.4 (Effective Curves are Not Enough)

The condition \((D \cdot C) > 0\) for every irreducible effective curve \(C \subseteq X\) is not sufficient to guarantee that \(D\) is ample: \[ D \text{ ample} \nLeftrightarrow \forall \text{ curve } C \subseteq X, \; (D \cdot C) > 0. \] One must test all classes in the closure \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)\) (e.g., Mumford’s famous construction of a non-ample divisor positive on all curves).

Theorem 22.2.5 (Openness of Amplitude in Families)

Let \(f: X \to T\) be a proper morphism of schemes, and let \(L\) be a line bundle on \(X\). Then the locus: \[ V := \big\{ t \in T \;\big|\; L_t \text{ is ample on } X_t \big\} \] is an open subset of \(T\).

Proof.

Being an open property is local on \(T\), so we may assume \(T = \operatorname{Spec} A\) is affine. It suffices to show that if \(0 \in T\) is a point such that \(L_0\) is ample on \(X_0 = f^{-1}(0)\), then there exists an open neighborhood \(U \subseteq T\) of \(0\) such that \(L_t\) is ample on \(X_t\) for all \(t \in U\).

Step 1: Vanishing of higher pushforwards. We claim that for any \(\mathcal{F} \in \operatorname{Coh}(X)\), there exists an integer \(m(\mathcal{F}, L) > 0\) such that for all \(i \ge 1\) and all \(m \ge m(\mathcal{F}, L)\), \[ R^i f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^{\otimes m}) = 0 \quad \text{in an open neighborhood of } 0. \] For \(i > \dim X_0\), upper semicontinuity of fiber dimensions implies \(\dim X_t \le \dim X_0 < i\) for \(t\) near \(0\), so \(H^i(X_t, \mathcal{F}_t \otimes L_t^{\otimes m}) = 0\). By the Theorem on Formal Functions, \((R^i f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^{\otimes m}))_0^\wedge = 0\), hence \(R^i f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^{\otimes m}) = 0\) in an open neighborhood of \(0\).

We proceed by decreasing induction on \(i\). Assume the claim holds for index \(i\); we establish it for \(i - 1\). First, assume \(0\) is a closed point with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak{m}_0 \subseteq A\). Choose generators \(u_1, \dots, u_p \in \mathfrak{m}_0\). This gives an exact sequence: \[ A^{\oplus p} \xrightarrow{\quad (u_j) \quad} A \longrightarrow A/\mathfrak{m}_0 \longrightarrow 0. \] Pulling back and tensoring with \(\mathcal{F}\), we obtain: \[ 0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{K} \longrightarrow \mathcal{F}^{\oplus p} \xrightarrow{\quad (f^* u_j) \quad} \mathcal{F} \longrightarrow \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{O}_{X_0} \longrightarrow 0. \] Let \(\mathcal{I} = \operatorname{Im}(f^* u_j)\). By the induction hypothesis, \(R^i f_*(\mathcal{K} \otimes L^{\otimes m}) = 0\) near \(0\) for \(m \gg 0\). Furthermore, because \(L_0\) is ample on \(X_0\), Serre vanishing gives \(H^{i-1}(X_0, \mathcal{F}_0 \otimes L_0^{\otimes m}) = 0\) for \(m \gg 0\), hence \(R^{i-1} f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{O}_{X_0} \otimes L^{\otimes m}) = 0\).

From the exact sequence \(0 \to \mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{O}_{X_0} \to 0\), the map \(R^{i-1} f_*(\mathcal{I} \otimes L^{\otimes m}) \to R^{i-1} f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^{\otimes m})\) is surjective at \(0\). Combining with the surjection \(\mathcal{F}^{\oplus p} \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{I}\), the map: \[ \big(R^{i-1} f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^{\otimes m})\big)^{\oplus p} \xrightarrow{\quad (u_j) \quad} R^{i-1} f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^{\otimes m}) \] has image equal to \(\mathfrak{m}_0 \cdot R^{i-1} f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^{\otimes m})\). At the stalk at \(0\): \[ \mathfrak{m}_0 \big(R^{i-1} f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^{\otimes m})\big)_0 = \big(R^{i-1} f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^{\otimes m})\big)_0. \] By Nakayama’s Lemma, \((R^{i-1} f_*(\mathcal{F} \otimes L^{\otimes m}))_0 = 0\). Since this sheaf is coherent, it vanishes on an open neighborhood of \(0\).

Step 2: Relative global generation and finiteness. Consider the ideal sheaf \(\mathcal{I}_{X_0/X}\) of \(X_0\) in \(X\). For \(m \gg 0\), Step 1 gives \(R^1 f_*(\mathcal{I}_{X_0/X} \otimes L^{\otimes m}) = 0\) near \(0\). Hence: \[ f_* L^{\otimes m} \longrightarrow f_*(L^{\otimes m} \otimes \mathcal{O}_{X_0}) = H^0(X_0, L_0^{\otimes m}) \] is surjective near \(0\). Since \(L_0\) is ample, \(L_0^{\otimes m}\) is globally generated on \(X_0\) for \(m \gg 0\). Thus the canonical evaluation: \[ \rho_m: f^* f_* L^{\otimes m} \longrightarrow L^{\otimes m} \] is surjective when restricted to \(X_0\). By Nakayama’s Lemma, \(\operatorname{coker}(\rho_m)\) vanishes on an open neighborhood of \(X_0\). Because \(f\) is proper, \(U_m := T \setminus f(\operatorname{Supp} \operatorname{coker} \rho_m)\) is an open neighborhood of \(0\). Shrinking \(T\), we may assume \(\rho_m\) is surjective everywhere on \(X\).

Thus \(L^{\otimes m}\) is relatively generated by global sections over \(T\), inducing a \(T\)-morphism: \[ \phi: X \longrightarrow \mathbb{P}_T^N. \] On the fiber \(X_0\), \(\phi|_{X_0}: X_0 \to \mathbb{P}_{k(0)}^N\) is an immersion, hence finite. The locus of points where the fibers of \(\phi\) have dimension 0: \[ F := \big\{ x \in X \;\big|\; \dim_x \phi^{-1}(\phi(x)) = 0 \big\} \] is open in \(X\) by upper semicontinuity of fiber dimensions, and contains \(X_0\). Since \(f\) is proper, \(U := T \setminus f(X \setminus F)\) is an open neighborhood of \(0\). The restricted morphism \(\phi|_{f^{-1}(U)}: f^{-1}(U) \to \mathbb{P}_U^N\) is proper and quasi-finite. By Zariski’s Main Theorem, it is finite. Consequently, \(L_t\) is ample on \(X_t\) for all \(t \in U\).

Non-closed points: If \(0\) is not closed, consider the local ring \(\mathcal{O}_{T, 0}\). By flat base change, the cohomology stalks match. Applying the above argument to the closed point of \(\operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{O}_{T, 0}\) produces the desired vanishing and openness, concluding the proof.

Corollary 22.2.6 (Fiber-Wise Amplitude)
  1. Let \(f: X \to T\) be a proper morphism and \(L\) a line bundle on \(X\). If \(L_t\) is ample on \(X_t\) for all \(t\) in a dense subset \(S \subseteq T\), then the ample locus contains an open dense subset. If \(L_t\) is ample for all \(t \in T\), then \(L\) is \(f\)-relatively ample.
  2. If \(\psi: X \to \mathbb{P}_T(\mathcal{E})\) is a finite morphism over \(T\), then \(\psi^* \mathcal{O}(1)\) is \(f\)-ample.
Corollary 22.2.7 (Relative Nakai-Moishezon Criterion)

Let \(f: X \to T\) be a proper morphism and \(D\) a \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor on \(X\). Then \(D\) is \(f\)-ample if and only if for every closed point \(t \in T\) and every irreducible subvariety \(V \subseteq X_t\) with \(\dim V \ge 1\), \[ (D^{\dim V} \cdot V) > 0. \]

Proposition 22.2.8 (Relative and Absolute Amplitude)

Let \(f: X \to T\) be a morphism of proper schemes, \(L\) a line bundle on \(X\), and \(A\) an ample line bundle on \(T\). Then \(L\) is \(f\)-ample if and only if \(L \otimes f^* A^{\otimes m}\) is ample on \(X\) for all \(m \gg 0\).

Proof.

\((\implies)\): If \(L\) is \(f\)-ample, then for \(k \gg 0\), \(f^* f_* L^{\otimes k} \twoheadrightarrow L^{\otimes k}\). Since \(A\) is ample on \(T\), \(f_* L^{\otimes k} \otimes A^{\otimes m}\) is globally generated for \(m \gg 0\). Choosing a surjection \(\mathcal{O}_T^{\oplus p} \twoheadrightarrow f_* L^{\otimes k} \otimes A^{\otimes m}\), we obtain a surjection \(\mathcal{O}_X^{\oplus p} \twoheadrightarrow L^{\otimes k} \otimes f^* A^{\otimes m}\), which defines a morphism: \[ X \longrightarrow \mathbb{P}^{p-1} \times T. \] Over each \(t \in T\), the restricted map \(X_t \to \mathbb{P}_{k(t)}^{p-1}\) is finite since \(L_t\) is ample. Hence \(X \to \mathbb{P}^{p-1} \times T\) is quasi-finite and proper, hence finite. Thus \(L^{\otimes k} \otimes f^* A^{\otimes m}\) is ample, which implies \(L \otimes f^* A^{\otimes m'}\) is ample for large \(m'\).

\((\impliedby)\): If \(L \otimes f^* A^{\otimes m}\) is ample on \(X\), its restriction to each fiber \(X_t\) is ample. But \((f^* A)|_{X_t} \cong \mathcal{O}_{X_t}\), so \((L \otimes f^* A^{\otimes m})|_{X_t} \cong L_t\). Hence \(L_t\) is ample for all \(t\), so \(L\) is \(f\)-ample by ?@cor-fiberwise-amplitude.

Definition 22.2.9 (Relative Nefness)

Let \(f: X \to T\) be a proper morphism. A line bundle \(L\) on \(X\) is \(f\)-nef if \(L_t\) is nef on \(X_t\) for every \(t \in T\). Equivalently, \((L \cdot C) \ge 0\) for every irreducible curve \(C \subseteq X\) mapped to a point by \(f\).

Lemma 22.2.10 (Higher Cohomology of Nef Divisors)

Let \(X\) be a projective scheme of dimension \(n\), and let \(D\) be a nef Cartier divisor on \(X\). Then for any coherent sheaf \(\mathcal{F} \in \operatorname{Coh}(X)\) and every \(i \ge 1\), \[ h^i(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = O(m^{n-i}) \quad \text{as } m \to \infty. \]

Proof.

By standard filtering techniques, we reduce immediately to the case where \(X\) is an integral variety. We proceed by induction on \(n = \dim X\):

  • If \(n = 1\), the only higher cohomology is \(i = 1\), and \(h^1(X, \mathcal{F}(mD)) = O(1) = O(m^0)\) by Serre duality and Riemann-Roch for curves.
  • Assume the statement holds in dimension \(\le n - 1\).

Let \(r = \operatorname{rank} \mathcal{F}\). For \(p \gg 0\), \(\mathcal{F}(p)\) is generically generated by \(r\) sections, yielding an exact sequence: \[ 0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(-p)^{\oplus r} \longrightarrow \mathcal{F} \longrightarrow \mathcal{C} \longrightarrow 0, \] where \(\dim \operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{C}) \le n - 1\). By the induction hypothesis, \(h^i(X, \mathcal{C} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = O(m^{n-1-i}) = O(m^{n-i})\). Thus it suffices to prove the result for \(\mathcal{O}_X(-p)\), and slicing by a hyperplane reduces it to proving the theorem for \(\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{O}_X\).

Write \(D \sim A - B\) with \(A, B\) effective very ample divisors. Tensoring the defining sequences of \(A\) and \(B\): \[ 0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD - B) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X((m+1)D) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_A((m+1)D) \longrightarrow 0, \] \[ 0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD - B) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_B(mD) \longrightarrow 0. \] For \(i \ge 2\), applying the induction hypothesis to the \((n-1)\)-dimensional subvarieties \(A\) and \(B\) gives: \[ |h^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) - h^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - B))| \le h^i(B, \mathcal{O}_B(mD)) = O(m^{n-1-i}) = O(m^{n-i}). \] Similarly, \(|h^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X((m+1)D)) - h^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - B))| = O(m^{n-i})\). Hence: \[ |h^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X((m+1)D)) - h^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))| = O(m^{n-1-i}), \] which integrates to \(h^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = O(m^{n-i})\) for all \(i \ge 2\).

For \(i = 1\): by Asymptotic Riemann-Roch I (?@thm-asymptotic-rr-1), \[ \chi(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) - h^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) + O(m^{n-2}) = \frac{(D^n)}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1}). \] - If \(h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = 0\) for all \(m \gg 0\), then \(-h^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = \frac{(D^n)}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1}) \le 0\). Since \(D\) is nef, Kleiman’s Theorem forces \((D^n) \ge 0\), whence \((D^n) = 0\), giving \(h^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = O(m^{n-1})\). - If \(h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m_0 D)) > 0\) for some \(m_0 > 0\), choose an effective section \(E \in |m_0 D|\) and consider: \[ 0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X((m - m_0)D) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_E(mD) \longrightarrow 0. \] Applying the induction hypothesis to the subvariety \(E\) of dimension \(n - 1\): \[ h^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) - h^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X((m - m_0)D)) \le h^1(E, \mathcal{O}_E(mD)) = O(m^{n-2}). \] Summing telescopically over \(m\) proves \(h^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = O(m^{n-1})\).

Proposition 22.2.11 (Asymptotic Riemann-Roch III (for Nef Divisors))

Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety of dimension \(n\), and let \(D\) be a nef divisor on \(X\). For every coherent sheaf \(\mathcal{F} \in \operatorname{Coh}(X)\), \[ h^0(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) = \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{F}) \cdot \frac{(D^n)}{n!} m^n + O(m^{n-1}). \]

Theorem 22.2.12 (Generalized Hodge Inequality)

Let \(X\) be an irreducible complex projective variety of dimension \(n\). For any nef classes \(\delta_1, \dots, \delta_n \in \operatorname{Nef}(X) \subseteq N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\), \[ (\delta_1 \cdots \delta_n)^n \ge (\delta_1^n) \cdots (\delta_n^n). \]

Corollary 22.2.13 (Khovanskii-Teissier and Brunn-Minkowski Inequalities)

Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety of dimension \(n\).

  1. For nef classes \(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_p, \beta_1, \dots, \beta_{n-p} \in \operatorname{Nef}(X)\): \[ (\alpha_1 \cdots \alpha_p \cdot \beta_1 \cdots \beta_{n-p})^p \ge \prod_{i=1}^p (\alpha_i^p \cdot \beta_1 \cdots \beta_{n-p}). \]
  2. For any two nef classes \(\alpha, \beta \in \operatorname{Nef}(X)\) and any \(0 \le p \le n\): \[ (\alpha^p \cdot \beta^{n-p})^n \ge (\alpha^n)^p (\beta^n)^{n-p}. \]
  3. For any \(0 \le i \le n\): \[ (\alpha^i \cdot \beta^{n-i})^2 \ge (\alpha^{i+1} \cdot \beta^{n-i-1}) (\alpha^{i-1} \cdot \beta^{n-i+1}). \]
  4. Brunn-Minkowski Inequality: For any nef classes \(\alpha, \beta \in \operatorname{Nef}(X)\), \[ \big((\alpha + \beta)^n\big)^{1/n} \ge (\alpha^n)^{1/n} + (\beta^n)^{1/n}. \]
