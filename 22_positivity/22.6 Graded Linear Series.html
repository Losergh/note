22.6 Graded Linear Series

Graded Linear Series, Asymptotic Multiplicities, and Finite Generation of Section Rings

Published

September 20, 2026

Definition 1 (Graded Linear Series) Let \(X\) be an irreducible variety over \(\mathbb{C}\).

  1. A graded linear series \(V_\bullet = \{V_m\}_{m \ge 0}\) on \(X\) associated with a Cartier divisor \(D \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\) (or line bundle \(L = \mathcal{O}_X(D)\)) consists of a collection of finite-dimensional vector subspaces: \[ V_m \subseteq H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)), \quad m \ge 0, \] such that \(V_0 = \mathbb{C}\) and for all \(k, \ell \ge 0\), the natural multiplication of sections satisfies: \[ V_k \cdot V_\ell := \operatorname{Im}\big(V_k \otimes_{\mathbb{C}} V_\ell \longrightarrow H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X((k+\ell)D))\big) \subseteq V_{k+\ell}. \]
  2. The direct sum: \[ R(V_\bullet) := \bigoplus_{m=0}^\infty V_m \] forms a graded \(\mathbb{C}\)-subalgebra of the complete section ring \(R(X, D) = \bigoplus_{m=0}^\infty H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))\), called the section ring associated with \(V_\bullet\).

Definition 2 (Finite Generation) A graded linear series \(V_\bullet\) on a projective variety \(X\) is said to be finitely generated if its associated section ring \(R(V_\bullet)\) is finitely generated as a \(\mathbb{C}\)-algebra.

Definition 3 (Graded Linear Series Associated with an Ideal Sheaf) Let \(\mathfrak{b} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X\) be a non-zero ideal sheaf, and let \(c \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\). Setting: \[ V_m := H^0\big(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathfrak{b}^{\lceil m c \rceil}\big), \] the collection \(V_\bullet = \{V_m\}_{m \ge 0}\) forms a graded linear series because \(\lceil k c \rceil + \lceil \ell c \rceil \ge \lceil (k+\ell)c \rceil\), which guarantees that \(\mathfrak{b}^{\lceil k c \rceil} \cdot \mathfrak{b}^{\lceil \ell c \rceil} \subseteq \mathfrak{b}^{\lceil (k+\ell)c \rceil}\). This series is denoted by: \[ V_\bullet = \Gamma\big(X, \mathcal{O}_X(D) \otimes \mathfrak{b}^c\big). \]

Definition 4 (Linear Series Restricted to a Subvariety) Let \(X \subseteq Y\) be an irreducible subvariety and \(D' \in \operatorname{Div}(Y)\) a Cartier divisor on \(Y\) such that \(D = D'|_X\) is well-defined. The trace linear series on \(X\) is defined by: \[ V_m := \operatorname{Im}\big(H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(mD')) \longrightarrow H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))\big), \] and is denoted by \(V_\bullet = \operatorname{Tr}_X(D')\).

Definition 5 (Operations on Graded Linear Series)  

  1. Product: If \(V_\bullet\) and \(W_\bullet\) are graded linear series associated with \(D\) and \(E\) respectively, their product \((V \cdot W)_\bullet\) is defined by: \[ (V \cdot W)_m := \operatorname{Im}\big(V_m \otimes W_m \longrightarrow H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m(D+E)))\big). \]
  2. Intersection and Span: If \(V_\bullet, W_\bullet\) are both associated with \(D\), we define: \[ (V \cap W)_m := V_m \cap W_m, \qquad \operatorname{Span}(V_\bullet, W_\bullet)_m := \sum_{k+\ell=m} V_k \cdot W_\ell. \]
  3. Veronese Subseries: For an integer \(p \ge 1\), the \(p\)-th Veronese subseries is: \[ (V^{(p)})_m := V_{m p}, \] which is associated with \(p D\).

Lemma 1 (Finite Generation of Graded Algebras) Let \(R = \bigoplus_{i=0}^\infty R_i\) be a graded algebra over a Noetherian domain \(R_0\).

  1. \(R\) is a finitely generated \(R_0\)-algebra if and only if the Veronese subring \(R^{(m)} = \bigoplus_{k=0}^\infty R_{k m}\) is a finitely generated \(R_0\)-algebra for any \(m \ge 1\).
  2. If \(R\) is a finitely generated \(R_0\)-algebra, then there exists an integer \(p \ge 1\) such that \(R^{(p)}\) is generated in degree 1 by \(R_p\).

Theorem 1 (Preservation of Finite Generation) Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety.

  1. If \(V_\bullet\) and \(W_\bullet\) are finitely generated, then \((V \cdot W)_\bullet\) is finitely generated.
  2. If \(V_\bullet\) and \(W_\bullet\) are finitely generated, then \(\operatorname{Span}(V_\bullet, W_\bullet)\) is finitely generated.
  3. \(V_\bullet\) is finitely generated if and only if \(V^{(p)}\) is finitely generated for any \(p \ge 1\).

Proof. Statements (2) and (3) follow directly from standard commutative algebra and Lemma 1.

For (1), by Lemma 1, it suffices to show that the Veronese subseries \(((V \cdot W)^{(p)})_\bullet\) is finitely generated for some \(p \ge 1\). Choose \(m \gg 0\) such that both \(R(V_\bullet)^{(m)}\) and \(R(W_\bullet)^{(m)}\) are generated in degree 1. Suppose \(V_m\) is spanned by \(f_1, \dots, f_a\) and \(W_m\) is spanned by \(g_1, \dots, g_b\). Then the degree-\(k\) piece \((V \cdot W)_{k m}\) is spanned by elements of the form: \[ \left(\prod_{i=1}^a f_i^{\alpha_i}\right) \cdot \left(\prod_{j=1}^b g_j^{\beta_j}\right), \quad \sum_{i=1}^a \alpha_i = k, \; \sum_{j=1}^b \beta_j = k. \] Every such monomial can be regrouped into a product of \(k\) terms of the form \(f_i g_j \in V_m \cdot W_m\). Hence \(R((V \cdot W)_\bullet)^{(m)}\) is generated in degree 1 by \(V_m \cdot W_m\). Therefore, \((V \cdot W)_\bullet\) is finitely generated.

Definition 6 (Semigroups, Iitaka Dimension, and Stable Base Locus) Let \(V_\bullet\) be a graded linear series on \(X\).

  1. The semigroup of non-zero multiples is \(N(V_\bullet) := \{ m \ge 0 \mid V_m \neq 0 \}\) with exponent \(e(V_\bullet) = e(N(V_\bullet))\). The semigroup of free multiples is \(M(V_\bullet) := \{ m \ge 0 \mid |V_m| \text{ is base-point-free} \}\) with exponent \(f(V_\bullet) = e(M(V_\bullet))\).
  2. The Iitaka dimension of \(V_\bullet\) is defined by: \[ \kappa(V_\bullet) := \begin{cases} \max_{m \in N(V_\bullet)} \big\{ \dim \overline{\phi_m(X)} \big\}, & N(V_\bullet) \neq \{0\}, \\ -\infty, & V_m = 0 \text{ for all } m > 0, \end{cases} \] where \(\phi_m: X \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P} V_m\) is the rational map given by \(|V_m|\).
  3. The stable base locus of \(V_\bullet\) is the algebraic subset: \[ \mathbf{B}(V_\bullet) := \bigcap_{m \ge 0} \operatorname{Bs}(|V_m|). \]
  4. The volume of \(V_\bullet\) is defined by: \[ \operatorname{vol}(V_\bullet) := \limsup_{m \in N(V_\bullet), \, m \to \infty} \frac{\dim_{\mathbb{C}} V_m}{m^n / n!}, \quad \text{where } n = \dim X. \]

Example 1 (Graded Linear Series with Irrational Volume) Let \(x = [1:0:\dots:0] \in \mathbb{P}^n\), and let \(\mathfrak{m} = \mathcal{I}_{x/\mathbb{P}^n}\) be its maximal ideal sheaf. For a fixed real number \(0 < c < 1\), consider the graded linear series: \[ V_\bullet = \Gamma\big(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(1) \otimes \mathfrak{m}^c\big), \quad V_p = H^0\big(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{O}(p) \otimes \mathfrak{m}^{\lceil p c \rceil}\big). \] In homogeneous coordinates, \(V_p\) consists of homogeneous polynomials \(\sum a_\alpha x^\alpha\) of degree \(p\) satisfying \(\alpha_1 + \dots + \alpha_n \ge \lceil p c \rceil\), which is equivalent to \(\alpha_0 \le p - \lceil p c \rceil\). Thus: \[ \dim_{\mathbb{C}} V_p = \binom{n+p}{n} - \binom{n + \lceil p c \rceil - 1}{n} = \frac{1}{n!} (p^n - c^n p^n) + O(p^{n-1}). \] Hence the volume is given by: \[ \operatorname{vol}(V_\bullet) = 1 - c^n. \] Taking \(n = 3\) and \(c = 1/\sqrt{2}\), we obtain \(\operatorname{vol}(V_\bullet) = 1 - \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}} \notin \mathbb{Q}\), yielding an irrational volume.

Definition 7 (Graded Families of Ideals)  

  1. A graded family of ideals on an irreducible variety \(X\) is a sequence \(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet = \{\mathfrak{a}_m\}_{m \ge 0}\) of ideal sheaves \(\mathfrak{a}_m \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X\) such that \(\mathfrak{a}_0 = \mathcal{O}_X\) and: \[ \mathfrak{a}_k \cdot \mathfrak{a}_\ell \subseteq \mathfrak{a}_{k+\ell} \quad \text{for all } k, \ell \ge 0. \]
  2. The Rees algebra of \(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet\) is the graded \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebra \(\operatorname{Rees}(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet) := \bigoplus_{m \ge 0} \mathfrak{a}_m\). The family \(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet\) is finitely generated if \(\operatorname{Rees}(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet)\) is a finitely generated \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebra.
  3. For an ideal \(\mathfrak{b} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X\), the trivial family \(\mathfrak{a}_m = \mathfrak{b}^m\) is denoted by \(\operatorname{Pow}_\bullet(\mathfrak{b})\).
  4. For any graded linear series \(V_\bullet\), the base ideals \(\mathfrak{b}_m := \mathfrak{b}(|V_m|)\) form a graded family of ideals, denoted \(\mathfrak{b}(V_\bullet)\).

Definition 8 (Operations and Semigroups for Graded Ideals) For graded families of ideals \(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet\) and \(\mathfrak{b}_\bullet\): \[ (\mathfrak{a} \cap \mathfrak{b})_m := \mathfrak{a}_m \cap \mathfrak{b}_m, \qquad (\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathfrak{b})_m := \mathfrak{a}_m \cdot \mathfrak{b}_m, \qquad (\mathfrak{a} + \mathfrak{b})_m := \sum_{k+\ell=m} \mathfrak{a}_k \cdot \mathfrak{b}_\ell, \qquad (\mathfrak{a}^{(p)})_m := \mathfrak{a}_{m p}. \] The non-zero semigroup is \(N(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet) := \{ m \ge 0 \mid \mathfrak{a}_m \neq (0) \}\) with exponent \(e(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet) = e(N(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet))\).

Proposition 1 (Powers of Finitely Generated Graded Ideals) If \(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet = \{\mathfrak{a}_m\}_{m \ge 0}\) is a finitely generated graded family of ideals, then there exists an integer \(p \ge 1\) such that: \[ \mathfrak{a}_\bullet^{(p)} = \operatorname{Pow}_\bullet(\mathfrak{a}_p), \quad \text{that is}, \quad \mathfrak{a}_{m p} = (\mathfrak{a}_p)^m \quad \text{for all } m \ge 1. \]

Proof. By Lemma 1, the Rees algebra \(\operatorname{Rees}(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet)^{(p)}\) is generated in degree 1 for some \(p \ge 1\). Hence its degree-\(m\) piece is given by the \(m\)-th power of its degree-1 piece: \(\mathfrak{a}_{m p} = (\mathfrak{a}_p)^m\).

Lemma 2 (Castelnuovo-Mumford Regularity) Let \(X\) be a projective scheme and \(B\) an ample line bundle on \(X\). A coherent sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) is said to be \(m\)-regular with respect to \(B\) if \(H^i(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes B^{\otimes(m-i)}) = 0\) for all \(i \ge 1\). If \(\mathcal{F}\) is \(m\)-regular, then for every \(k \ge 0\):

  1. \(\mathcal{F} \otimes B^{\otimes(m+k)}\) is generated by its global sections;
  2. The multiplication map on global sections is surjective: \[ H^0(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes B^{\otimes m}) \otimes H^0(X, B^{\otimes k}) \twoheadrightarrow H^0(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes B^{\otimes(m+k)}); \]
  3. \(\mathcal{F}\) is \((m+k)\)-regular with respect to \(B\).

Proposition 2 (Finite Generation for Semiample Bundles and Base Ideals) Let \(X\) be a normal projective variety.

  1. If \(L\) is a base-point-free line bundle on \(X\), then there exists an integer \(m_0 \ge 1\) such that for all \(a, b \ge m_0\), the multiplication map: \[ H^0(X, L^{\otimes a}) \otimes H^0(X, L^{\otimes b}) \longrightarrow H^0(X, L^{\otimes(a+b)}) \] is surjective.
  2. If \(L\) is semiample, then \(R(X, L)\) is a finitely generated \(\mathbb{C}\)-algebra.
  3. Let \(D \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\). If the graded family of base ideals \(\mathfrak{b}_\bullet = \{\mathfrak{b}(|mD|)\}_{m \ge 0}\) is finitely generated, then the section ring \(R(X, D)\) is finitely generated.

Proof. Proof of (1): Let \(\phi: X \to Y\) be the semiample fibration associated with \(L\) (?@thm-semiample-fibration), so that \(\phi_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y\) and \(L \cong \phi^* A\) for an ample line bundle \(A\) on \(Y\) (assuming \(f(L) = 1\)). By the projection formula, \(H^0(X, L^{\otimes m}) \cong H^0(Y, A^{\otimes m})\). Since \(A\) is ample, it is \(m_0\)-regular for some \(m_0 \gg 0\). By Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity (Lemma 2), multiplication of sections is surjective on \(Y\), hence on \(X\).

Proof of (2): Choose \(n \ge 1\) such that \(L^{\otimes n}\) is base-point-free. By (1), the Veronese subring \(R(X, L^{\otimes n})^{(m_0)} = R(X, L^{\otimes n m_0})\) is generated in degree 1. Hence \(R(X, L^{\otimes n m_0})\) is finitely generated, and by Lemma 1, \(R(X, L)\) is finitely generated.

Proof of (3): By Proposition 1, replacing \(D\) by a multiple \(p D\), we may assume \(\mathfrak{b}(|mD|) = \mathfrak{b}_1^m\) for all \(m \ge 1\). Let \(\nu: X^+ \to X\) be the normalization of the blow-up \(\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathfrak{b}_1}(X)\), let \(N^+\) be the exceptional divisor on \(X^+\), and set \(D^+ := \nu^* D - N^+\). As in the proof of ?@thm-iitaka-fibration, \(D^+\) is base-point-free, so \(R(X^+, D^+)\) is finitely generated by (2). Furthermore, because \(\mathfrak{b}(|mD|) = \mathfrak{b}_1^m\), the pullback of the base locus is precisely \(m N^+\), which implies that all global sections of \(\nu^* (mD)\) vanish along \(m N^+\). Thus: \[ H^0(X^+, \mathcal{O}_{X^+}(m D^+)) \cong H^0(X^+, \mathcal{O}_{X^+}(m \nu^* D)) \cong H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)). \] Consequently, \(R(X^+, D^+) \cong R(X, D)\), proving that \(R(X, D)\) is finitely generated.

Definition 9 (Multiplicity of a Graded System of Ideals) Let \(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet = \{\mathfrak{a}_m\}_{m \ge 0}\) be a graded system of \(\mathfrak{m}_x\)-primary ideals at a closed point \(x \in X\). The multiplicity of \(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet\) is defined by: \[ \operatorname{mult}(\mathfrak{a}_\bullet) := \lim_{m \to \infty} \frac{\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}_{X, x}}(\mathcal{O}_{X, x} / \mathfrak{a}_m)}{m^n / n!}. \]

Theorem 2 (Wilson’s Theorem) Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety of dimension \(n\), and let \(D\) be a nef and big Cartier divisor on \(X\). Then there exists an integer \(m_0 \ge 1\) and an effective Cartier divisor \(N\) such that the linear system: \[ |m D - N| \] is base-point-free for all \(m \ge m_0\).

Proof. By Fujita’s Vanishing Theorem (?@thm-fujita-vanishing), there exists a very ample divisor \(B\) such that for all \(i > 0\) and any nef divisor \(P\), \(H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(B + P)) = 0\). Since \(D\) is big, ?@cor-characterization-bigness implies that there exists an integer \(m_0 > 0\) and an effective divisor \(N\) such that: \[ N \sim m_0 D - (n+1)B. \] For any \(m \ge m_0\), we rewrite: \[ m D - N \sim (m - m_0)D + (n+1)B = B + n B + (m - m_0)D. \] For each \(1 \le i \le n\), the divisor \((m D - N) - i B \sim B + (n-i)B + (m-m_0)D\) has the form \(B + P\) with \(P = (n-i)B + (m-m_0)D\) nef. Thus: \[ H^i\big(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - N - i B)\big) = 0 \quad \text{for all } 1 \le i \le n. \] Therefore, \(\mathcal{O}_X(m D - N)\) is 0-regular with respect to \(B\). By Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity (Lemma 2), \(|m D - N|\) is base-point-free.

Definition 10 (Multiplicity of a Linear System at a Point) Let \(X\) be a projective variety and \(x \in X\) a fixed point. For any linear system \(|V| \subseteq |D|\), the multiplicity of \(|V|\) at \(x\) is: \[ \operatorname{mult}_x |V| := \min_{D' \in |V|} \big\{ \operatorname{mult}_x(D') \big\}. \]

Proposition 3 (Uniform Boundedness of Multiplicities) Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety of dimension \(n\), and let \(D\) be a nef and big Cartier divisor. Then for any point \(x \in X\), there exists a constant \(C > 0\) and an integer \(m_0\) such that: \[ \operatorname{mult}_x |m D| \le C \quad \text{for all } m \ge m_0. \]

Proof. By Wilson’s Theorem (Theorem 2), there exist \(m_0\) and an effective divisor \(N\) such that \(|m D - N|\) is free for all \(m \ge m_0\). Choose an element \(E \in |m D - N|\) not passing through \(x\), so that \(\operatorname{mult}_x(E) = 0\). Then \(E + N \in |m D|\), which yields: \[ \operatorname{mult}_x |m D| \le \operatorname{mult}_x(E + N) = \operatorname{mult}_x(N) =: C. \]

Theorem 3 (Criterion for Finite Generation of Section Rings) Let \(X\) be a normal projective variety, and let \(D\) be a nef and big Cartier divisor on \(X\). Then: \[ R(X, D) \text{ is finitely generated} \iff D \text{ is semiample}. \]

Proof. \((\impliedby)\): This is Proposition 2(2).

\((\implies)\): Assume \(R(X, D)\) is finitely generated. By Lemma 1, there exists an integer \(\ell \ge 1\) such that \(R(X, D)^{(\ell)}\) is generated in degree 1 by \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(\ell D))\). Consequently, the natural symmetric algebra evaluation: \[ \operatorname{Sym}^k H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(\ell D)) \twoheadrightarrow H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(k \ell D)) \] is surjective for all \(k \ge 1\). This implies that every element in \(|k \ell D|\) is a sum of \(k\) elements from \(|\ell D|\), whence: \[ \operatorname{mult}_x |k \ell D| \ge k \cdot \operatorname{mult}_x |\ell D|. \] However, by Proposition 3, the left-hand side is bounded by a constant \(C\) independent of \(k\). Letting \(k \to \infty\) forces: \[ \operatorname{mult}_x |\ell D| = 0. \] Since this holds for every closed point \(x \in X\), the linear system \(|\ell D|\) has no base points. Hence \(D\) is semiample.
