22.4 Big Line Bundles

Asymptotic Growth, Kodaira’s Lemma, Numerical Criteria, and the Big Cone

Published

September 20, 2026

Proposition 1 (Asymptotic Bounds for \(h^0(X, L^{\otimes m})\)) Let \(X\) be an irreducible normal projective variety over a field \(k\) of characteristic zero, and let \(L\) be a line bundle on \(X\) with Iitaka dimension \(\kappa = \kappa(X, L) \ge 1\). Then there exist positive constants \(a, A > 0\) such that: \[ a \cdot m^\kappa \le h^0(X, L^{\otimes m}) \le A \cdot m^\kappa \] for all \(m \in N(X, L)\) sufficiently large.

Proof. Replacing \(L\) by \(L^{\otimes e(L)}\), we may assume without loss of generality that the exponent \(e(L) = 1\). Passing to the Iitaka fibration model \(X_\infty \to Y\), we may assume \(X = X_\infty\) and \(L = L_\infty\). By Hironaka’s resolution of singularities, there exists a projective birational morphism \(f: \widetilde{X} \to X\) with \(\widetilde{X}\) smooth. Because \(f\) is projective, birational, and surjective between normal varieties, \(f\) is an algebraic fiber space (?@prp-afs-function-fields). Hence \(h^0(X, L^{\otimes m}) = h^0(\widetilde{X}, f^* L^{\otimes m})\). Thus, we may assume \(X\) is smooth.

Lower Bound: By the construction of the Iitaka fibration (?@cor-iitaka-fibers), there exists an ample line bundle \(B\) on \(Y\) and an integer \(m_0 \gg 0\) such that \(L^{\otimes m_0} \otimes \phi^* B^\vee\) has a non-zero global section. For any \(\ell \ge 1\), multiplication by this section yields: \[ H^0(Y, B^{\otimes \ell}) \cong H^0(X, \phi^* B^{\otimes \ell}) \hookrightarrow H^0(X, L^{\otimes \ell m_0}). \] By Asymptotic Riemann-Roch I (?@thm-asymptotic-rr-1) on \(Y\) (where \(\dim Y = \kappa\)), \[ h^0(X, L^{\otimes \ell m_0}) \ge h^0(Y, B^{\otimes \ell}) = \frac{(B^\kappa)}{\kappa!} \ell^\kappa + O(\ell^{\kappa-1}) \ge a' \cdot \ell^\kappa \] for some \(a' > 0\) and all \(\ell \gg 0\). Choose a system of representatives \(k_1, \dots, k_{m_0} \in N(X, L)\) for the congruence classes modulo \(m_0\). For any \(m \in N(X, L)\) with \(m \gg 0\), write \(m = \ell m_0 + k_i\). Then: \[ h^0(X, L^{\otimes m}) \ge h^0(X, L^{\otimes \ell m_0}) \ge a' \left(\frac{m - k_i}{m_0}\right)^\kappa \ge a \cdot m^\kappa. \]

Upper Bound: Fix \(u \in N(X, L)\) and choose an effective divisor \(D \in |L^{\otimes u}|\). Since \(X\) is smooth, \(\operatorname{CaCl}(X) = \operatorname{WDiv}(X)\). Write \(D = \sum a_i W_i\) and decompose \(D = D_v + D_h\) into vertical and horizontal components: \[ D_v := \sum_{\phi(W_i) \subsetneq Y} a_i W_i, \qquad D_h := \sum_{\phi(W_i) = Y} a_i W_i. \]

Claim: \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m D_v)) = H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m D))\) for all \(m \ge 0\). Suppose toward a contradiction that there exists a sequence \(m_n \to \infty\) with sections \(s_n \in H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m_n D)) \setminus H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m_n D_v))\). Then \(C_n := \operatorname{div}(s_n) + m_n D \ge 0\), but \(C_n - m_n D_h \not\ge 0\). For a very general point \(y \in Y\), the fiber \(F = X_y\) is smooth, \(\dim F = \dim X - \kappa\), and \(F \cap \operatorname{Supp}(D_v) = \emptyset\). Thus \(L^{\otimes m u}|_F \cong \mathcal{O}_F(m D_h|_F)\). Restriction gives non-trivial sections \(s_n|_F \in H^0(F, \mathcal{O}_F(m_n D_h|_F))\) that are not constant multiples of the canonical section. This implies \(h^0(F, L^{\otimes m_n u}|_F) \ge 2\), contradicting ?@cor-iitaka-fibers (\(\kappa(F, L|_F) = 0\)).

Using the Claim, write \(D_v = \sum a_i E_i\) with \(\phi(E_i) \subsetneq Y\). Choose an ample effective divisor \(H\) on \(Y\) and \(b \gg 0\) such that \(B := b H\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Supp}(B) \supseteq \bigcup \phi(E_i)\). Then \(\phi^* B \ge D_v\) after scaling. Thus: \[ h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m D)) = h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m D_v)) \le h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m r \phi^* B)) = h^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(m r B)) \le A' \cdot m^\kappa. \] For general \(m \gg 0\), write \(m = u r - q\) with \(q \in N(X, L)\), which yields \(h^0(X, L^{\otimes m}) \le h^0(X, L^{\otimes u r}) \le A \cdot m^\kappa\).

Lemma 1 (Bigness by Growth of \(h^0\)) Let \(X\) be a projective variety of dimension \(n\). A Cartier divisor \(D\) on \(X\) is big if and only if there exists a constant \(C > 0\) such that: \[ h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \ge C \cdot m^n \quad \text{for all } m \in N(X, D), \; m \gg 0. \]

Proof. If \(X\) is normal, this is immediate from Proposition 1 by setting \(\kappa(X, D) = n\). In general, let \(\nu: X^\nu \to X\) be the normalization and \(D^\nu = \nu^* D\). The exact sequence \(0 \to \mathcal{O}_X \to \nu_* \mathcal{O}_{X^\nu} \to \mathcal{C} \to 0\) has \(\dim \operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{C}) \le n - 1\). Tensoring with \(\mathcal{O}_X(mD)\) yields: \[ h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \le h^0(X^\nu, \mathcal{O}_{X^\nu}(m D^\nu)) \le h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) + O(m^{n-1}). \] Hence the maximal growth rate \(m^n\) on \(X^\nu\) is equivalent to that on \(X\).

Proposition 2 (Kodaira’s Lemma) Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety of dimension \(n\). Let \(D\) be a big Cartier divisor and \(F\) an arbitrary effective Cartier divisor on \(X\). Then: \[ H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - F)) \neq 0 \quad \text{for all } m \in N(X, D) \text{ with } m \gg 0. \]

Proof. Consider the standard restriction sequence: \[ 0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD - F) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_F(mD) \longrightarrow 0. \] By Lemma 1, \(h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \ge C \cdot m^n\) for some \(C > 0\). Because \(\dim F = n - 1\), the growth of global sections on \(F\) is bounded by: \[ h^0(F, \mathcal{O}_F(mD)) \le C' \cdot m^{n-1}. \] For \(m \gg 0\), \(C m^n > C' m^{n-1}\), forcing the restriction map to have a non-trivial kernel. Thus \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - F)) \neq 0\).

Corollary 1 (Characterization of Bigness) Let \(D\) be a Cartier divisor on an irreducible projective variety \(X\). The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. \(D\) is big;
  2. For every ample Cartier divisor \(A\) on \(X\), there exists an integer \(m > 0\) and an effective divisor \(N\) such that \(m D \sim A + N\);
  3. There exists an ample Cartier divisor \(A\), an integer \(m > 0\), and an effective divisor \(N\) such that \(m D \sim A + N\);
  4. There exists an ample \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor \(A\), an integer \(m > 0\), and an effective \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor \(N\) such that \(m D \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} A + N\).

Proof.

  • \((1) \implies (2)\): Fix \(r \gg 0\) such that \(r A \sim H_r\) and \((r+1)A \sim H_{r+1}\) are effective. By Kodaira’s Lemma (Proposition 2), \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - H_{r+1})) \neq 0\) for \(m \gg 0\). Hence \(m D \sim H_{r+1} + N' \sim A + (H_r + N')\). Setting \(N = H_r + N'\) gives the result.
  • \((2) \implies (3) \implies (4)\): Trivial.
  • \((4) \implies (1)\): If \(m D \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} A + N\), then \(m D - N\) is ample. For \(k \gg 0\), \(k(m D - N)\) is very ample. Since \(k N \ge 0\), we have \(\kappa(X, D) = \kappa(X, k m D) \ge \kappa(X, k(m D - N)) = \dim X\), so \(D\) is big.

Corollary 2 (Numerical Nature and Exponent of Big Divisors)  

  1. Bigness is a numerical property: if \(D_1 \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} D_2\), then \(D_1\) is big if and only if \(D_2\) is big.
  2. If \(D\) is big, its exponent is \(e(D) = 1\).

Proposition 3 (Twisting Theorem for Bigness) A Cartier divisor \(D\) on an irreducible projective variety \(X\) is big if and only if for every coherent sheaf \(\mathcal{F} \in \operatorname{Coh}(X)\), there exists an integer \(m_0 = m(\mathcal{F})\) such that for all \(m \ge m_0\): \[ H^0(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \otimes_k \mathcal{O}_X \longrightarrow \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \] is generically surjective.

Proof. \((\implies)\): Fix an ample divisor \(H\). By Serre generation, \(\mathcal{F}(m H)\) is globally generated for all \(m \ge m_0\). By Corollary 1, there exists \(n > 0\) such that \(n D \sim H + N\) with \(N \ge 0\). The canonical injection \(\mathcal{F}(m H) \hookrightarrow \mathcal{F}(m n D)\) induces a commutative evaluation diagram. Since \(\mathcal{O}_X(m n D) \to \mathcal{O}_X(m H)\) is an isomorphism away from \(\operatorname{Supp}(N)\), generic surjectivity follows.

\((\impliedby)\): Taking \(\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{O}_X(-H)\) for an ample divisor \(H\), generic surjectivity implies \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - H)) \neq 0\) for \(m \gg 0\). Hence \(m D \sim H + N\) for some \(N \ge 0\), which implies \(D\) is big.

Proposition 4 (Restriction of Big Divisors to Subvarieties) Let \(L\) be a big line bundle on an irreducible projective variety \(X\). There exists a proper closed algebraic subset \(V \subsetneq X\) such that for every irreducible subvariety \(Y \not\subseteq V\), the restriction \(L|_Y\) is big on \(Y\). In particular, if \(H\) is a general hyperplane section, \(L|_H\) is big.

Proof. Write \(L \cong \mathcal{O}_X(D)\). By Corollary 1, \(m D \sim H + N\) with \(H\) very ample and \(N \ge 0\). Set \(V = \operatorname{Supp}(N)\). If \(Y \not\subseteq V\), then by Bertini’s Theorem we may choose \(H' \sim H\) such that \(Y \not\subseteq \operatorname{Supp}(H')\). The restriction gives \(m D|_Y \sim H|_Y + N|_Y\). Because \(H|_Y\) is ample and \(N|_Y \ge 0\), \(L|_Y\) is big by Corollary 1(3).

Definition 1 (Big \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Divisors)  

  1. An integral divisor \(D\) is big if and only if \(m D\) is big for some \(m \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}\).
  2. A \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\) is big if \(m D\) is an integral big Cartier divisor for some \(m \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}\).
  3. Bigness is an open numerical condition on \(N^1(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\).

Theorem 1 (Numerical Criterion for Bigness) Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety of dimension \(n\). Let \(D, E\) be nef \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisors on \(X\). If: \[ (D^n) > n (D^{n-1} \cdot E), \] then the difference \(D - E\) is big.

Proposition 5 (Bigness of Nef Divisors) Let \(D\) be a nef \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor on an irreducible projective variety \(X\) of dimension \(n\). Then: \[ D \text{ is big} \iff (D^n) > 0. \]

Proof. If \(D\) is big, \(h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \sim \Theta(m^n)\). By Asymptotic Riemann-Roch III (?@prp-asymptotic-rr-3), \((D^n) = n! \lim_{m \to \infty} \frac{h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))}{m^n} > 0\). The converse follows from Theorem 1 by setting \(E = 0\).

Definition 2 (\(\mathbb{Q}\)-Effective and Big \(\mathbb{R}\)-Divisors)  

  1. A \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor \(D\) is effective if \(m D\) is effective for some \(m \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}\).
  2. A \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor \(D\) is big if and only if \(D \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} A + N\) for an ample \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor \(A\) and an effective \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor \(N\).
  3. An \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor \(D\) is effective if \(D = \sum a_i D_i\) with \(a_i \ge 0\) and \(D_i\) effective Cartier divisors.

Definition 3 (Big \(\mathbb{R}\)-Divisors) An \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\) is big if \(D = \sum a_i D_i\) where each \(D_i\) is an integral big Cartier divisor and \(a_i \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}\).

Proposition 6 (Properties of Big \(\mathbb{R}\)-Divisors) Let \(D, D' \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\).

  1. Numerical invariance: \(D \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} D' \implies (D \text{ big} \iff D' \text{ big})\).
  2. Kodaira decomposition: \(D\) is big if and only if \(D \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} A + N\) for an ample \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor \(A\) and an effective \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor \(N\).
  3. Openness: If \(D\) is big and \(E_1, \dots, E_r \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)\), then \(D + \sum_{i=1}^r \varepsilon_i E_i\) is big for all \(\sum |\varepsilon_i| \ll 1\).

Definition 4 (The Big Cone and Pseudoeffective Cone)  

  1. The big cone \(\operatorname{Big}(X) \subseteq N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\) is the convex cone of all big \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor classes.
  2. The effective cone \(\operatorname{Eff}(X) \subseteq N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\) is the convex cone spanned by effective classes. Its closure: \[ \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X) = \operatorname{Pseff}(X) \] is called the pseudoeffective cone.

Theorem 2 (The Interior of the Pseudoeffective Cone) Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety. Then: \[ \operatorname{Big}(X) = \operatorname{int}\big(\operatorname{Eff}(X)\big) = \operatorname{int}\big(\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)\big), \qquad \overline{\operatorname{Big}(X)} = \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X). \]

Proof. By Proposition 6, every big class has the form \([D] = [A] + [E]\) with \([A]\) ample and \([E]\) effective. Because the ample cone is open, \(\operatorname{Big}(X)\) is open, so \(\operatorname{Big}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{int}(\operatorname{Eff}(X)) \subseteq \operatorname{int}(\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X))\).

Conversely, let \(\eta \in \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)\). There exists a sequence of effective classes \(\eta_k \in \operatorname{Eff}(X)\) converging to \(\eta\). Fixing an ample class \(\alpha \in \operatorname{Amp}(X)\), the perturbed classes \(\eta_k + \frac{1}{k} \alpha\) are big for all \(k \ge 1\). Since \(\lim_{k \to \infty} (\eta_k + \frac{1}{k} \alpha) = \eta\), we see that \(\eta \in \overline{\operatorname{Big}(X)}\). Hence \(\overline{\operatorname{Big}(X)} = \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)\), and taking interiors yields \(\operatorname{Big}(X) = \operatorname{int}(\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X))\).

Corollary 3 (Inclusions of Cones)  

  1. The following inclusions hold: \[ \operatorname{Amp}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Big}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Eff}(X) \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X), \qquad \operatorname{Nef}(X) \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X). \]
  2. Let \(\operatorname{SA}(X) := \{ \sum a_i [D_i] \mid D_i \text{ semiample}, a_i \ge 0 \}\) be the semiample cone. Then: \[ \operatorname{Amp}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{SA}(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Nef}(X). \]

Theorem 3 (Nakai-Moishezon Criterion for \(\mathbb{R}\)-Divisors) Let \(X\) be a projective scheme. An \(\mathbb{R}\)-divisor class \(\delta \in N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\) is ample if and only if for every irreducible subvariety \(V \subseteq X\) with \(\dim V \ge 1\): \[ (\delta^{\dim V} \cdot V) > 0. \]

Proof. We reduce to the case where \(X\) is an irreducible and reduced variety of dimension \(n\). If \(n = 1\), the condition \((\delta \cdot X) > 0\) implies amplitude immediately. We proceed by induction on \(n\):

Assume the criterion holds in dimensions \(< n\). For every proper subvariety \(Y \subsetneq X\), \(\delta|_Y\) is ample, hence \(\delta\) is nef. Choose small ample classes \(\alpha, \alpha' \in N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\) such that \(\delta + \alpha'\) is ample and \(\delta - \alpha\) is rational. For \(\alpha, \alpha'\) sufficiently small, continuity of intersection products ensures: \[ ((\delta + \alpha')^n) > n \big((\delta + \alpha')^{n-1} \cdot (\alpha + \alpha')\big). \] By Theorem 1, the rational class \((\delta + \alpha') - (\alpha + \alpha') = \delta - \alpha\) is big.

Write \(\delta - \alpha \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} E\) for an effective \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisor \(E = \sum q_i Y_i\). For \(0 < \varepsilon \ll 1\), the restriction \((\delta - \varepsilon \alpha)|_{Y_i}\) is nef by induction. For any curve \(C \subseteq X\): - If \(C \subseteq Y_i\), then \(((\delta - \varepsilon \alpha) \cdot C) \ge 0\); - If \(C \not\subseteq \operatorname{Supp}(E)\), then \((E \cdot C) \ge 0\), which implies \(((\delta - \varepsilon \alpha) \cdot C) \ge 0\).

Thus \(\delta - \varepsilon \alpha\) is nef. Finally, decomposing: \[ \delta = (\delta - \varepsilon \alpha) + \varepsilon \alpha \] as the sum of a nef class and an ample class, Kleiman’s perturbation theorem (?@cor-kleiman-perturbation) guarantees that \(\delta\) is ample.
