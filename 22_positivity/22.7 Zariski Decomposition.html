22.7 Zariski Decomposition

Zariski Decomposition on Surfaces, Extremal Rays, and Mori’s Cone Theorem

Published

September 20, 2026

Theorem 1 (Zariski Decomposition on Surfaces) Let \(X\) be a smooth projective surface and let \(D\) be a pseudoeffective integral Cartier divisor on \(X\). Then there exists a unique decomposition into \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisors: \[ D = P + N \] satisfying the following properties:

  1. \(P\) is nef;
  2. \(N = \sum_{i=1}^r a_i E_i\) is effective (\(a_i > 0\)), and if \(N \neq 0\), the intersection matrix \((E_i \cdot E_j)_{1 \le i, j \le r}\) is negative definite;
  3. \(P \cdot E_i = 0\) for every irreducible component \(E_i\) of \(N\). (In particular, if \(N \neq 0\), \(P \in \partial \operatorname{Nef}(X)\)).

Moreover, the natural inclusion induces an isomorphism on global sections: \[ H^0\big(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD - \lceil m N \rceil)\big) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} H^0\big(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)\big) \quad \text{for all } m \ge 1. \] In particular, \(\operatorname{vol}(D) = \operatorname{vol}(P) = (P^2)\).

Proof. We prove the isomorphism on global sections. It suffices to show that for every effective divisor \(D' \sim m D\), we have \(D' \ge m N\). Clearing denominators, we may assume \(m = 1\) and \(P, N\) are integral.

Let \(D' \sim D\) be an effective divisor. Write \(D' = N_1 + M_1\), where \(N_1 = \sum c_i E_i \in \bigoplus \mathbb{Z} E_i\) and \(M_1\) shares no components with \(N\). Because \(P \cdot E_i = 0\), we have: \[ (D' - N) \cdot E_i = (D' - D) \cdot E_i + (D - N) \cdot E_i = 0 + P \cdot E_i = 0. \] Since \(D' = N_1 + M_1\) and \(M_1 \cdot E_i \ge 0\), this implies: \[ (N_1 - N) \cdot E_i = (D' - N) \cdot E_i - M_1 \cdot E_i \le 0 \quad \text{for all } i. \] Write \(N_1 - N = N' - N''\), where \(N', N'' \in \bigoplus \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0} E_i\) have no common components. We must show \(N'' = 0\). If \(N'' \neq 0\), then \((N''^2) < 0\) by the negative definiteness of \((E_i \cdot E_j)\). Hence: \[ (N_1 - N) \cdot N'' = N' \cdot N'' - (N''^2) > 0, \] since \(N' \cdot N'' \ge 0\). On the other hand, \((N_1 - N) \cdot E_i \le 0\) for all \(i\) implies that \((N_1 - N) \cdot N'' \le 0\), a contradiction. Thus \(N'' = 0\), which proves \(N_1 \ge N\).

Corollary 1 (Finite Generation via the Positive Part) Let \(D\) be a big divisor on a smooth projective surface \(X\), with Zariski decomposition \(D = P + N\). Then the section ring \(R(X, D)\) is finitely generated if and only if \(P\) is semiample.

Proof. By Theorem 1, \(R(X, D) \cong R(X, P)\). Since \(\operatorname{vol}(P) = \operatorname{vol}(D) > 0\) and \(P\) is nef, \(P\) is big and nef. The result follows immediately from ?@thm-criterion-fg-nef-big.

Definition 1 (Extremal Ray) Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional real vector space and \(K \subseteq V\) a closed convex cone. A 1-dimensional subcone \(R = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \cdot v \subseteq K\) is called an extremal ray of \(K\) if whenever \(x, y \in K\) satisfy: \[ x + y \in R, \] then both \(x, y \in R\).

Example 1 (Curves on a Surface and Extremal Rays) Let \(X\) be a smooth projective surface. Poincaré duality identifies 1-cycles with divisors, so \(N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \cong N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\).

  1. \(\operatorname{Nef}(X) \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)\). If \((C^2) \ge 0\) for every irreducible curve \(C \subseteq X\), then \(\operatorname{Nef}(X) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)\).
  2. If \(C \subseteq X\) is an irreducible curve with \((C^2) < 0\), then: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} [C] + \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)_{C_{\ge 0}}, \quad \text{where } \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)_{C_{\ge 0}} := \{ \gamma \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) \mid \gamma \cdot C \ge 0 \}. \]
  3. If \(C\) is an irreducible curve with \((C^2) < 0\), then \([C]\) spans an extremal ray in \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)\).

Proof. Proof of (2) and (3): For any irreducible curve \(C' \neq C\), \((C' \cdot C) \ge 0\), so \([C'] \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)_{C_{\ge 0}}\). Any effective 1-cycle decomposes as \(E = a C + E'\) where \(C \not\subseteq \operatorname{Supp}(E')\), proving the sum formula.

Since \((C^2) < 0\), \([C] \notin \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)_{C_{\ge 0}}\). Suppose \(v, w \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)\) satisfy \(v + w = \lambda [C] \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} [C]\). Decomposing \(v = v_1 + v_2\) and \(w = w_1 + w_2\) with \(v_1, w_1 \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} [C]\) and \(v_2, w_2 \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)_{C_{\ge 0}}\), we obtain \(v_2 + w_2 = 0\). Because \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)\) contains no straight lines (as \((H \cdot \gamma) > 0\) for ample \(H\)), this forces \(v_2 = w_2 = 0\), so \(v, w \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} [C]\).

Example 2 (Kollár’s Example: Unboundedness of Very Ample Multiples) On a curve \(Y\) of genus \(g\), every ample line bundle \(D\) satisfies that \(m D\) is very ample for \(m \ge 2g + 1\). Kollár showed that in dimension \(\ge 2\), there is no uniform bound \(m(Y)\) such that \(m D\) is very ample for all ample divisors \(D\).

Construction: Let \(X = E \times E\) for an elliptic curve \(E\). For \(n \ge 2\), define the divisor: \[ A_n := n F_1 + (n^2 - n + 1) F_2 - (n-1)\Delta. \] Computing intersection numbers yields \((A_n^2) = 2\) and \(A_n \cdot (F_1 + F_2) = n^2 - 2n + 3 > 0\), so \(A_n\) is ample. Set \(R = F_1 + F_2\). Choose a smooth curve \(B \in |2 R|\) and let \(\pi: Y \to X\) be the double cover branched along \(B\).

Pulling back gives an ample divisor \(D_n := \pi^* A_n\) on \(Y\). We compute: \[ \pi_* \mathcal{O}_Y(n D_n) = \mathcal{O}_X(n A_n) \oplus \mathcal{O}_X(n A_n - R). \] The self-intersection of \(n A_n - R\) is: \[ (n A_n - R)^2 = 2n^2 - 2n(n^2 - 2n + 3) + 2 = -2(n-1)^3 < 0. \] If \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(n A_n - R)) \neq 0\), the effective divisor would lie in \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) = \operatorname{Nef}(X)\), forcing its self-intersection to be non-negative, a contradiction. Thus: \[ H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(n A_n)) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} H^0(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y(n D_n)). \] Because the global sections of \(n D_n\) all factor through \(X\), they cannot separate the two sheets of the double cover \(\pi: Y \to X\). Hence \(n D_n\) is not very ample, which forces the very ample threshold \(m(D_n) > n\) to grow arbitrarily large.

Definition 2 (The Null Cone) Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety of dimension \(n\), and let \(V \subseteq X\) be a subvariety of dimension \(d\).

  1. The polynomial map associated with \(V\) is: \[ q_V: N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}, \quad q_V(\xi) := (\xi^d \cdot V). \]
  2. The null cone of \(V\) is the algebraic variety: \[ \mathcal{N}_V := \big\{ \xi \in N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \;\big|\; q_V(\xi) = 0 \big\}. \]

Definition 3 (The Nef Boundary) The nef boundary of \(X\) is the boundary of the nef cone: \[ \mathcal{B}_X := \partial \operatorname{Nef}(X) \subseteq N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}. \]

Proposition 1 (Boundary Covered by Null Cones) For every \(\xi \in \mathcal{B}_X\), there exists an irreducible subvariety \(V \subseteq X\) such that \(q_V(\xi) = 0\). That is: \[ \mathcal{B}_X \subseteq \bigcup_{V \subseteq X} \mathcal{N}_V. \]

Proof. By definition of the nef cone, \(q_V(\xi) \ge 0\) for all subvarieties \(V \subseteq X\). If \(q_V(\xi) > 0\) for all \(V\), the Nakai-Moishezon criterion (?@thm-nakai-r-divisors) implies that \(\xi\) is ample, which means \(\xi \in \operatorname{int}(\operatorname{Nef}(X))\), contradicting \(\xi \in \mathcal{B}_X\).

Theorem 2 (Campana-Peternell Theorem) Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety. There exists an open dense subset \(\mathcal{CP}(X) \subseteq \mathcal{B}_X\) such that for every \(\xi \in \mathcal{CP}(X)\), there exists an open neighborhood \(U = U(\xi) \subseteq N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\) and an irreducible subvariety \(V \subseteq X\) such that: \[ \mathcal{B}_X \cap U = \mathcal{N}_V \cap U. \] In other words, the nef boundary is generically real-analytic, cut out locally by the polynomials \(q_V\).

Proof. For each subvariety \(V \subseteq X\), define the open subset of the boundary: \[ \mathcal{O}_V := \big\{ \xi \in \mathcal{B}_X \;\big|\; \exists U(\xi) \text{ s.t. } U(\xi) \cap \mathcal{B}_X \subseteq \mathcal{N}_V \big\}, \qquad \mathcal{O} := \bigcup_{V \subseteq X} \mathcal{O}_V. \]

Claim: \(\mathcal{O}\) is dense in \(\mathcal{B}_X\). Suppose it is not. Then there exists an open set \(U \subseteq N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\) intersecting \(\mathcal{B}_X\) such that \(U \cap \mathcal{O} = \emptyset\), so \(U \cap \mathcal{O}_V = \emptyset\) for all \(V\). Thus \(\overline{U} \setminus \mathcal{N}_V\) is dense in \(\overline{U}\) for each \(V\). Since the topological cycle class \([V] \in H_{2d}(X, \mathbb{Z})\) belongs to a finitely generated abelian group, there are at most countably many distinct null cones \(\mathcal{N}_V\). By the Baire Category Theorem, the countable intersection: \[ \bigcap_V (\overline{U} \setminus \mathcal{N}_V) = \overline{U} \setminus \bigcup_V \mathcal{N}_V \] is dense in \(\overline{U}\). But this contradicts Proposition 1, which states that \(\mathcal{B}_X \subseteq \bigcup_V \mathcal{N}_V\).

Now, let \(\mathcal{P} \subseteq \mathcal{B}_X\) be the set of points satisfying the local equality of the theorem. Fix \(\xi_1 \in \mathcal{O}_V\) with \(\dim V\) minimal. Choose a general very ample divisor \(H\) such that \(H \cap V\) is proper. By the minimality of \(\dim V\), \(\xi_1 \notin \mathcal{O}_{H \cap V}\). Hence there exists a sequence \(\eta_n \to \xi_1\) with \(q_{H \cap V}(\eta_n) > 0\). The directional derivative satisfies: \[ \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{q_V(\eta_n + t [H]) - q_V(\eta_n)}{t} = \dim V \cdot (\eta_n^{\dim V - 1} \cdot H \cdot V) = \dim V \cdot q_{H \cap V}(\eta_n) > 0. \] Thus the differential \(D q_V(\eta_n) \neq 0\), so \(\mathcal{N}_V\) is a smooth manifold near \(\eta_n\). Projecting locally onto the tangent space \(T_{\eta_n} \mathcal{N}_V\), topological invariance of domain implies that \(\mathcal{B}_X\) and \(\mathcal{N}_V\) must coincide in a small neighborhood of \(\eta_n\).

Corollary 2 (Dual Campana-Peternell Theorem) By cone duality between \(N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\) and \(N_1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\), there exists an open dense subset \(\mathcal{CP}(X)^* \subseteq \partial \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)\) that is locally cut out by linear hyperplanes associated with subvarieties of \(X\).

Theorem 3 (Mori’s Cone Theorem) Let \(X\) be a smooth projective variety of dimension \(n\).

  1. There exists a countable family of rational curves \(\{C_i\}_{i \in I}\) on \(X\) satisfying \(0 < -(K_X \cdot C_i) \le n + 1\) such that: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)_{K_X \ge 0} + \sum_{i \in I} \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} [C_i]. \]
  2. For any ample divisor \(H\) and any \(\varepsilon > 0\), there are only finitely many curves \(C_1, \dots, C_t\) spanning extremal rays in the half-space \((K_X + \varepsilon H)_{\le 0}\), so that: \[ \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) = \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)_{K_X + \varepsilon H \ge 0} + \sum_{i=1}^t \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} [C_i]. \]

Corollary 3 (Cone of Fano Varieties) If \(X\) is a Fano variety (that is, \(-K_X\) is ample), then the cone of curves \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X) = \operatorname{NE}(X)\) is a rational polyhedral cone generated by finitely many classes of rational curves.

Corollary 4 (Numerical Ampleness Bounds) Let \(X\) be a smooth projective variety of dimension \(n\).

  1. If \(D\) is an ample divisor such that \((D \cdot C) \ge n + 2\) for all rational curves \(C \subseteq X\), then \(K_X + D\) is ample.
  2. If \(D\) is a nef divisor such that \((D \cdot C) \ge n + 1\) for all rational curves \(C \subseteq X\), then \(K_X + D\) is nef.

Proof. By the Cone Theorem (Theorem 3), any class \(\gamma \in \overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)\) decomposes as \(\gamma = \gamma_0 + \sum a_i [C_i]\) with \(K_X \cdot \gamma_0 \ge 0\) and \(-(K_X \cdot C_i) \le n+1\). For (1): \[ (K_X + D) \cdot C_i = (K_X \cdot C_i) + (D \cdot C_i) \ge -(n+1) + (n+2) = 1 > 0, \] and \((K_X + D) \cdot \gamma_0 \ge D \cdot \gamma_0 > 0\). By Kleiman’s criterion (?@thm-kleiman-cone-criterion), \(K_X + D\) is ample. Statement (2) follows similarly.

Example 3 (Extremal Rays on Blowups of \(\mathbb{P}^2\)) Let \(X = \operatorname{Bl}_{P_1, \dots, P_n}(\mathbb{P}^2)\) be the blow-up of \(\mathbb{P}^2\) at \(n\) points in general position. Let \(\ell\) be the pullback of the line class and \(e_1, \dots, e_n\) the exceptional divisors, so \(-K_X = 3\ell - \sum_{i=1}^n e_i\).

  • For \(1 \le n \le 8\), \(-K_X\) is ample (so \(X\) is a del Pezzo surface). By the adjunction formula: \[ (C^2) + (C \cdot K_X) = 2 g - 2. \] Every \(K_X\)-negative extremal ray must have \(g = 0\) and \((C^2) = -1\), meaning it is spanned by a \((-1)\)-curve. Writing \(C = d \ell - \sum m_i e_i\), the conditions \(d^2 = \sum m_i^2 - 1\) and \(3d - 1 = \sum m_i\) combined with Cauchy-Schwarz give: \[ (9 - n) d^2 - 6d + (1 - n) \le 0. \] Since \(9 - n > 0\), this inequality yields only finitely many integer solutions for \(d\), reflecting the finiteness of the cone.
  • For \(n \ge 10\), \(-K_X\) is no longer nef. There exist infinitely many \((-1)\)-curves with degrees \(d \to \infty\). Their normalized classes in the affine slice accumulate on the boundary of the cone of curves.

Example 4 (Variational Pathologies of the Iitaka Dimension) Let \(T\) be a smooth curve. There exists a family of smooth projective surfaces \(X \to T\) and a line bundle \(L\) on \(X\) such that: \[ \kappa(X_t, L_t) = -\infty \quad \text{for general } t \in T, \qquad \text{while} \qquad \kappa(X_0, L_0) = 2 \quad (\text{big}) \] at a special closed point \(0 \in T\).

Construction: Let \(S \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2\) be a set of 9 distinct points, and consider \(X_S = \operatorname{Bl}_S(\mathbb{P}^2)\) with divisor \(D_S = 2 H_S - E_S\). - If the 9 points lie on a smooth cubic \(B\), the strict transform \(C = 3H - E\) satisfies \((D_S \cdot C) = -3 < 0\). Slicing by multiples of \(C\) reveals that \(H^0(X_S, \mathcal{O}_{X_S}(m D_S)) = 0\) for all \(m \ge 1\). Hence \(\kappa(X_S, D_S) = -\infty\) for general configurations \(S\). - If the 9 points become collinear, \(D_S - H_S\) becomes effective, which implies \(D_S\) is big, so \(\kappa = 2\).
