22.9 Connectedness I

Bertini Theorems, Fulton–Hansen Theorem, Grothendieck’s Connectedness Theorem, and Applications

Published

November 12, 2026

Theorem 22.9.0 (Bertini’s Theorem on Irreducibility)

Let \(X\) be a variety over \(\mathbb{C}\), and let \(f: X \to \mathbb{P}^r\) be a morphism. Fix an integer \(d < \dim \overline{f(X)}\). If \(L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r\) is a general \((r - d)\)-plane (i.e., belonging to a dense open subset in the Grassmannian \(\mathbb{G}(r - d, r)\)), then the preimage: \[ f^{-1}(L) \] is irreducible.

Lemma 22.9.1 (Irreducibility of Fibers Admitting a Section)

Let \(p: V \to T\) be a dominant morphism of irreducible complex varieties. If \(p\) admits a section \(s: T \to V\) such that \(s(T) \nsubseteq \operatorname{Sing}(V)\), then the fiber: \[ V_t = p^{-1}(t) \] is irreducible for general \(t \in T\).

Proof.

Since \(s(T) \nsubseteq \operatorname{Sing}(V)\), the generic point \(\eta_T \notin s^{-1}(\operatorname{Sing} V)\). Define the open subset \(U := T \setminus s^{-1}(\operatorname{Sing} V)\). Shrinking \(T\) to \(U\) and \(V\) to \(p^{-1}(U)\), we may assume without loss of generality that \(T\) is smooth, \(s(T) \cap \operatorname{Sing}(V) = \varnothing\), and \(p\) is flat (hence every fiber has pure dimension).

Now consider \(V_t \cap \operatorname{Sing}(V)\). Since \(s(t) \notin \operatorname{Sing}(V)\), we have \(V_t \nsubseteq \operatorname{Sing}(V)\), so \(V_t \setminus \operatorname{Sing}(V)\) is dense in \(V_t\). Replacing \(V\) by \(V \setminus \operatorname{Sing}(V)\), we may assume that \(V\) is smooth. By generic smoothness, shrinking the target \(T\) further, we may assume that \(p\) is smooth.

It now suffices to show that the fibers of \(p\) are generally connected: 1. By [Stacks Project, Tag 055J], for a smooth morphism, the fibers are geometrically irreducible if and only if they are geometrically connected. Hence it suffices to show that the geometric generic fiber \(V_{\bar{\eta}}\) is irreducible. 2. By [Stacks Project, Tag 054Q], this is equivalent to showing that the function field \(k(T)\) is separably closed in \(k(V)\).

Consider the Stein factorization: let \(\widetilde{k(T)}\) be the separable closure of \(k(T)\) in \(k(V)\), and let \(\widetilde{T}\) be the normalization of \(T\) with respect to \(\widetilde{k(T)}\). This yields the factorization: \[ \begin{CD} V @>{\tilde{p}}>> \widetilde{T} \\ @V{p}VV @VV{f}V \\ T @= T \end{CD} \] where \(\tilde{p}\) is induced by \(\mathcal{O}_T \hookrightarrow \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_T \hookrightarrow p_* \mathcal{O}_V\) (using that \(V\) is normal).

Then \(f: \widetilde{T} \to T\) is finite, and \(\tilde{p}: V \to \widetilde{T}\) has geometrically connected fibers. Because \(p \circ s = \operatorname{id}_T\), we have: \[ f \circ (\tilde{p} \circ s) = \operatorname{id}_T, \] so \(\tilde{p} \circ s\) is a section of \(f\). Since \(T\) is normal and \(f\) is a finite morphism admitting a section, \(f\) must be an isomorphism (\(\widetilde{T} \cong T\)). Hence \(\widetilde{k(T)} = k(T)\), which proves that the fibers of \(p\) are irreducible for general \(t \in T\).

Proof.

(Proof of ?@thm-bertini-irreducibility) Let \(n := \dim \overline{f(X)}\). It suffices to prove the theorem for \(d = n - 1\).

Indeed, suppose the result holds for \(d = n - 1\). For any other \(d < n - 1\), let \(L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r\) be an \((r - d)\)-plane. If \(f^{-1}(L)\) were reducible for general \(L\), we could choose an \((r - n + 1)\)-plane \(L' \subseteq L\) such that \(L'\) is “general” (i.e., \(f^{-1}(L')\) is irreducible).

Writing \(f^{-1}(L) = Y_1 \cup Y_2\) with \(Y_1, Y_2\) irreducible, each component has dimension \(\dim Y_i \ge n - d\). Setting \(k := n - 1 - d \ge 1\), we have \(\dim Y_i \ge k + 1\). Then: \[ f^{-1}(L') = \big(f^{-1}(L') \cap Y_1\big) \cup \big(f^{-1}(L') \cap Y_2\big). \] Adjust \(L'\) so that \(\dim\big(f^{-1}(L') \cap Y_1 \cap Y_2\big) = \dim(Y_1 \cap Y_2) - k\). If \(f^{-1}(L') \cap Y_1 \subseteq f^{-1}(L') \cap Y_2\), then \(f^{-1}(L') \cap Y_1 \subseteq f^{-1}(L') \cap Y_1 \cap Y_2\), which yields: \[ \dim\big(f^{-1}(L') \cap Y_1\big) \ge \dim Y_1 - 1, \quad \text{while} \quad \dim\big(f^{-1}(L') \cap Y_1 \cap Y_2\big) \le \dim Y_1 - 1 - k, \] a contradiction! The same argument applies to \(Y_2\), which would imply that \(f^{-1}(L')\) is reducible, contradicting the choice of \(L'\). Therefore, \(f^{-1}(L)\) must be irreducible.

Now assume \(d = n - 1\). Fix a general \((r - n)\)-plane \(\Lambda \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r\). The set of all \((r - n + 1)\)-planes \(L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r\) passing through \(\Lambda\) is parametrized by \(T = \mathbb{P}^{n-1}\). Consider the incidence family: \[ V := \big\{ (x, t) \in X \times T \;\big|\; f(x) \in L_t \big\} \subseteq X \times T, \] equipped with the projection \(p: V \to T\). It suffices to show that the general fiber of \(p\) is irreducible.

Observe the projection \(V \to X\). For any \(x \in X \setminus f^{-1}(\Lambda)\), the point \(f(x)\) and \(\Lambda\) span a unique \((r - n + 1)\)-plane \(L_t\), which defines a unique \(t \in T\). Thus: \[ V \setminus \big(f^{-1}(\Lambda) \times T\big) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} X \setminus f^{-1}(\Lambda). \] Since \(X \setminus f^{-1}(\Lambda)\) is irreducible, \(V \setminus (f^{-1}(\Lambda) \times T)\) is irreducible and dense in \(V\), so \(V\) is irreducible.

Note that the blow-up \(\mathrm{Bl}_\Lambda \mathbb{P}^r \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^r \times T\) has image \(\big\{ (x, L_t) \mid x \in L_t \big\}\). We obtain a commutative diagram with strict transforms: \[ \begin{CD} \mathrm{Bl}_{f^{-1}(\Lambda)} X @>>> X \times T @>>> X \\ @VV{\text{strict transf.}}V @VVV @VVV \\ \mathrm{Bl}_\Lambda \mathbb{P}^r @>>> \mathbb{P}^r \times T @>>> \mathbb{P}^r \end{CD} \] Choosing \(\Lambda\) such that \(f^{-1}(\Lambda)\) is cut out by a regular sequence (i.e., intersects properly), we have \(V = \mathrm{Bl}_{f^{-1}(\Lambda)} X\). In particular, for any \(x \in f^{-1}(\Lambda) \cap X^{\mathrm{reg}}\), \(V\) is smooth near \(x\).

Since \(X \to \overline{f(X)}\) is generically smooth on the source, there exist open dense subsets \(U \subseteq X^{\mathrm{reg}}\) and \(W \subseteq \overline{f(X)}\) such that \(U \to W\) is smooth. Choosing \(\Lambda\) such that \(\Lambda \cap W \neq \varnothing\), there exists a point: \[ x_0 \in f^{-1}(\Lambda) \cap X^{\mathrm{reg}} \cap f^{-1}(W)^{\mathrm{reg}}. \] Then the map: \[ s: T \longrightarrow V, \quad t \longmapsto (x_0, t) \] defines a section of \(p: V \to T\) with \(s(T) \subseteq V^{\mathrm{reg}}\). By ?@lem-irreducibility-fibers-section, the general fiber of \(p\) is irreducible.

Theorem 22.9.2 (Bertini’s Theorem for Connectedness (Proper Version))

Let \(X\) be an irreducible proper variety over \(\mathbb{C}\), and let \(f: X \to \mathbb{P}^r\) be a morphism. Let \(L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r\) be a linear subspace of codimension \(d < \dim \overline{f(X)}\). Then the preimage: \[ f^{-1}(L) \] is connected for all \(L\). (More generally, this holds whenever \(f^{-1}(L)\) is proper).

Proof.

Let \(G = \mathbb{G}(r - d, r)\) be the Grassmannian parametrizing all codimension \(d\) linear spaces in \(\mathbb{P}^r\). Consider the closed incidence variety: \[ Z := \big\{ (x, L) \in X \times G \;\big|\; x \in f^{-1}(L) \big\} \subseteq X \times G. \] The projection \(Z \to X\) realizes \(Z\) as a Grassmannian bundle over \(X\). Since \(Z \to X\) is an irreducible smooth bundle with irreducible fibers and \(X\) is irreducible, \(Z\) is irreducible.

Let \(p: Z \to G\) be the projection onto the second factor. Because \(X\) is proper, \(p\) is proper. Consider its Stein factorization: \[ Z \xrightarrow{\;\tilde{p}\;} W \xrightarrow{\;r\;} G. \] The general fiber of \(p\) is irreducible by ?@thm-bertini-irreducibility, so \(r: W \to G\) is generically bijective. Since \(G\) is smooth, it is normal. By Zariski’s Main Theorem, \([K(W) : K(G)] = 1\) implies \(W \cong G\). Therefore, every fiber of \(p\) is connected, which shows that \(f^{-1}(L)\) is connected for all \(L\).

Corollary 22.9.3 (Curves Connecting Two Points)

Let \(X\) be an irreducible quasi-projective variety over \(\mathbb{C}\). Any two distinct points \(x_1, x_2 \in X\) can be joined by an irreducible curve \(C \subseteq X\) (which may not be smooth).

Proof.

Let \(X' := \mathrm{Bl}_{\{x_1, x_2\}} X\), and let \(E_1, E_2 \subseteq X'\) be the exceptional divisors. Choose a quasi-finite morphism \(f: X' \to \mathbb{P}^r\). Let \(L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r\) be a linear subspace of codimension \(\dim X - 1\) such that \(f(E_i) \cap L \neq \varnothing\). For a general choice of \(L\), the restriction \(f|_{E_i}\) is finite.

By ?@thm-bertini-irreducibility, \(f^{-1}(L)\) is irreducible of dimension \(1\). Setting \(\widetilde{C} := f^{-1}(L)\) and taking its image \(C := \pi\big(f^{-1}(L)\big) \subseteq X\) under the blow-down morphism \(\pi: X' \to X\), we obtain an irreducible curve \(C\) joining \(x_1\) and \(x_2\).

Theorem 22.9.4 (Fulton–Hansen Connectedness Theorem)

Let \(X\) be an irreducible proper variety over \(\mathbb{C}\), and let \(f: X \to \mathbb{P}^r \times \mathbb{P}^r\) be a morphism such that \(\dim \overline{f(X)} > r\). Then: \[ f^{-1}(\Delta) \subseteq X \] is connected, where \(\Delta \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r \times \mathbb{P}^r\) is the diagonal projective subspace.

Proof.

Observation: Consider the open subvariety \(V \subseteq \mathbb{P}^{2r+1}\) defined by \(V := \mathbb{P}^{2r+1} \setminus (L_1 \cup L_2)\), where: \[ L_1 = \big\{ [0, \dots, 0, y_0, \dots, y_r] \big\}, \qquad L_2 = \big\{ [x_0, \dots, x_r, 0, \dots, 0] \big\}. \] The morphism \(p: V \to \mathbb{P}^r \times \mathbb{P}^r\) is defined by: \[ [x_0, \dots, x_r, y_0, \dots, y_r] \longmapsto \big([x_0, \dots, x_r], [y_0, \dots, y_r]\big). \] Locally on \(\mathbb{P}^r \times D(y_i)\), we have \(V|_{\mathbb{P}^r \times D(y_i)} \cong (\mathbb{A}^{r+1} \setminus \{0\}) \times \mathbb{A}^r\), fitting into the Cartesian diagram: \[ \begin{CD} V|_{\mathbb{P}^r \times D(y_i)} @>{\sim}>> (\mathbb{A}^{r+1} \setminus \{0\}) \times \mathbb{A}^r \\ @VVV @VVV \\ \mathbb{P}^r \times D(y_i) @>{\sim}>> \mathbb{P}^r \times \mathbb{A}^r \end{CD} \] where \(\mathbb{A}^{r+1} \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{P}^r\) is the natural projection. Hence \(V \to \mathbb{P}^r \times \mathbb{P}^r\) is a principal \(\mathbb{G}_m\)-bundle.

Define the projective subspace: \[ \mathcal{L} := \big\{ [x_0, \dots, x_r, y_0, \dots, y_r] \in V \;\big|\; x_i = y_i \text{ for all } i \big\} \subseteq V. \] Then \(\mathcal{L} \xrightarrow{\sim} \Delta\) under \(p\).

Now, given \(f: X \to \mathbb{P}^r \times \mathbb{P}^r\), form the fiber product: \[ \begin{CD} V_X @>{f_V}>> V \\ @V{p}VV @VV{p}V \\ X @>{f}>> \mathbb{P}^r \times \mathbb{P}^r \end{CD} \] Since \(X\) is irreducible and \(V_X \to X\) is smooth with irreducible fibers (hence an open map), \(V_X\) is irreducible. Moreover, \(f\) proper implies \(f_V\) is proper.

Consider the factorization through the image: \[ \begin{CD} V_X @>>> V_{f(X)} @>>> V \\ @VVV @VVV @VV{p}V \\ X @>>> f(X) @>>> \mathbb{P}^r \times \mathbb{P}^r \end{CD} \] where \(V_{f(X)} := \operatorname{Im}(f_V)\) is a \(\mathbb{G}_m\)-bundle over \(f(X)\). By flatness, \(\dim V_{f(X)} = \dim \overline{f(X)} + 1\). Thus: \[ \dim \overline{f_V(V_X)} = \dim \overline{f(X)} + 1 > r + 1 = \operatorname{codim}_{\mathbb{P}^{2r+1}}(\mathcal{L}). \] By ?@thm-bertini-connectedness-proper, \(f_V^{-1}(\mathcal{L})\) is connected.

Set-theoretically, we have \(p\big(f_V^{-1}(\mathcal{L})\big) = f^{-1}(\Delta)\): - First, \(p\big(f_V^{-1}(\mathcal{L})\big) \subseteq f^{-1}(\Delta)\) is clear because \(p(\mathcal{L}) = \Delta\). - Conversely, for any \(x \in f^{-1}(\Delta)\), lift \(f(x)\) to the unique point \(y \in \mathcal{L}\). Then there exists a unique \((x, y) \in V_X\) such that \(f_V(x, y) = y \in \mathcal{L}\) and \(p(x, y) = x\). Thus \((x, y) \in f_V^{-1}(\mathcal{L})\), which gives \(x \in p\big(f_V^{-1}(\mathcal{L})\big)\).

Since \(p\) is continuous, \(f^{-1}(\Delta) = p\big(f_V^{-1}(\mathcal{L})\big)\) is connected.

Example 22.9.5 (Intersection of Subvarieties in Projective Space)

Let \(X, Y \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r\) be irreducible subvarieties over an algebraically closed field. If \(\dim X + \dim Y > r\), then: \[ X \cap Y \] is connected.

Proof: Notice that \(X \cap Y\) is the preimage of \(\Delta\) under the inclusion \(X \times Y \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^r \times \mathbb{P}^r\). Since \(\dim(X \times Y) = \dim X + \dim Y > r\), the connectedness follows directly from ?@thm-fulton-hansen.

Example 22.9.6 (Generalization to Multi-Projective Spaces)

Let \(f: X \to \prod_{i=1}^k \mathbb{P}^r\) be a morphism from an irreducible projective variety \(X\). If: \[ \dim \overline{f(X)} > (k - 1)r, \] then \(f^{-1}(\Delta)\) is connected, where \(\Delta \cong \mathbb{P}^r\) is the diagonal (using the embedding \(V \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^{kr + k - 1}\)).

Definition 22.9.7 (Connectedness in Dimension k)

A Noetherian scheme \(X\) is said to be connected in dimension \(k\) (or \(k\)-connected) if \(\dim X > k\) and for every closed subset \(T \subseteq X\) with \(\dim T < k\), the complement: \[ X \setminus T \] is connected.

Example 22.9.8 (Basic Properties of k-Connectedness)
  1. \(X\) is connected if and only if \(X\) is \(0\)-connected (since \(\dim \varnothing = -1\)).
  2. If \(X\) is \(d\)-connected and \(d' \le d\), then \(X\) is \(d'\)-connected.
  3. If \(X\) is irreducible of dimension \(n\), then \(X\) is \((n - 1)\)-connected (in fact, \(X\) is irreducible if and only if \(X\) is “\(\dim X\)-connected”).
Proposition 22.9.9 (Characterization via Chains of Irreducible Components)

For any \(k < \dim X\), a Noetherian scheme \(X\) is \(k\)-connected if and only if for every pair of irreducible components \(X \neq X'\), there exists a sequence of components: \[ X_0 = X, \; X_1, \; \dots, \; X_p = X' \quad (X_i \neq X_j) \] such that for all \(1 \le i \le p\): \[ \dim(X_{i-1} \cap X_i) \ge k. \]

Remark 22.9.10 (Restriction on the Dimension of T)

Why do we require \(\dim T < k \le \dim X\), and what happens if \(\dim T = \dim X - 1\)?

If \(X = X_1 \cup \dots \cup X_m\) is the decomposition into irreducible components with \(\dim X_1 \ge \dots \ge \dim X_m\), then: \[ \dim(X_1 \cap X_i) \le \dim X_1 - 1 = \dim X - 1. \] Since \(\big(X_1 \setminus \bigcup_{i \ge 2} X_i\big) \cap \big(\bigcup_{i \ge 2} X_i \setminus X_1\big) = \varnothing\), removing the closed subset: \[ T := \bigcup_{i \ge 2} (X_1 \cap X_i) \] of codimension \(\ge 1\) disconnects \(X\). Thus, to have a non-trivial notion of connectedness, one must test against closed subsets \(T\) with \(\dim T \le \dim X - 2\).

Theorem 22.9.11 (Grothendieck’s Connectedness Theorem)

Let \((A, \mathfrak{m})\) be a complete local Noetherian ring, and let \(k \ge 1\). If \(X = \operatorname{Spec} A\) is \(k\)-connected, then for any \(f \in \mathfrak{m}\), the scheme: \[ \operatorname{Spec}(A / fA) \] is \((k - 1)\)-connected.

Corollary 22.9.12 (Complete Local Noetherian Domains)

Let \((A, \mathfrak{m})\) be a complete local Noetherian domain of dimension \(n\). Fix \(d \le n - 1\). For any \(f_1, \dots, f_d \in \mathfrak{m}\), the scheme: \[ \operatorname{Spec}\big(A / (f_1, \dots, f_d)\big) \] is \((n - d - 1)\)-connected.

Corollary 22.9.13 (Stronger Bertini for Connectedness)

Let \(X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r\) be a closed algebraic subset that is \(k\)-connected, and let \(L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r\) be a linear subspace of codimension \(d\). Then: \[ X \cap L \] is connected in dimension \(k - d\).

In particular, if \(X\) is irreducible of dimension \(n\), it is \((n - 1)\)-connected. If \(d < n\), then \(k - d = n - 1 - d \ge 0\), so \(X \cap L\) is \(0\)-connected (i.e., connected).

Example 22.9.14 (Verification of Set-Theoretic Complete Intersections)

Let \(X \subseteq \mathbb{A}^6_{\mathbb{C}}\) be the union of two \(3\)-planes meeting only at the origin: \[ X = V(x_1, x_2, x_3) \cup V(x_4, x_5, x_6) = V\big(\{x_i x_j \mid 1 \le i \le 3, \; 4 \le j \le 6\}\big) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^6, \quad \dim X = 3. \]

Question: Is \(X\) a set-theoretic local complete intersection (LCI) defined by \(3\) functions at the origin? (Check on stalk/completion).

Analysis: Since \(V(x_1, x_2, x_3) \cap V(x_4, x_5, x_6) = \{0\}\), the scheme \(X\) is \(0\)-connected, but not \(1\)-connected.

For any affine open neighborhood \(U = \operatorname{Spec} A \ni 0\) with \(A\) regular of dimension \(6\), if \(U \cap X = \operatorname{Spec}\big(A / (f_1, f_2, f_3)\big)\), then by ?@cor-complete-local-domain: \[ U \cap X \quad \text{would be } (6 - 1 - 3 = 2)\text{-connected}, \] which is a contradiction. The claim is even false for \(4\) functions (which would force \(U \cap X\) to be \(1\)-connected). What about \(5\) functions?

Answer: \(X\) is a set-theoretic complete intersection of \(5\) functions. Consider the polynomials in the indeterminate \(T\): \[ f_1(T) = x_1 + x_2 T + x_3 T^2, \qquad f_2(T) = x_4 + x_5 T + x_6 T^2. \] Then \(f_1(T) f_2(T) \equiv 0\) if and only if \(f_1 \equiv 0\) or \(f_2 \equiv 0\), which is equivalent to \(x \in X\). Expanding the product: \[ f_1 f_2 = x_1 x_4 + (x_1 x_5 + x_2 x_4) T + (x_1 x_6 + x_2 x_5 + x_3 x_4) T^2 + (x_2 x_6 + x_3 x_5) T^3 + x_3 x_6 T^4. \] The vanishing of these \(5\) coefficients cuts out \(X\) set-theoretically.

Theorem 22.9.15 (Unramified Morphisms into Projective Space)

Let \(X\) be a complete irreducible variety of dimension \(n\), and let \(f: X \to \mathbb{P}^r\) be an unramified morphism. If: \[ 2n > r, \] then \(f\) is a closed embedding.

Proof.

Fact: A morphism from a complete variety that is injective on closed points and unramified is a closed immersion.

Consider the product morphism \(F = f \times f: X \times X \to \mathbb{P}^r \times \mathbb{P}^r\). Then: \[ F^{-1}(\Delta_{\mathbb{P}^r}) = X \times_{\mathbb{P}^r} X = \big\{ (x, y) \in X \times X \;\big|\; f(x) = f(y) \big\}. \] The morphism \(f\) is injective on closed points if and only if \(F^{-1}(\Delta_{\mathbb{P}^r}) = \Delta_X\).

Because \(\dim \overline{\operatorname{Im} F} = \dim(X \times X) = 2n > r\), ?@thm-fulton-hansen implies that: \[ X \times_{\mathbb{P}^r} X \] is connected.

Since \(f: X \to \mathbb{P}^r\) is separated and unramified, the diagonal morphism: \[ \Delta_{X / \mathbb{P}^r}: X \longrightarrow X \times_{\mathbb{P}^r} X \] is an open and closed immersion (unramified and proper implies finite, hence clopen). Since \(X \times_{\mathbb{P}^r} X\) is connected, the clopen subset \(\Delta_X\) must be the entire space: \[ \Delta_X = X \times_{\mathbb{P}^r} X. \] Therefore, \(f\) is injective, which implies that \(f\) is a closed embedding.

Remark 22.9.16 (Sharpness and Geometric Intuition)

The inequality \(2n > r\) is sharp: for \(n = 1\) and \(r = 2\), let \(C \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2\) be a nodal curve and let \(X = \widetilde{C} \cong \mathbb{P}^1\) be its normalization. Then \(X \to C \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^2\) is unramified, but not an embedding.

Geometrically, one has to fold something to map \(X\) into \(\mathbb{P}^r\) when \(2n > r\).

Example 22.9.17 (Nonexistence of Hypersurfaces with Normal Crossing Singularities)

If \(r \ge 3\), there are no irreducible singular hypersurfaces \(X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r\) having only normal crossing singularities.

Proof.

A hypersurface \(X\) has a normal crossing singularity at \(p\) if and only if: - Formally: \(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X, p} \cong k[[x_1, \dots, x_r]] / (x_1 \cdots x_k)\) for some \(k \ge 2\); - Étale-locally: there exists an étale neighborhood \((V, v) \to (X, p)\) and \((V, v) \to (U, 0)\), where \(U = \operatorname{Spec}\big(k[x_1, \dots, x_r]/(x_1 \cdots x_k)\big)\) (by Artin approximation [Alper, Theorem B.5.2]); - Analytically: there exists a neighborhood \(U \ni p\) with \(U \cong V \cap V(z_1 \cdots z_k) \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n\) for \(V \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n\) open (Grothendieck–Riemann extension).

If \(X\) has only normal crossing singularities, then at each singular point \(x\): \[ \widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X, x} \cong k[[x_1, \dots, x_r]] / (x_1 \cdots x_k). \] The normalization \(\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{X, x}\) satisfies: \[ \widetilde{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X, x}} \cong \prod_{i=1}^k k[[x_1, \dots, x_r]] / (x_i). \] Checking the tangent space: \[ T_x X^\vee = T(\mathcal{O}_{X, x})^\vee = T(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X, x})^\vee = (x_1, \dots, x_r) / \big((x_1 \cdots x_k) + (x_i x_j)\big). \] For \(r \ge 2\), this equals \((x_1, \dots, x_r) / (x_i x_j) = \big\{ \sum_{i=1}^r a_i x_i \mid a_i \in k \big\}\).

Because normalization commutes with localization, we have the Cartesian diagram: \[ \begin{CD} \prod_{i=1}^k \mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}, y_i} @>>> \widetilde{X} \\ @VVV @VV{\nu}V \\ \mathcal{O}_{X, x} @>>> X \end{CD} \] Analytically, \(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}, y_i} \cong k[[x_1, \dots, x_{r-1}]]\), which has cotangent space \(k^{r-1}\). The differential map on cotangent spaces: \[ T_x X^\vee \twoheadrightarrow T_{y_i} \widetilde{X}^\vee, \quad \sum_{j=1}^r a_j x_j \longmapsto (a_1, \dots, \hat{a}_i, \dots, a_r) \] is surjective. Hence the normalization map \(\widetilde{X} \to X\) is unramified, and \(\widetilde{X}\) is regular of dimension \(r - 1\).

Now consider the composition: \[ \widetilde{X} \xrightarrow{\;\nu\;} X \xhookrightarrow{\phantom{\to}} \mathbb{P}^r. \] Then \(\widetilde{X} \to \mathbb{P}^r\) is an unramified morphism from a complete variety of dimension \(\dim \widetilde{X} = r - 1\). Since \(r \ge 3\): \[ 2(r - 1) = 2r - 2 > r. \] By ?@thm-ramification-closed-embedding, \(\widetilde{X} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^r\) must be a closed embedding. But this implies that \(X \cong \widetilde{X}\) is smooth, contradicting the assumption that \(X\) is singular.

Example 22.9.18 (Étale Fundamental Group of Large Subvarieties)

Let \(X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r\) be an irreducible subvariety of dimension \(n\). If: \[ 2n > r, \] then the étale fundamental group of \(X\) is trivial: \[ \pi_1^{\text{ét}}(X) = \{e\}. \]

Proof.

Suppose \(p: Y \to X\) is a connected finite étale cover. Let \(Y' \subseteq Y\) be an irreducible component of \(Y\). The composite morphism: \[ Y' \xhookrightarrow{\phantom{\to}} Y \xrightarrow{\;p\;} X \xhookrightarrow{\phantom{\to}} \mathbb{P}^r \] is unramified. Since \(\dim Y' = n\) and \(2n > r\), ?@thm-ramification-closed-embedding implies that \(Y' \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^r\) is a closed embedding.

Therefore, \(p|_{Y'}: Y' \xrightarrow{\sim} X\) is an isomorphism. Because \(Y\) is connected and étale over \(X\), it follows that \(Y = Y' \cong X\), so \(X\) admits no nontrivial connected finite étale covers. Hence \(\pi_1^{\text{ét}}(X) = \{e\}\).
