22.5 Volume

Fujita Vanishing, Continuous Extension to \(N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\), and Pathologies of Section Rings

Published

November 5, 2026

Theorem 22.5.0 (Big Input: Fujita’s Vanishing Theorem)

Let \(X\) be a projective scheme and \(H\) an ample Cartier divisor on \(X\). For every coherent sheaf \(\mathcal{F} \in \operatorname{Coh}(X)\), there exists an integer \(m(\mathcal{F}, H) > 0\) such that for all \(i > 0\), all \(m \ge m(\mathcal{F}, H)\), and every nef Cartier divisor \(D\): \[ H^i\big(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{O}_X(m H + D)\big) = 0. \]

Definition 22.5.1 (Volume of a Line Bundle)

Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety of dimension \(n\), and let \(L \in \operatorname{Pic}(X)\). The volume of \(L\) is defined by: \[ \operatorname{vol}(L) = \operatorname{vol}_X(L) := \limsup_{m \to \infty} \frac{h^0(X, L^{\otimes m})}{m^n / n!}. \] - \(\operatorname{vol}(L) = 0 \iff L\) is not big. - \(\operatorname{vol}(L) > 0 \iff L\) is big. - If \(L\) is nef, then by Asymptotic Riemann-Roch III (?@prp-asymptotic-rr-3), \(\operatorname{vol}(L) = (L^n)\).

Proposition 22.5.2 (Volume Lower Bound for Differences of Nef Divisors)

Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety of dimension \(n\). If \(D, E\) are nef Cartier divisors on \(X\), then: \[ \operatorname{vol}(D - E) \ge (D^n) - n (D^{n-1} \cdot E). \]

Proposition 22.5.3 (Extension of Volume to ℚ-Divisors)
  1. Homogeneity: For any Cartier divisor \(D\) and any integer \(a \ge 0\), \[ \operatorname{vol}(a D) = a^n \operatorname{vol}(D). \]
  2. The volume function extends well-definedly to \(\mathbb{Q}\)-divisors: for \(D \in \operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\) with \(a D \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\) (\(a \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}\)), \[ \operatorname{vol}(D) := \frac{1}{a^n} \operatorname{vol}(a D). \]
  3. Ray continuity: For any divisor \(N\) and any \(\varepsilon > 0\), there exists \(p_0 \ge 1\) such that for all \(p \ge p_0\): \[ \left| \operatorname{vol}\left(D \pm \frac{1}{p} N\right) - \operatorname{vol}(D) \right| < \varepsilon. \]
Proposition 22.5.4 (Numerical Invariance of Volume)

If \(D \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} D'\) in \(\operatorname{Div}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\), then \(\operatorname{vol}(D) = \operatorname{vol}(D')\). Hence the volume descends to a well-defined function on the rational Néron-Severi space: \[ \operatorname{vol}: N^1(X)_{\mathbb{Q}} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}. \]

Proof.

Lemma: There exists a fixed Cartier divisor \(N\) such that \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N + P)) \neq 0\) for every nef divisor \(P\). Proof of Lemma: Fix a very ample divisor \(B\). By Fujita’s Vanishing Theorem (?@thm-fujita-vanishing), there exists \(m \gg 0\) such that \(H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(m B + A)) = 0\) for all \(i > 0\) and any nef divisor \(A\). Setting \(A = P + (n - i) B\) for \(0 \le i \le n\), the sheaf \(\mathcal{O}_X((m+n)B + P)\) is 0-regular with respect to \(B\), hence globally generated. Choosing \(N = (m+n)B\) yields \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N + P)) \neq 0\).

Now, if \(D\) is not big, then \(D'\) is not big, so \(\operatorname{vol}(D) = \operatorname{vol}(D') = 0\). Assume \(D\) is big. It suffices to show \(\operatorname{vol}(D) = \operatorname{vol}(D + P)\) for any \(P \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} 0\). Because \(-P\) is nef, the Lemma provides a fixed divisor \(N\) with \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(N - p P)) \neq 0\). Hence: \[ \mathcal{O}_X\big(m(p(D + P) - N)\big) \hookrightarrow \mathcal{O}_X(m p D) \implies \operatorname{vol}(p(D + P) - N) \le \operatorname{vol}(p D). \] Dividing by \(p^n\) gives \(\operatorname{vol}(D + P - \frac{1}{p} N) \le \operatorname{vol}(D)\). Taking \(p \to \infty\), ray continuity yields \(\operatorname{vol}(D + P) \le \operatorname{vol}(D)\). By symmetry, \(\operatorname{vol}(D) = \operatorname{vol}(D + P)\).

Theorem 22.5.5 (Local Lipschitz Continuity and Extension to N1(X)ℝ)

Let \(X\) be an irreducible projective variety of dimension \(n\). Fix any norm \(\|\cdot\|\) on the finite-dimensional real vector space \(N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\).

  1. There exists a constant \(C > 0\) such that for all \(\xi, \xi' \in N^1(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\): \[ |\operatorname{vol}(\xi) - \operatorname{vol}(\xi')| \le C \cdot \big(\max\{\|\xi\|, \|\xi'\|\}\big)^{n-1} \|\xi - \xi'\|. \]
  2. Consequently, \(\operatorname{vol}\) is locally Lipschitz continuous on \(N^1(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\), and extends uniquely to a continuous function: \[ \operatorname{vol}: N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}, \] satisfying the same Lipschitz bound for all \(\xi, \xi' \in N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\).
  3. For any nef classes \(\xi, \eta \in \operatorname{Nef}(X)\), \[ \operatorname{vol}(\xi - \eta) \ge (\xi^n) - n (\xi^{n-1} \cdot \eta). \]
  4. Monotonicity: If \(\xi \in N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\) and \(e \in \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)\), then \(\operatorname{vol}(\xi + e) \ge \operatorname{vol}(\xi)\).
Proposition 22.5.6 (Functorial Properties of Cones)

Let \(f: X \to Y\) be a morphism of projective varieties.

  1. The pullback preserves numerical triviality: if \(\alpha \equiv_{\mathrm{num}} 0\), then \((f^* \alpha \cdot C) = (\alpha \cdot f_* C) = 0\). Thus \(f\) induces a continuous linear map \(f^*: N^1(Y)_{\mathbb{R}} \to N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\).
  2. Preservation of cones:
    • \(f^*(\operatorname{Nef}(Y)) \subseteq \operatorname{Nef}(X)\);
    • \(f^*(\operatorname{SA}(Y)) \subseteq \operatorname{SA}(X)\);
    • \(f^*(\operatorname{Amp}(Y)) \subseteq \operatorname{Amp}(X)\) if \(f\) is finite;
    • \(f^*(\operatorname{Eff}(Y)) \subseteq \operatorname{Eff}(X)\) and \(f^*(\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(Y)) \subseteq \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)\) if \(f\) is surjective.
Theorem 22.5.7 (Birational Invariance of Volume)

Let \(\nu: X' \to X\) be a projective birational morphism between irreducible varieties of dimension \(n\).

  1. For every Cartier divisor \(D \in \operatorname{Div}(X)\) and \(D' = \nu^* D\): \[ \operatorname{vol}_{X'}(D') = \operatorname{vol}_X(D), \qquad D' \text{ is big} \iff D \text{ is big}. \]
  2. For every real class \(\xi \in N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\) and \(\xi' = \nu^* \xi\): \[ \operatorname{vol}_{X'}(\xi') = \operatorname{vol}_X(\xi), \qquad \xi' \text{ is big} \iff \xi \text{ is big}. \]
Example 22.5.8 (Non-Finitely Generated Section Ring (Zariski’s Construction))

Let \(C_0 \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2\) be a smooth plane cubic curve, and let \(\ell\) be the hyperplane class on \(\mathbb{P}^2\). Choose 12 distinct points \(P_1, \dots, P_{12} \in C_0\) such that: \[ \eta := \mathcal{O}_{C_0}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{12} P_i - 4\ell\right) \in \operatorname{Pic}^0(C_0) \] is a non-torsion element (which is possible because the torsion group \(\operatorname{Tor}(\operatorname{Pic}^0(C_0))\) is countable while \(C_0\) is uncountable).

Let \(\mu: X = \operatorname{Bl}_{P_1, \dots, P_{12}}(\mathbb{P}^2) \to \mathbb{P}^2\) be the blow-up, with exceptional divisors \(E_i\) and \(E = \sum_{i=1}^{12} E_i\). Let \(H = \mu^* \ell\), and let \(C = 3H - E\) be the strict transform of \(C_0\). Consider the divisor: \[ D := 4H - E \sim C + H. \] Then:

  1. \(D\) is nef and big, because \(D \cdot H = 4 > 0\), \(D \cdot E_i = 1 > 0\), and \((D^2) = 16 - 12 = 4 > 0\).
  2. \(\mathcal{O}_C(mD) \cong \eta^{-m}\). Because \(\eta\) is non-torsion, \(h^0(C, \mathcal{O}_C(mD)) = 0\) for all \(m \ge 1\). Hence the restriction map \(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD)) \to H^0(C, \mathcal{O}_C(mD))\) is zero, so \(C \subseteq \operatorname{Bs}(|mD|)\) for all \(m \ge 1\).
  3. By Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing, \(H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X((m-1)D + H - C)) = 0\), so \(|mD - C|\) is base-point-free. Thus, there exist sections \(s_m \in H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))\) with vanishing order \(\operatorname{ord}_C(s_m) = 1\) along \(C\) for all \(m \ge 1\).
  4. If the section ring \(R(X, D) = \bigoplus_{m=0}^\infty H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(mD))\) were finitely generated by elements of degree \(\le d\), the vanishing orders along \(C\) for large \(m\) would be forced to grow linearly, contradicting the existence of sections with order 1 for all \(m\).

Thus, \(R(X, D)\) is not finitely generated as a \(k\)-algebra.

Lemma 22.5.9 (Cohomology and Bigness on ℙ(⨁𝒪V(Ai)))

Let \(V\) be an irreducible projective variety of dimension \(v\), and let \(A_0, \dots, A_r\) be Cartier divisors on \(V\). Set \(\mathcal{E} := \bigoplus_{i=0}^r \mathcal{O}_V(A_i)\) and \(X := \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})\) with projection \(p: X \to V\).

  1. For every \(k \ge 0\): \[ H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(k)) \cong \bigoplus_{\sum a_i = k, \, a_i \ge 0} H^0\left(V, \mathcal{O}_V\left(\sum_{i=0}^r a_i A_i\right)\right). \]
  2. The tautological line bundle \(\mathcal{O}_X(1)\) is big if and only if: \[ \left( \sum_{i=0}^r \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0} A_i \right) \cap \operatorname{Big}(V) \neq \emptyset. \]
Example 22.5.10 (Another Non-Finitely Generated Ring on a Projective Bundle)

Let \(V\) be a smooth projective curve of genus \(g \ge 1\). Choose a non-torsion degree 0 bundle \(P \in \operatorname{Pic}^0(V)\) and an ample divisor \(A\) with \(\deg A \gg 0\). Set \(X = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_V(P) \oplus \mathcal{O}_V(A))\).

By ?@lem-projective-bundle-sums, \(R(X, \mathcal{O}_X(1)) = \bigoplus_{i+j=k} H^0(V, \mathcal{O}_V(i P + j A))\). The subspace \(R_{k-1, 1} = H^0(V, \mathcal{O}_V((k-1)P + A))\) is non-zero for all \(k \ge 1\) by Riemann-Roch. However, for any \(2 \le p \le k\), \[ R_{k-p, 1} \otimes R_{p-1, 0} = 0 \] because \(R_{p-1, 0} = H^0(V, \mathcal{O}_V((p-1)P)) = 0\). Hence the elements in \(R_{k-1, 1}\) cannot be generated by elements of lower degrees. Therefore, \(R(X, \mathcal{O}_X(1))\) is not finitely generated.

Remark 22.5.11 (Stable Base Locus of the Projective Bundle)

In ?@exm-non-fg-section-ring-2, the projection \(\mathcal{E} \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}_V(P)\) determines a section \(\sigma: V \hookrightarrow X\) such that \(\mathcal{O}_X(1)|_{\sigma(V)} \cong \mathcal{O}_V(P)\). Since \(H^0(V, \mathcal{O}_V(k P)) = 0\) for all \(k \ge 1\), we have \(\sigma(V) \subseteq \operatorname{Bs}(|\mathcal{O}_X(k)|)\) for every \(k\).

Example 22.5.12 (Integral Divisors with Arbitrarily Small Volume)

Let \(V\) be an elliptic curve and \(x \in V\). For \(a \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}\), set \(A_0 = (1-a)x\) and \(A_1 = x\). Let \(X = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_V(A_0) \oplus \mathcal{O}_V(A_1))\). Then \(\dim X = 2\), and: \[ h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(k)) = \sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k/a \rfloor} h^0\big(V, \mathcal{O}_V((k - j a)x)\big) = \frac{k^2}{2a} + O(k). \] Therefore: \[ \operatorname{vol}_X(\mathcal{O}_X(1)) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(k))}{k^2 / 2!} = \frac{1}{a}. \] As \(a \to \infty\), the volume of the integral divisor \(\mathcal{O}_X(1)\) becomes arbitrarily small.

Example 22.5.13 (A Divisor with Irrational Volume (Cutkosky))

Let \(V = E \times E\) be the product of an elliptic curve with itself. Then \(N^1(V)_{\mathbb{R}} \cong \mathbb{R}^3\), and the nef cone \(\operatorname{Nef}(V)\) is the round circular cone \(\{ \alpha \mid (\alpha^2) \ge 0, (\alpha \cdot H) \ge 0 \}\).

Let \(F_1, F_2\) be the two fibers and \(\Delta \subseteq E \times E\) the diagonal. Then \((F_1^2) = (F_2^2) = (\Delta^2) = 0\) and \((F_1 \cdot F_2) = (F_1 \cdot \Delta) = (F_2 \cdot \Delta) = 1\). Consider the ample divisors: \[ D := F_1 + F_2, \qquad H := 3(F_2 + \Delta). \] The intersection polynomial is \(((\delta - t h)^2) = 2(9 t^2 - 9 t + 1)\). Its smallest positive root: \[ \sigma = \frac{3 - \sqrt{5}}{6} \notin \mathbb{Q} \] marks the boundary of the nef cone along the ray \(D - t H\).

Let \(X = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_V(D) \oplus \mathcal{O}_V(-H))\) over \(V\), so \(\dim X = 3\). Then: \[ h^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(k)) = \sum_{i+j=k, \, j \le \sigma i} h^0(V, \mathcal{O}_V(i D - j H)). \] By Kodaira vanishing and Riemann-Roch on \(V\), \(h^0(V, \mathcal{O}_V(i D - j H)) = \frac{1}{2} (i D - j H)^2\). Passing to the Riemann sum: \[ \operatorname{vol}_X(\mathcal{O}_X(1)) = 3 \int_{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}^1 \big(t D - (1-t)H\big)^2 \, dt = 3 \int_{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}^1 (38 t^2 - 54 t + 18) \, dt. \] Substituting \(\sigma = \frac{3 - \sqrt{5}}{6}\) yields an irrational number. Hence, the volume of an integral divisor can be irrational!

Remark 22.5.14 (Base Locus and Non-Rational Multipliers)

In ?@exm-irrational-volume, the section \(E = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_V(-H)) \subseteq X\) satisfies: \[ \mathfrak{b}(|\mathcal{O}_X(k)|) = \mathcal{O}_X\left(-\left(\left\lfloor \frac{k}{1+\sigma} \right\rfloor + 1\right) E\right) = \mathcal{O}_X(-\lceil c k \rceil E), \] where \(c = \frac{1}{1+\sigma} = \frac{6}{9 - \sqrt{5}} \notin \mathbb{Q}\). Thus the jumping coefficients of the base ideals are irrational multiples of \(k\).
